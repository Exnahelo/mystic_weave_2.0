# Temple of Mordrax

The sealed temple beneath the [[Shadowed Hollows]], raised in ages long before the founding of Drakenvale and inherited by the survivors of the [[War of the Fallen]] as a fact of the land they had chosen to settle. Already ancient when the founders gathered, the Temple's existence forced the founding compact to address a question the founders could not avoid: what to do with the place where [[Mordrax]]'s primal force had once been honored, in a sanctuary that could not honor it. The answer became the [[Oath of the Fallen]]'s final working — the Temple was sealed, the wards laid through stone and threshold, and the seal made the founding compact's last act. The seal has held for ages, with one catastrophic interruption. The [[Discordant War]] began when the [[Mordraxian Rebels]] excavated their ceremonial shaft at the [[Dark Hold]] to reach the Temple's buried power; it ended when the [[Vindrael|Platinum Warden]]'s sacrifice collapsed the shaft and the [[Draconic Council]] restored the Temple's wards. The seal has held since. The [[Vigil]] maintains its wards. The elite [[Wardens]] patrol its threshold on rotation. No one living has seen its interior, and the realm's continuing peace depends on that not changing.

## Scene Texture

The Temple is not visited. The description that follows is of the threshold — the approach corridor and the outer face of the seal itself. What lies beyond the seal is not canonically known to any living figure except possibly [[Mordrax]], and canon does not confirm her awareness.

The approach is a descent. From the [[Shadowed Hollows]]' deeper interior, a single passage descends through rock of increasing density and decreasing warmth, opening eventually into a vaulted forechamber carved into the bedrock far below the surface corruption. The forechamber is small — perhaps thirty paces across — and its walls bear the founders' ward-work, layered over older stonework whose origins predate the realm itself. The ward-inlay is silver-platinum, traced fine across the dark surface. It glows faintly. It has glowed faintly for ages.

The forechamber ends at the seal.

The seal is a single pair of massive stone doors, sized for dragons and sealed closed by work the founding coalition performed together across weeks of continuous effort. The doors are carved with a composition depicting the founding compact itself — the founding dragons rendered at equal scale, circled around the [[Heartstone]], with [[Solveris]] above and [[Mordrax]] below. The carving is masterwork. The seal itself is not visible as a physical object; it is magical work woven through the doors, the frame, the surrounding stone, and the air itself. A visitor standing in the forechamber can feel the seal. Trained visitors can see traces of it — faint lines of light tracing the doors and the walls, pulsing slowly in patterns that correspond to the ongoing maintenance cycle.

The seal is active. It requires continuous attention. The [[Vigil]] conducts ward-maintenance rotations on a schedule that has not varied in a century: a senior Vigil member descends with a small team of elite [[Wardens]], spends approximately three days at the threshold conducting inspection and reinforcement work, and departs. A fresh team arrives before the previous team's effects have fully ended. The seal has never been unattended since the [[Draconic Council|Council]] restored it in the [[Discordant War]]'s final days. It will not be.

The air in the forechamber is cold — not the ordinary cold of deep stone, but a specific cold that visitors describe as subtracted rather than present. The forechamber is silent in the same subtracted way. Sounds made here travel oddly. A visitor speaking in ordinary tones finds their voice muted, absorbed by something, returning with a quality that is not echo. Most visitors do not speak much in the forechamber. The ones who do say what they came to say and leave.

Beyond the seal, behind the doors, is the Temple's interior. Its architecture is recorded in the founding documents, which remain in the Archive Block of the [[Administrative Quarter]] under [[Draconic Council|Council]] seal. Those documents describe what the founders found when they descended into the forechamber for the first time — the chambers, the altars, the cardinal arrangements left from the Temple's ancient era of devotion. The documents are not publicly available. The [[Vigil]] has read them. The elite [[Wardens]] have read excerpts relevant to their duty. The [[Draconic Council|Council]] has read all of them. No one else in the realm has.

The interior is sealed precisely because it is not empty. What the Temple held before the founders sealed it — the objects, the inscriptions, the residue of ages of devotion to [[Mordrax]] — remained inside, bound by the seal but not destroyed. What pressure the [[Mordraxian Rebels]]' shaft placed on the seal during the years they channeled toward it, and what the Temple may have absorbed from that pressure before the [[Vindrael|Warden]] severed their channel, is not recorded in canon. The seal exists to keep all of it inside.

## Historical Weight

The Temple of Mordrax presented the founding coalition with a question that had no easy answer. The [[Hollow Crown]] had been chosen as the sanctuary's site because of its hidden character and the survivors' practical need to begin again. None of those reasons accounted for the fact that beneath the southern reaches of the basin lay an ancient temple to [[Mordrax]] — neither active nor dormant in any easy sense, but present and unavoidable.

The founders considered three paths.

The first was destruction. If the Temple could be unmade, then the question would be removed entirely. Those who argued for this path believed that no sanctuary built on [[Solveris]]'s principles could permit [[Mordrax]]'s seat to remain at its foundations, regardless of how deeply it was buried. Destruction proved infeasible. The Temple had endured ages, had absorbed weight no surface working could match, and was woven into the bedrock at depths the founders could not safely breach. Practical reality and the weight of what the Temple held made unmaking it impossible without unleashing precisely what the founders sought to contain.

The second was inaction. Some argued the Temple was so deeply buried, so far from the realm's surface life, that simply ignoring it would suffice. The argument did not survive serious examination. A temple to [[Mordrax]], even unattended, was not an absence. The founders understood that something so old and so weighted could not simply be left.

The third was sealing. This was the path taken. The founders wove the seal through the Temple's threshold as the [[Oath of the Fallen]]'s final working — the founding compact's last act, performed only after the rest of the sanctuary's foundational structures were in place. The seal was not built outside the Oath; the seal was woven into the Oath itself. The Temple's containment became part of the realm's founding identity, inseparable from the compact that brought Drakenvale into being.

This arrangement held through the long [[Age of Harmony]]. The Temple's existence was acknowledged in civic and religious canon. Its sealed state was treated not as a compromise but as the architecture the founders intended. Drakenvale residents learned of the Temple as part of their common education. They did not visit it. They did not need to. They needed only to know that the founders had attended to it.

The [[Discordant War]] broke this arrangement. The [[Mordraxian Rebels]], working from their fortified [[Dark Hold]] in the southern reaches, sought a different relationship with what the Temple held. They did not attempt to unseal the Temple directly — that working was beyond them, and the seal woven through the Oath itself was beyond what any faction within the realm could break. Instead, they excavated a ceremonial shaft into the earth at the [[Dark Hold]]'s center, descending toward the Temple's buried power and seeking to channel it without breaching the seal proper.

The shaft was the war's central work. Through it, the rebels grew bolder over years of channeling. Their workings accumulated. The pressure they placed on the realm — and on the seal far below — built toward something that could not be permitted to complete.

The [[Vindrael|Platinum Warden]] ended it. He fought to the heart of the [[Dark Hold]] and gave his life at the pit's edge. His sacrifice collapsed the shaft, severed the rebels' channel to [[Mordrax]]'s power, and broke the foundation of the rebellion. With the channel closed, the [[Draconic Council]] restored and reinforced the wards on the Temple itself — repairing what the rebels' channeling had stressed and adding new protections to the founding seal. The [[Vindrael|Warden]]'s act made the resealing possible; the [[Draconic Council|Council]]'s working completed it.

The seal as it stands today is the founders' original work, plus the [[Draconic Council|Council]]'s post-war reinforcement. The [[Vigil]] maintains both layers as a single continuous system. The distinction between them matters only to specialists.

## Contemporary Significance

The Temple's contemporary role is threefold.

First, it is a historical monument that cannot be visited. The realm's theological and civic memory is bound up in its existence. Every generation's children learn that the Temple is there. Every adult knows that the continued sealing is a duty the realm collectively carries. The Temple shapes the realm's self-understanding even though almost no one alive has been near it.

Second, it is the [[Vigil]]'s defining responsibility. The [[Platinum Oath Monastery]] exists, in its deepest purpose, to maintain the Temple's seal. Everything else the [[Vigil]] does — the Rift monitoring, the elite [[Wardens|Warden]] training, the [[Greymantle]] trade, the public monastic face — is structured around and funded by this central duty. The [[Vigil]] has not forgotten. They will not.

Third, it is a latent question the realm does not voluntarily confront. What would happen if the seal failed? Is the seal truly permanent? What, exactly, is behind the doors? The [[Draconic Council|Council]] addresses these questions only in confidence. The [[Vigil]]'s senior members carry working answers that are not shared outside their order. The elite [[Wardens]] understand their duty without needing to know the full stakes. Ordinary residents do not ask. The questions are present, however. They are the background silence of the realm's sacred life.

## Access

The Temple is not accessible to ordinary travelers. The surface descent from the [[Shadowed Hollows]] is protected, patrolled, and physically difficult to locate without specific guidance. The [[Vigil]] and the elite [[Wardens]] know the path. Senior [[Draconic Council|Council]] members know the path. Specific [[Arcane Conservatory]] ward-specialists know the path as a professional necessity. That is the complete list of living figures with ordinary canonical knowledge of the approach.

A character attempting to find the Temple without this knowledge would face:

- **The [[Shadowed Hollows]].** The surface region above the Temple is itself a corrupted zone. Traversal requires skill, preparation, and luck. Getting lost here is how visitors die. The [[Vigil]] does not maintain patrol routes through the Hollows' interior, because doing so would compromise the Temple's security; characters entering the Hollows are on their own.
- **The descent.** The path from the Hollows' deeper interior to the Temple forechamber is not marked. Multiple false passages exist. The actual route requires specific knowledge that the [[Vigil]] protects rigorously.
- **The forechamber threshold.** A character somehow reaching the forechamber without [[Vigil]] authorization would be in a space where the [[Vigil]] patrols. Interception is likely. Non-hostile interception means escort back to the surface and debriefing. Hostile interception means different outcomes, depending on the character's intent and the [[Vigil]]'s judgment.

Approaching the Temple with [[Vigil]] sanction follows a different protocol: escorted descent, controlled entry, specific purpose. This is the standard for ward-maintenance rotations. It is not a protocol casual visitors can invoke.

Breaching the seal — unsealing the Temple — is not addressed here in mechanical terms. Any storyline attempting this is a realm-shaking event requiring explicit narrative authorization. It is not an action a player character performs casually.

## Authoring Notes

The Temple of Mordrax carries the `sealed-site` tag, indicating a location that is canonically known and mapped but not navigable in ordinary play. Characters can approach the threshold under specific conditions; they cannot ordinarily enter. The interior of the Temple is canonically NOT AUTHORED. The founding documents describing the interior exist in the [[Administrative Quarter]]'s [[Draconic Council|Council]]-sealed archives but are not available in this node or elsewhere in public canon. This opacity is deliberate and should be preserved. If a major storyline ever requires Temple interior content, that content should be authored at that time as a distinct [[Draconic Council|Council]]-level narrative event.

The Temple predates the realm. This is canonical and consistent with `history.md` and `world.md`: the Temple was already ancient when the survivors of the [[War of the Fallen]] gathered in the basin. The founders did not build the Temple. They inherited it, debated what to do with it, and ultimately sealed it as the [[Oath of the Fallen]]'s final working. Any future worldbuilding that touches the founding should preserve this framing.

The two-act sealing sequence is canonical. The original founders' seal was woven through the [[Oath of the Fallen]] at the founding. The post-war reinforcement was the [[Draconic Council]]'s working, made possible by the [[Vindrael|Platinum Warden]]'s sacrifice at the [[Dark Hold]]'s pit. The seal as it stands today is the layered combination of both. The distinction between layers matters only to specialists; for ordinary narration, treat the seal as a single continuous system.

The [[Mordraxian Rebels]] did not breach the Temple itself during the [[Discordant War]]. Their effort was channeled through the ceremonial shaft they excavated at the [[Dark Hold]]; they sought to draw on the Temple's buried power without breaching the seal proper. The [[Vindrael|Warden]]'s sacrifice severed their channel before the workings could complete. Any storyline involving the Discordant War's relationship to the Temple should preserve this canonical separation: the rebels never reached the threshold; the seal proper was never opened.

The connection to [[Shadowed Hollows]] is the canonical surface-to-subterranean descent path. [[Shadowed Hollows]] (and the Shadowed Hollows Proper sub-zone) is currently a placeholder exit per the topology baseline. When that node is authored (scheduled as a later concern), the reciprocal connection should be established. Until then, this connection is a forward reference.

The connection to [[Beneath the Southern Dark Quadrant]] is the regional parent reference. The underworld region beneath the southern dark quadrant is the Temple's broader geographic context.

The Temple's hazard level is high. The forechamber itself is survivable for brief periods with adequate training; prolonged exposure or attempts to breach the seal are canonically catastrophic. The threat is not combat — the [[Vigil]] is not hostile to recognized visitors — but environmental, psychological, and consequential.

There are no resident named NPCs at the Temple. The [[Vigil]] members conducting maintenance are transient and rotate. Scene-specific instantiation is appropriate when the narrative requires a [[Vigil]] figure.

The Temple's existence is public knowledge; its physical approach path is not. A character who has not canonically visited or been briefed on the approach does not know how to reach the threshold.

The Temple's interior contents — objects, inscriptions, configurations from its ancient era — are canonically acknowledged as existing but not described. This reserve is intentional. Specific narrative elements (a named artifact within the Temple, a specific inscription, a particular chamber) should be authored only when a storyline requires them and with [[Draconic Council|Council]]-level narrative weight.

The "background silence" framing — the Temple as a present but unaddressed question in the realm's sacred life — is deliberate tonal guidance. The realm does not pretend the Temple does not exist; the realm simply does not discuss it more than necessary. Narration in any Temple-adjacent scene should reflect this restraint.
