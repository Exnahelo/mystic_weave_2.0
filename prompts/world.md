# Drakenvale

> A hidden sanctuary within the Hollow Crown, governed by the Codex of Remembrance — justice, balance, and wisdom — where dragons of many callings coexist alongside drakari, kobolds, and mortals.

## Overview

Drakenvale is the central city and sanctuary-realm hidden within the Hollow Crown, a vast enclosed basin roughly 350 kilometers across, concealed inside the shattered shell of an ancient mountain. In ages past, a great celestial body broke against the world, and one of its largest fragments struck the mountain’s summit with catastrophic force, hollowing its interior and leaving behind a hidden interior world concealed by jagged alpine heights, broken ridges, cloud-wreathed summits, and ancient wards. From the outside, the Hollow Crown appears to be only an impossible mountain. Most who see it judge it impassable. Few imagine that a living realm lies within.

At the center of the Hollow Crown rises the Heartmass, a colossal formation of fused celestial stone and platinum-veined mountain heartrock created where the impact lodged. The Stronghold of Drakenvale was carved directly from this pale white-and-silver mass. Within the Stronghold, at the center of the Draconic Hall, rests the Heartstone — a radiant crystal discovered during the earliest excavations and later filled with the gathered magic of the founding dragons. The Heartstone serves as the spiritual and political center of the sanctuary.

From the Stronghold, sacred waters descend through terraces, falls, and reflecting basins into the Sacred Pools and then into the great southern lake below. Around the Stronghold spread the lower terraces and districts of the city of Drakenvale. Beyond the city lie the Draconic Grasslands, then the broader forest, wetland, cavern, highland, and dark regions of the Hollow Crown. The weather of the basin is said to mirror the emotional state of its dragons — calm mists in times of peace, powerful storms in times of unrest. The land is rich in platinum, gemstones, magical flora, and deep mineral resonance, all saturated by draconic presence and old magic.

The governing philosophy of the sanctuary is the **Codex of Remembrance**: justice tempered by mercy, power tempered by restraint, and harmony held above individual ambition. Drakenvale exists because dragons who once destroyed civilizations in their wars chose to build something else instead: a hidden order where coexistence is maintained not by trust alone, but by law, ritual, memory, and constant vigilance.

Drakenvale does not stand alone. The **Feywood**, the elven realm rooted in the temperate forest west of the basin, has been a sovereign-equal ally since the founding — a separate polity with its own governance, its own people, and its own ancient compact with the sanctuary. The two realms operate by mutual non-interference and mutual aid in crisis. Drakenvale’s history and the Feywood’s are entwined without being merged.

## Governance

### The Draconic Council

Three ancient dragons rule by consensus. Major decisions of lasting political, legal, military, or theological consequence require unanimous approval.

| Dragon | Type | Alignment | Domain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eryndor the Radiant | Ancient dragon | Lawful Good | Justice, diplomacy, moral guidance |
| Zarkeros the Inferno | Ancient dragon | Lawful Evil | Strength, defense, resource management |
| Varethyn of the Amethyst Gaze | Ancient dragon | Neutral | Wisdom, arcane knowledge, balance |

When the Council cannot reach consensus, disputes may be referred to the **Heartstone**, the radiant artifact housed at the center of the Draconic Hall. It is both a remnant of the sanctuary’s founding and a living instrument of guidance, invoked only in matters of genuine deadlock or exceptional communal significance.

### The Codex of Remembrance

The Codex of Remembrance is the legal and moral framework of Drakenvale. Its core principles are:

- **Justice and Mercy** — fairness, proportionality, and opportunity for redemption
- **Balance and Unity** — harmony among alignments and peoples within the sanctuary
- **Responsibility and Restraint** — power wielded for the greater good
- **Sanctity of Sacred Sites** — the Platinum Heart, Amethyst Vault, and sealed Temple of Mordrax are inviolable
- **Secrecy of the Sanctuary** — disclosure of Drakenvale’s location requires unanimous Council approval

Legal violations are addressed through reparations, exile, community obligation, magical binding, or atonement rituals. Execution exists as a penalty but requires unanimous Council approval and is reserved for the gravest offenses, such as attempts to unseal the Temple of Mordrax or acts that place the sanctuary itself at existential risk.

The Codex is a living document. Amendments are proposed and debated at the **Draconic Conclave**.

### Advisory and Civic Roles

Drakenvale’s society is hierarchical, but not draconic in isolation.

- **Drakari Stewards** serve as civic officers, mediators, military leaders, record-keepers, and direct enforcers of Council rulings.
- **Mortals** may advise the Council, petition for redress, and hold skilled roles throughout the sanctuary, but do not possess a formal Council vote.
- **Kobolds** are essential to maintenance, logistics, wardcraft, mining, and engineering. They are protected under the Codex and integrated into education, labor, and sanctuary life.

### The Draconic Conclave

The Draconic Conclave is a gathering of all resident dragons, convened approximately every century or when major structural questions or external threats demand it. Its purpose is to debate amendments to the Codex of Remembrance, address major sanctuary-wide concerns, and formalize decisions too large for ordinary Council process. Its timing is not fixed. It is called by Council decree.

### Sovereign Allies — The Feywood

The **Feywood** is the elven realm bordering the Hollow Crown’s western temperate forest, governed by its own **Council of Elders** at the elven city of Thornveil. The Feywood is not part of Drakenvale; it is a sovereign-equal ally bound to the sanctuary by the **Oath of the Fallen** since the founding. The compact establishes mutual non-interference — each polity governs its own territory — and mutual aid in true crisis. The Feywood honored the mutual-aid clause once in recorded history, during the Discordant War.

Day-to-day relations are conducted at the Feywood Border (the realm-side threshold) and at the elven outer settlement of **Vaelmere**, which handles routine trade and diplomatic contact. Council-level matters route to Thornveil through formal channels. The Feywood’s institutions, geography, and internal life are documented in their own files; this section establishes only the canonical relationship.

## History

### The Founding

After the war remembered as the **War of the Fallen** — culminating in the annihilation of a mortal civilization caught in the crossfire — a coalition led by the **Platinum Warden**, a devotee of Solveris, sought to establish a sanctuary where dragons, drakari, and surviving mortal lineages could coexist under law rather than endless conflict. From that effort came the **Oath of the Fallen**, the foundation of Drakenvale and the beginning of the Codex of Remembrance. The elves of the Feywood, who had lived in their bordering forest since before the founding, emerged in their own time and entered the Oath as sovereign-equal signatories.

### The Hollow Crown and the Central Stone

The sanctuary was not built in an ordinary valley but within the Hollow Crown, the vast basin left inside the broken mountain after the impact. At the center of that basin lay the fused white-and-platinum Heartmass. During the earliest carving of the Stronghold from this central formation, the founders discovered the Heartstone within it.

### Construction

Dragons, drakari followers, kobold laborers, and mortal artisans carved the **Stronghold of Drakenvale** from the Heartmass and expanded the first civic and sacred terraces around it. Ancient glyphs and collective dragon magic wove protective wards into the Hollow Crown itself. The city of Drakenvale grew outward from the fortress, and sacred waters descending from the Stronghold shaped both ritual life and central settlement. The urban core is concentrated most heavily on the southern, lake-facing side of the Stronghold, with lighter spread around its other accessible edges.

### The Age of Harmony

Drakenvale flourished as a center of learning, reflection, magical craft, and guarded prosperity. The Draconic Conclave was established. The Heartstone became the focal artifact of governance. The Trial of Wings — ancient even at the founding — was preserved and reformed as non-lethal, formalized in this age into its current ceremonial structure. The Infernal Forge grew into the realm’s center of enchanted craft and strategic metallurgy, and the city of Zarkharath formed around it over time.

### The Discordant War

A faction known as the **Mordraxian Rebels** sought to unseal the **Temple of Mordrax** beneath what is now the southern dark quadrant. From their fortified keep — the **Dark Hold** — they excavated a ceremonial shaft into the earth, pursuing the dark energy they sought to channel. The **Platinum Warden** fought to the heart of the Hold and gave his life at the pit’s edge, collapsing the shaft and severing the rebels’ connection to Mordrax. With the channel closed, the **Draconic Council** restored the wards on the Temple itself. **Lethariel Heartwood** of the Feywood led an elven contingent alongside the Warden through the war’s final days, fulfilling the Oath’s mutual-aid clause; she was at the pit’s edge when the Warden gave his life. The wound where the Dark Hold once stood is what the realm now calls the **Rift of Discord**. In the aftermath, the Codex of Remembrance was amended to more explicitly forbid actions that destabilized the realm’s hard-won harmony, and redemption rituals were formalized.

### The Trial of Wings

A ritualized duel overseen by the Council, used to settle disputes of honor, grievance, or principle. The Trial is an ancient draconic ritual once fought to the death; preserved by the founders after the War of the Fallen but reformed as non-lethal, and formalized into its current ceremonial structure during the Age of Harmony. It is rarely invoked but highly respected. It is open to dragons and drakari; mortals and kobolds may request it, but most such matters are directed into mediation instead.

## Economy

- **Internal** — barter in enchanted artifacts, knowledge, obligations, and services. Coin exists for practical accounting and everyday exchange, but it is secondary rather than foundational.
- **External** — limited trade with trusted allies, tightly controlled by secrecy and Council policy
- **Key exports** — enchanted goods, magical expertise, rare crafted artifacts, selected refined materials
- **Key imports** — rare materials unavailable within the Hollow Crown, including select metals and specialized arcane substances
- **Trade structure** — regulated through approved channels and trusted intermediaries

Drakenvale’s economy is sustained not only by treasure and craft, but by agriculture, mining, fisheries, magical flora harvesting, and the labor networks that connect the city to the wider basin. The Silver Scale Trading Company handles much of the realm’s major import-export movement, bulk transport, and external trade interface, but it is not the same thing as the city’s ordinary merchant life.

## Religion

| Deity / Figure | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Solveris | Primary sacred anchor — justice, mercy, law, protection; the Platinum Heart is his sanctum |
| Mordrax | Forbidden but theologically unavoidable figure; her sealed Temple stands as a warning against unchecked ambition and corruption |

Spiritual life is maintained by the **Platinum Acolytes**, a religious order aligned with Solveris’s ideals and closely associated with Eryndor’s moral authority. Seasonal ceremonies, atonement rites, sacred observances at the Sacred Pools and Platinum Heart, and the Trial of Wings reinforce Drakenvale’s spiritual identity. Other peaceful faiths consistent with the Codex may be tolerated, but none rival the symbolic centrality of Solveris in the sanctuary’s public religious life.

## Institutions and Public Structure

Drakenvale is sustained by a small number of major institutions whose detailed structure belongs in `groups.md`.

- **Draconic Council** — supreme governing authority of the sanctuary
- **Platinum Acolytes** — spiritual guidance, sacred rites, and holy site maintenance
- **Dragon Guard** — primary external military force
- **Wardens** — internal security, sacred-site protection, and anomaly response
- **Infernal Forge Guild** — enchanted craft, metallurgy, and strategic production
- **Arcane Conservatory** — elite advanced magical study and arcane refinement
- **Hall of Scales** — broad higher learning, scholarship, and formal education
- **Silver Wing Envoys** — controlled diplomacy and external contact
- **Sapphire Sentinels** — covert monitoring of threats to the sanctuary

Other institutions, covert networks, trade bodies, remnant orders, and criminal or dissident groups are maintained in `groups.md` rather than duplicated here.

## Society and Civic Life

Drakenvale is an idealized honor-and-service society, not a utopia. It is orderly, aspirational, and built around the belief that power should improve the realm rather than dominate it, but it still contains underworld networks, cult activity, criminal opportunists, hidden rivalries, and bad actors who exploit secrecy, distance, or trust. Public life is shaped by law, duty, reciprocity, and the expectation that each resident contributes meaningfully to the realm’s stability. Most criminal activity remains hidden because open lawlessness is usually crushed quickly by local authority, Wardens, or guards. As a result, organized underworld activity survives most visibly at the margins of the sanctuary, especially in places like Greymantle, where distance from the civic center, proximity to danger, and negotiated autonomy allow harder elements to persist more openly than they could elsewhere.

In ordinary life, Drakenvale functions as a normal high-functioning fantasy society: markets, workshops, farms, shrines, guild activity, local trade, domestic life, and civic administration all operate openly and continuously. The difference is cultural emphasis. Knowledge, magical learning, martial competence, discipline, courage, and honorable service all carry unusually high prestige. A hardworking farmer, a skilled engineer, a disciplined steward, a capable guard, and a respected scholar may all be honored as people who strengthen the realm.

The society is species-mixed by default. There are no formal species enclaves or standing race conflicts built into the structure of everyday life. People live where their work, family, means, and opportunities place them. Some local clustering may occur by trade, kinship, or convenience, but mixed settlement is normal.

The sanctuary is merit-based in principle and prestige-weighted in practice. Advancement is open to anyone who proves useful, disciplined, capable, or honorable. Good work leads to greater trust, responsibility, and status. At the same time, inheritance, mentorship, family reputation, proximity to power, and resemblance to draconic ideals still influence opportunity. Nepotism exists, but it is culturally frowned upon and politically dangerous if exposed.

Drakenvale places heavy moral weight on belonging and obligation. Residents are expected to make the realm better through service, labor, craft, scholarship, vigilance, cultivation, protection, or governance.

Citizenship exists in two meaningful layers. **Basic citizenship** is ordinarily acquired by birth within the realm or through formally granted acceptance into it, and it carries the protections and expectations of ordinary belonging. **Standing citizenship** is something deeper: the social and political recognition earned when a person proves their worth through service, discipline, office, or meaningful contribution to the sanctuary. Access to the highest levels of trust, institutional authority, and the halls of power depends less on simple residence than on demonstrated value and good standing.

Leaving the Hollow Crown without approval is treated as betrayal and as a major security threat because the sanctuary’s secrecy is one of its foundational protections.

## Social Order

Drakenvale is hierarchical but not caste-segregated. Formal authority, cultural prestige, and day-to-day function do not map perfectly onto one another.

- **Dragons** are treated as the elder-protector nobility of the realm: founders, guardians, exemplars, and potentially catastrophic threats if they ever turn from the Codex. They are deeply respected, but not worshiped.
- **Drakari** hold naturally high standing within society as the closest civic inheritors of draconic culture, governance, and military service.
- **Mortals** are broadly integrated into the same civic world as drakari and may rise to positions of serious responsibility, expertise, and honor.
- **Kobolds** are protected, respected, and indispensable, especially in engineering, maintenance, logistics, mining, and ward support. They can rise very high by merit, but most occupy support and specialist-assistant roles rather than the most publicly prestigious offices.

All dragons stand equal under the Codex of Remembrance and retain the same legal rights. In practice, however, the legacy of the Discordant War leaves an unspoken layer of caution and distrust around questions of loyalty, especially in matters touching the Temple of Mordrax, secrecy, or old fault lines within the realm. This distrust is cultural rather than legal. Solveris devotion is common across the sanctuary, but it does not map cleanly to any single draconic lineage or embodiment.

Kinship in Drakenvale is shaped as much by mentorship, service, oath, and shared work as by bloodline. Social identity is deeply tied to what one contributes and how one carries oneself.

The elves of the Feywood are not part of Drakenvale’s social order. They are sovereign-equal allies under the Oath of the Fallen — guests when they visit, hosts when sanctuary residents reach the Feywood, neighbors at all times. See **Sovereign Allies — The Feywood** under Governance.

## Non-Council Dragons

The Draconic Council are the most visible dragons in public political life, but they are not the only dragons in the Hollow Crown.

Most resident dragons live away from the main city in secluded lairs, caverns, peaks, remote shelves, or other difficult-to-reach regions of the basin. Even the Council dragons do not live as ordinary urban residents; each maintains a distinct sanctum, lair, or domain linked to the Stronghold or to their sphere of influence.

Non-Council dragons are more common in Drakenvale than in most realms, but they are not constantly present in daily street life. Younger dragons are the most visible non-Council dragons. Ancient dragons besides the Council are rare and are more often visitors, recluses, or occasional presences than standing civic actors. Resident dragons typically appear more often during festivals, major rites, public ceremonies, crises, and periods of collective decision-making than in ordinary daily routine.

Resident dragons are not bound to direct compulsory service in a rigid feudal sense. The sanctuary is built on mutual respect, shared conviction, and honor rather than forced service. Still, dragons who reside in the Hollow Crown long-term are generally expected to contribute in some meaningful way, whether through guardianship, teaching, scholarship, mediation, patrol, ritual duty, stewardship of a region, or support of the sanctuary’s institutions. Hermit and recluse dragons exist, but they are exceptions rather than the norm.

In ordinary governance, only the Council votes. In a Draconic Conclave, all dragons in good standing may participate and vote.

For practical record-keeping, non-Council dragons may be understood through a few simple status categories: **Resident Dragons** who live within the Hollow Crown on a sustained basis; **Visiting Dragons** who return seasonally, ritually, or diplomatically without permanent residence; **Sworn Guardians** who take on explicit long-term protective or institutional obligations; and **Recluses** who remain within the sanctuary but outside most civic life. These are descriptive categories rather than rigid castes or offices.

Dragon births are rare, but when dragons aligned with the sanctuary’s values have offspring, it is normal for the young to be raised or initially educated within Drakenvale. In this way, the Hollow Crown functions not only as a refuge, but as a true draconic homeland.

## Education and Advancement

Education is a right in Drakenvale. Basic childhood education is expected throughout the sanctuary, though its form varies by region, trade, and household. Agricultural settlements, workshop communities, and remote towns balance practical labor with foundational literacy, numeracy, civic instruction, and moral formation under the Codex of Remembrance. Wealthier or more connected families may secure more formal schooling, but basic education is not meant to be exclusive.

As residents grow older, education diverges into several major paths:

- **Guild paths** train artisans, engineers, miners, traders, builders, logisticians, and other skilled workers through apprenticeship and structured practice.
- **Civic paths** prepare stewards, administrators, record-keepers, legal assistants, and local functionaries.
- **Temple paths** prepare acolytes, ritual assistants, healers, moral instructors, and sacred-site personnel.
- **Military and security paths** train future guards, Wardens, patrol personnel, escorts, and defenders through formal instruction, drilled discipline, and practical service.
- **Arcane paths** range from foundational magical education to advanced specialization under formal institutions or individual masters.

The principal general institution of higher learning is the **Hall of Scales**, which functions as the sanctuary’s broad academy and university-level center for scholarship, formal education, and general higher learning. It provides education across multiple disciplines, including civic learning, martial theory, history, trade, scholarship, magical foundations, and other advanced study appropriate to a high-functioning sanctuary. It is the main formal institution through which residents pursue broad higher education.

Above it stands the **Arcane Conservatory**, which is not ordinary schooling but elite advanced study. The Conservatory functions as a high-specialization institution for exceptional candidates, dangerous magical theory, refined arcane research, and advanced mastery beyond the level of general education. It is where the most promising arcane talents, advanced spellcasters, and unusually gifted magical students are refined beyond the level of the Hall of Scales.

Advancement in Drakenvale is accessible to anyone who can prove talent, discipline, utility, integrity, or excellence in service. A person may rise through scholarship, military skill, stewardship, engineering, agriculture, craft, ritual service, or public duty. The realm’s ideal is simple: if a person strengthens the Hollow Crown, the Hollow Crown should make room for them to rise.

## Summary

Drakenvale is not merely a hidden city or a dragon keep. It is a complete interior world hidden inside catastrophe: a sanctuary raised from impact-struck stone, centered on a fortress carved from celestial ruin, governed by law, ritual, and old memory, and held together by dragons who chose order over annihilation. Around its Stronghold spread the city, the sacred waters, the grasslands, the forests, the wetlands, the caverns, the highlands, and the scars of past war. Beyond its western threshold lies the Feywood, the elven realm sworn to the Oath of the Fallen as a sovereign-equal ally. Drakenvale’s peace is real, but never effortless. Its unity is genuine, but never simple. Everything in Drakenvale exists in the shadow of what dragons once were — and in the hope of what they might yet become.
