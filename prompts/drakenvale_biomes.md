# Drakenvale Biomes

> Drakenvale contains ten distinct biomes, each shaped by centuries of draconic presence and the valley's deep magical saturation. Each biome has a dominant magical character that affects crafting, spellcasting, and encounter tone.

## Alpine Peaks

**Zone:** The outer mountain ring encircling the valley. Natural barrier and first line of concealment.

**Description:** Towering snow-capped peaks form a natural fortress around Drakenvale. Jagged cliffs, icy slopes, and glacial streams define the terrain. The cold here is magical as much as physical — glacial crystals embedded in the rock are infused with ice magic and serve as passive wards against fire-based intrusion.

**Key Features:**

- Perches and cave systems large enough for ancient dragons
- Glacial streams that feed the valley below
- Ice-infused mineral deposits along cliff faces

**Notable Flora:** Frost-tolerant ironwood trees, alpine moss, crystalline flowers that bloom in ice, Everfrost Moss (stabilizes soil, insulates small creatures), Moonlight Orchid (rare; blooms under moonlight; used in high-level frost enchantments), Frostfire Lily (draws magical creatures).

**Notable Fauna:** Winter wolves, sabertooth tigers, giant eagles, ice drakes, frost wyverns, Aurora Griffon (rare; competes with giant eagles for sky dominance), Snowbound Yeti (territorial; intelligent), Frostwing Owl (silent nocturnal hunter).

**Monsters:** Frost Linnorm, Ice Drake (Chaotic Evil; freezing touch), White Dragons, Silver Dragons, Mountain Landwyrm (Lawful Evil; colossal; sleeps for long periods; its waking causes earth tremors).

**Magical Properties:**

- Glacial crystals act as natural fire-ward components
- Moonlight Orchid petals enhance frost enchantments at high level
- Bloodroot (saprophyte) has necromantic properties usable in frost-aligned dark rituals

**GM Notes:** The Alpine Peaks are Drakenvale's outer shell — players approaching the valley encounter this biome first. Ice drakes and frost wyverns patrol here, not as organized guards but as territorial creatures. The Mountain Landwyrm is a sleeping catastrophe; its lair is a dungeon site in waiting. Silver and White Dragons here are not Council members — they are independent residents or visitors, each with their own agenda.

## Temperate Forest

**Zone:** Valley floor. The most inhabited non-stronghold terrain in Drakenvale.

**Description:** Dense forest of ancient, towering trees with leaves that shimmer with faint magical auras. Thick underbrush alive with enchanted plants. Mystical groves of silver-barked trees and glowing fungi. The forest floor is layered — canopy, understory, floor, and subterranean root systems all host distinct ecologies.

**Key Features:**

- Silverwood trees (silver bark; prized for magical crafting)
- Glowing mushrooms and moonlight lilies throughout
- Whispering vines that "speak" faintly in the wind
- Subterranean root networks connecting to other biomes

**Notable Flora:** Silverwood trees, glowing mushrooms, moonlight lilies, Hangman Tree, Treant and Wood Woad (awakened), Bloodthorn Vine, Assassin Vine, Obliviax (Memory Moss — subterranean; erases memories of creatures that linger), Phantom Fungus (subterranean; invisible fungus).

**Notable Fauna:** Dire wolves, blink dogs, pseudodragons, magical deer (shimmering coats), Sprites (arboreal), Phase Spiderlings, Feyfire Owl, Shambling Mound, Awakened Shrub, Sylvan Stag, Feyfire Fox.

**Magical Properties:**

- Enchanted flora components for potions, scrolls, and magical crafting are abundant here
- Silverwood timber is a premium crafting material
- Obliviax (Memory Moss) is extremely dangerous — contact risks memory loss; used in rare ritual magic
- The whispering vines occasionally carry actual information — ambient echoes of conversations held nearby

**GM Notes:** The forest is the most common zone for non-dragon residents to move through. Players will encounter it regularly. The Obliviax and Phantom Fungus are excellent stealth/horror tools. Treants here are not hostile by default but will act if the forest is damaged. The whispering vines hook — players may overhear something real if they stop to listen.

## Volcanic Highlands

**Zone:** Mountain interior, centered around Zarkeros's lair. Home of the Infernal Forge.

**Description:** Active and dormant volcanoes, jagged basalt formations, steaming geothermal vents, and rivers of flowing magma. The air tastes of ash and iron. The terrain is hazardous to the unprepared and richly rewarding to those who can navigate it. The Infernal Forge Guild operates here.

**Key Features:**

- Active magma rivers feeding underground reservoirs
- Geothermal vents used to power the Infernal Forge
- Fire-infused mineral deposits in basalt formations

**Notable Flora:** Ashfern (filters volcanic toxins), Pyrocactus (heat-resistant; glowing red spines), Cinderbloom Orchid (rare; petals glow like molten lava; used in fire artifact enchanting), Ashwood Cypress (fire-resistant bark; ground into fireproofing powder), Smokeflower (releases ash-vapor clouds when disturbed; used for smokescreens and fire resistance elixirs).

**Notable Fauna:** Fire elementals, salamanders, magma mephits, red drakes, Infernal Wyrmling (territorial juvenile fire drake), Lavafang Basilisk (molten gaze petrification), Ashfeather Roc (massive; creates ash storms with wings), Inferno Bear (fire-infused; hibernates in heated caves).

**Magical Properties:**

- Fire-infused minerals are primary components for weapon and armor crafting at the Infernal Forge
- Cinderbloom Orchid is a rare reagent for high-power fire enchantments
- Ashwood Cypress bark is harvested for fireproofing applications
- Geothermal vents provide power for large-scale enchanting operations

**GM Notes:** This is Zarkeros's home territory. Players who enter without invitation or escort are entering his domain. The terrain itself is a threat — magma rivers, steam vents, unstable basalt. The Infernal Forge Guild operates here but maintains its own internal order. Red drakes and fire elementals are not affiliated with the Dragon Guard — they are wild creatures that Zarkeros tolerates because they reinforce the region's natural deterrent.

## Crystal Caverns

**Zone:** Underground labyrinth beneath the valley. Accessed through cave systems under the Stronghold and forest.

**Description:** A network of crystalline caves glowing with embedded gemstones and magical minerals. Radiant crystals provide soft, shifting light. Pools of luminescent water dot the cavern floors. The walls amplify sound and magic in unexpected ways — spells cast here often behave differently than above ground.

**Key Features:**

- Radiant crystal formations throughout — naturally amplify spellcasting
- Luminescent pools with magical properties
- Connected to Varethyn's lair region

**Notable Flora:** Dreammoss (calming; induces visions in lingerers), Mooncap Mushrooms (restore magical energy when consumed), Mirror Moss (reflects light and illusions; hides secret entrances), Glowkelp (enhances visibility; stores magical energy), Wraithvine (parasitic; feeds on magical essence; appears as faintly glowing threads), Necrofingers (absorbs necrotic energy; spores repel undead).

**Notable Fauna:** Spectral Bat (semi-incorporeal; phases through walls), Crystal Wyrm (gem-scaled; tunnels through crystal walls), Arcane Scorpion (stinger injects magical venom; used in potion crafting), Shardrat (collects crystals and magical items for nests), Webspinner Arachnid (webs trap magical auras as well as creatures), Crystal Frog (amplifies magical vibrations; sought-after familiar).

**Magical Properties:**

- Crystal resonance amplifies all spell effects — beneficial and harmful equally
- Arcane Scorpion venom is a potent potion component
- Dreammoss lingering effect can be used deliberately to induce vision-states in willing subjects
- Mooncap Mushrooms are a renewable source of magical energy restoration

**GM Notes:** The Crystal Caverns are Varethyn's natural domain — his Lair Sight trait operates through every gemstone here. Players exploring the caverns should assume they may be observed. The amplification effect means that combat here is more dangerous for everyone. The Dreammoss vision mechanic is a delivery system for cryptic information or prophecy. Shardrats that have accumulated items are essentially random loot tables with legs.

## Mystic Wetlands

**Zone:** Valley edge, where the terrain transitions from forest to marsh. Borders the outside world more closely than other biomes.

**Description:** Enchanted marshlands of foggy terrain, glowing pools, floating islets, and naturally occurring magical mist. Dreamlike and disorienting. The water here is infused with restorative properties but the fog obscures navigation and harbors creatures that use it deliberately.

**Key Features:**

- Magical fog that impairs navigation and conceals terrain features
- Glowing pools with restorative water
- Floating islets of vegetation accessible only by flight or careful movement

**Notable Flora:** Dream Lotus (induces peaceful dreams; used in sleep and vision potions), Venomveil Vine (secretes disorienting mist), Spectertrap Pitcher (carnivorous; captures magical auras), Soulbind Vine (used in binding rituals), Ghostflame Pipe (fungus; glows faintly blue near magical decay), Twilight Mangrove (marks enchanted groves), Glowspike Reed (navigational aid; natural lantern).

**Notable Fauna:** Will-o'-wisps, giant frogs, spectral fish, Dreamstride Stag (glowing antlers; revered guardian), Mirage Lynx (blends with magical fog; appears and disappears unpredictably), Shadowmuck Salamander (teleports short distances), Bogchant Bullfrog (magical vibrations — calming or disorienting), Duskswirl Serpent (shimmering trails; magical venom), Faelight Nymph (larvae grow into marsh-lighting dragonflies).

**Magical Properties:**

- Wetlands water is a base component for healing and restorative potions
- Dream Lotus is a key reagent for sleep, vision, and divination spells
- Soulbind Vine is used in binding and containment rituals
- The fog itself can be harvested in small quantities for concealment enchantments

**GM Notes:** The Mystic Wetlands are the most liminal biome — closest to the outside world, dreamlike, disorienting. Will-o'-wisps here are not mindless; they are drawn to magic and may lead characters toward or away from things based on their own alien logic. The Mirage Lynx is an excellent recurring background detail — players may keep seeing it without ever being certain it's the same creature. Dream Lotus is a trade commodity the SSTC values highly.

## Draconic Grasslands

**Zone:** The open interior of the valley. Primary social and ceremonial space for dragons.

**Description:** Rolling meadows of shimmering, magically-charged grass. Scattered boulders serve as perches and markers. Seasonal wildflowers bloom in iridescent hues. Dragons bask here, hold informal council, and use the open space for the Trial of Wings and other ceremonies. The soil itself is charged with latent magic.

**Key Features:**

- Dragon perch boulders throughout the meadows
- Iridescent seasonal wildflower blooms
- Dragongrass — resilient to heat and cold; grows back within hours of being burned or frozen

**Notable Flora:** Dragongrass, sunflowers with golden seeds, shimmering fey-touched wildflowers. Flora here is largely mundane but magically enhanced — ordinary plants grow exceptionally fast and yield more than normal.

**Notable Fauna:** Scaled deer (draconic traits), iridescent birds, wyvern hatchlings, grazing creatures of various kinds. No apex predators — this is a protected zone by custom if not by law.

**Magical Properties:**

- Soil magic accelerates plant growth; agricultural plots near the grasslands yield double output
- Golden sunflower seeds are a trade good — minor magical properties when consumed
- The open sky and charged ground make this the optimal location for the Trial of Wings

**GM Notes:** The Draconic Grasslands are where players are most likely to encounter dragons in a social rather than threatening context — basking, talking, or observing. The Trial of Wings takes place here. Wyvern hatchlings wandering the area are not pets; their parents are nearby. The grasslands' visibility cuts both ways — there is nowhere to hide, which both protects and exposes players in equal measure.

## Feywood Glade

**Zone:** A fixed pocket within the Temperate Forest. Its boundary is identifiable from outside — its interior does not obey normal spatial logic.

**Description:** The Feywood Glade exists simultaneously in this plane and the Feywild. Its physical location in Drakenvale is fixed — the boundary can be found and recognized. But inside, geography does not behave normally. You may enter from one point and exit into a completely different part of Drakenvale. Paths between internal landmarks shift. Time inside does not correspond reliably to time outside. The glade is not playing tricks — it simply connects to the world differently than ordinary space does. It predates Drakenvale. The Draconic Council has no authority within it and is wise enough not to try.

**The Heart Tree:** At the deepest point of the glade stands an ancient tree of unidentifiable species at enormous scale. It is the land's consciousness made physical — the convergence point where the Feywild bleed-through is strongest and the glade's awareness is most legible. It is not a deity and does not communicate in language. Creatures near it receive impressions, emotional states, images that reflect the glade's current condition. Some receive nothing. It is not a character the GPT runs — it is a presence the GPT describes. Communications are felt rather than heard, interpreted rather than understood, and deliberately ambiguous.

**Key Features:**

- Fixed external location; variable internal spatial logic
- Entry and exit points do not correspond — plan for unexpected emergence locations
- Time variability — rough equivalence for short visits, increasing divergence for extended stays
- Ambient internal light regardless of outside time of day — never fully dark, never fully bright
- The Heart Tree at the deepest navigable point

**Notable Flora:** Fey Orchids (charm and compulsion magic component; taking without acknowledgment causes navigational difficulty), Illusion Blossoms (mild visual hallucinations on skin contact; stronger on consumption; used in illusion crafting), Moonlit Bark Trees (tended by dryads; bark has minor warding properties; harvesting without acknowledgment is the most reliable way to make the glade actively hostile to navigate), Glowing Toadstools in fey circles (stepping in without awareness risks brief Feywild displacement — disorienting, occasionally productive), edible flora interspersed with toxic and perception-distorting flora with no visual rule distinguishing them.

**Notable Fauna:** Pixies, dryads, sprites, magical butterflies, various fey creatures. Not hostile by default — capricious. They have no investment in player success or failure, only in whether players are interesting. Elves and long-lived peoples live at the glade's edges and serve as informal intermediaries.

**The Logic of the Glade:** The glade has no rules — it has a nature. Consequences flow from understanding or misunderstanding that nature. Respect the land as a living thing. Know what you're touching. Understand that a promise made here carries weight fey take seriously. Do not attempt to dominate or control — the glade is older than any such attempt and will outlast it.

**Magical Properties:**

- Fey Orchid extract: potent component for charm, compulsion, and counter-enchantment magic
- Ideal environment for illusion and enchantment spell crafting — the Feywild bleed-through amplifies these schools specifically
- Fey circle toadstools mark locally strong Feywild convergence points — useful for planar attunement work
- Heart Tree proximity: non-verbal divination, land-state readings, cryptic plot-relevant impressions

**Drakenvale's Relationship:** The glade is a known, culturally present space most residents know about and very few spend real time inside. Elven communities at its edges serve as the interface — they know the glade, know its current layout well enough to navigate it, and know who should and shouldn't go in. Among dragonborn youth, entering alone and returning safely is an informal test of capability. A small group of Drakenvale residents — mostly elves, some druids — have deep working relationships with the glade and harvest materials for the Arcane Conservatory. The Council's position is formally neutral: residents who enter knowing what it is do so at their own risk.

**GM Notes:** Tone is the opposite of the Shadowed Hollows — wonder and unpredictability rather than dread. Players who approach with curiosity and respect have a fundamentally different experience than players who treat it as a dungeon to be solved. The variable exit point is the primary mechanical tool — use it to move players to unexpected locations, create time pressure, or disorient confident parties. Use it narratively, not punitively. The Heart Tree encounter should feel genuinely alien: no dialogue, no quest, no explicit transfer. Impressions, emotional states, images that may or may not be relevant to what the players are doing. Fey inhabitants should be played with genuine capriciousness — interested in players as phenomena, not as threats or allies. The glade is highly rewarding for players who engage correctly: rare crafting materials, fey knowledge, long-term fey relationships, Heart Tree access as non-linear information delivery.

## Sacred Pools

**Zone:** Exterior grounds of the Platinum Heart. Considered part of the Platinum Heart's sacred perimeter.

**Description:** A series of serene, mirror-still pools connected to the Platinum Heart. The water reflects the sky with uncanny accuracy even when cloud cover should distort it. An aura of deep peace pervades the area — aggression is physically difficult to sustain here. The Platinum Acolytes tend these pools and conduct rituals at their edges.

**Key Features:**

- Mirror-still surfaces regardless of weather or disturbance
- Aura of tranquility — hostile intent is suppressed (DC 14 Wis save to act aggressively while within 30 ft. of a pool)
- Platinum Acolyte presence at all times

**Notable Flora:** Celestial lilies, lotus flowers, radiant moss. All plants here are infused with divine energy — they cannot be uprooted or damaged without desecrating sacred ground.

**Notable Fauna:** Spectral fish (semi-transparent; harmless), guardian serpents (divine; protect the pools; will engage threats), celestial swans (intelligent; Bahamut's symbolic presence).

**Magical Properties:**

- Pool water is used in divine rituals and holy artifact crafting
- The Platinum Chalice is filled here during Rites of Justice
- Drinking from a Sacred Pool grants 1d6 temporary HP and advantage on Wisdom saves for 1 hour (once per long rest per character)
- Extended contact with the pools is said to provide clarity on moral dilemmas — the GPT can use this as a divination/guidance mechanic

**GM Notes:** The Sacred Pools are the safest location in Drakenvale — and the most politically significant to approach uninvited. The tranquility aura is a useful brake on escalating conflicts. Characters seeking Eryndor's favor or Platinum Acolyte assistance will be directed here. The guardian serpents are not aggressive unless provoked; they observe and remember.

## Shadowed Hollows

**Zone:** Directly above the underground Temple to Tiamat. The valley's darkest quadrant, adjacent to the Rift of Discord.

**Description:** The Shadowed Hollows is not a natural biome — it is a symptom. Centuries of necrotic energy seeping upward through the rock from the sealed Temple below have saturated the soil, warped the flora, and made this zone inhospitable to anyone not attuned to Tiamat's power. The Rift of Discord lies nearby but is a separate wound with a separate origin — their proximity means this entire corner of Drakenvale carries a density of dark energy found nowhere else. The Hollows has no clean boundary; it bleeds into the surrounding forest gradually, the light dimming and air thickening as you move deeper. No one holds this space. It is watched, not controlled.

**Key Features:**

- Perpetually dim regardless of time of day — magical light sources function at half normal radius
- Shadows move independently of light sources; peripheral motion is constant
- Paths shift subtly between visits — reliable navigation requires magic or an experienced guide
- The air carries a faint metallic taste, like old blood or charged stone
- Sound is slightly muffled — the Hollows absorbs it
- In places where the rock above the Temple is thinnest, the ground is dead and black, nothing growing

**The Corruption:** The necrotic energy is passive — Tiamat is sealed, not directing it. Ambient, slow, cumulative. It works psychologically before physically.

*For neutral and good-aligned creatures:* Each hour without leaving, DC 13 Wisdom save. Failure advances one corruption level — **Unease** (narrative only; sense of being watched, intrusive thoughts), **Dread** (disadvantage on Wisdom checks), **Erosion** (disadvantage on Wisdom saves and attack rolls against evil-aligned creatures). A long rest outside clears one level. Trained Wardens and Sentinels have advantage on saves but still make them.

*For Tiamat's followers and chromatic-aligned creatures:* The opposite effect. The energy resonates rather than corrodes — clarifying, empowering, magic feels more responsive. Chromatic dragons entering feel something like coming home. This asymmetry is why the space cannot be cleared: no aligned force can sustain presence long enough.

**Notable Flora:** Shadow Ferns (absorb light; deepen local darkness; concealment enchantment component; harmless to touch but disorient distance and direction perception), Nightshade Blossoms (faint violet-black luminescence; pollen causes magical paralysis on skin contact; used in poison crafting; Sentinels track harvest activity as cult intelligence), Deathroot Vine (dark red tendrils; necrotic toxin on broken skin contact suppresses healing for 24 hours — healing spells restore half value; used by cult in binding and curse rituals; has passive proximity awareness and tightens when approached), Black Moss (absorbs ambient magical energy; spellcasters who rest in dense areas wake with one fewer spell slot; used in dark ritual preparation requiring magical silence).

**Notable Fauna:** Shadow Mastiffs (coordinated pack hunters; enlarged and more aggressive than standard from prolonged Temple exposure; appear to have rudimentary awareness of Tiamat's followers and leave them alone — functioning as passive cult guardians; primary threat to patrols), Giant Spiders (nest in deepest zones; webs nearly invisible in ambient dimness; venom carries mild magical paralytic not found in ordinary specimens), Necrotic Wraiths (echoes of creatures that died here over centuries, bound by the necrotic field; cannot leave the Hollows; hostile to positive energy — healing magic and divine light draw their attention; Tiamat's followers know how to move without triggering them; function unintentionally as sentinels), Corruption-Drawn Visitors (rogue chromatic dragons, corrupted dragonborn, Tiamat-attuned creatures passing through as waypoints, meeting locations, or power sources; Sentinels track these movements).

**The Cult Presence:** The Hollows is the primary gathering location for Tiamat's followers within Drakenvale. The resonant energy amplifies their magic; the natural defenses provide cover no structure in Drakenvale could offer. The cult maintains no fixed installation — they leave traces (ritual markings, Deathroot arrangements, blackened ceremony ground) but no permanent base. By the time a patrol reaches a recent site, they are gone. Sapphire Sentinel focus: tracking frequency and scale of activity, identifying recurring participants, monitoring for increases — because increasing activity is the leading indicator of a deteriorating Temple seal.

**Warden and Sentinel Operations:** No fixed presence. Wardens rotate on strict two-hour maximum in-zone windows. Sentinels work in shorter observation bursts. Patrol pattern is perimeter-focused — interior patrols only when Sentinel intelligence indicates specific activity. Rules of engagement: observe, document, withdraw. Do not engage shadow mastiff packs unless directly attacked. What they watch for: wraith population increases (more wraiths = stronger seepage = weakening seal), Nightshade and Deathroot harvesting, new ritual markings, any chromatic dragon presence (immediate escalation trigger).

**Magical Properties:**

- Necrotic energy for dark magic crafting — curses, necromantic components, shadow enchantments
- Nightshade Blossom pollen: magical paralysis component
- Deathroot Vine extract: potent healing-suppression poison
- Black Moss: magical silence component for ritual preparation
- The corruption asymmetry itself is a resource — Tiamat's followers gain tangible benefits from extended presence

**GM Notes:** The Hollows is a pressure gauge for the Temple seal. Current state — stable shadow mastiff population, moderate wraith presence, occasional cult activity — reflects sealed but leaking. If the seal begins to fail, the Hollows changes first: corruption radius expands, wraith numbers increase, mastiffs more aggressive, cult activity surges. Players can read the Temple's state by reading the Hollows.

This is the only location in Drakenvale where players may encounter Tiamat's cult, chromatic dragon agents, and corrupted residents with no Dragon Guard or Warden backup available. That asymmetry is intentional. A chromatic-aligned or Tiamat-following player character has significant advantage here and will be noticed by the cult — both an opportunity and a complication to play into rather than flatten.

## Rift of Discord

**Zone:** The valley's dark quadrant, adjacent to the Shadowed Hollows. A separate wound from the Discordant War — not the meteor impact site, not the Temple location, but a scar from the war itself.

**Description:** A chasm of floating rock shards, glowing rifts in the air, and completely unpredictable magical weather. No sustained life exists here — the magic is too chaotic. The ground around the Rift is scorched and fractured. The Rift itself pulses with energy that shifts color and intensity. Approaching it feels like approaching something that is aware of you.

**Key Features:**

- Floating rock shards at various heights — some stable, some rotating
- Rifts in the air that flicker between colors (each color corresponds to a different damage type or magical effect)
- Unpredictable magical weather — areas of wild magic surge, dead magic zones, and spatial distortions
- No flora; the only fauna are summoned or chaos-touched creatures

**Magical Effects (roll d6 on entering, or GM selects):**

1. Wild magic surge (roll on Wild Magic table)
2. Spell slots temporarily reduced by one level for 1 hour
3. Random damage type dealt to all creatures (1d6, type determined by rift color nearest)
4. Teleportation — creature moves 10 ft. in a random direction
5. Dead magic zone — no spells function for 1 minute
6. Heightened magic — next spell cast is maximized

**Notable Fauna:** Chaos elementals, shadow drakes, wandering undead drawn by residual necrotic energy from the Discordant War.

**Magical Properties:**

- Chaotic energy can be harnessed for powerful, unstable enchantments — high reward, high risk
- Rift shards (floating rock fragments) carry residual chaos magic; used as components in wild magic and chaos-themed crafting
- The Rift is the single location in Drakenvale where the Ptarian Code's prohibition on chaotic magic is most severely tested

**GM Notes:** The Rift is Drakenvale's defining wound. It is not a dungeon — it is a place of narrative weight and mechanical danger. The Platinum Warden died here. The Temple to Tiamat is closest to this zone. The Shadowed Hollows border it. A party that enters the Rift without a specific purpose is taking on genuine risk for no clear reward. A party that enters with purpose — to harness the chaos energy, to investigate a disturbance, to attempt to close or stabilize it — has one of the most significant possible story hooks in the world.

## Biome Connections and Adjacency

```
         [Alpine Peaks — outer mountain ring]
                        │
         [Volcanic Highlands] ── [Zarkeros's Lair]
                        │
[Temperate Forest] ── [Draconic Grasslands] ── [Stronghold / Meteor Center]
       │                        │
[Feywood Glade]          [Sacred Pools] ── [Platinum Heart]
       │
[Mystic Wetlands] ── [Valley Edge / Outside World]

[Crystal Caverns] ── [Beneath all of the above]

        [Shadowed Hollows] ── [Rift of Discord]
               │
        [Temple to Tiamat — underground, below Hollows]
```

**Key geographic notes:**

- The Stronghold sits at the valley's center — the original meteor impact site. The rare minerals throughout the valley radiate outward from this point.
- The Shadowed Hollows and Rift of Discord share the valley's darkest quadrant — neither is at the center. The Hollows sits above the underground Temple; the Rift is a nearby but separate scar from the Discordant War.
- The Feywood Glade is physically within the Temperate Forest but spatially separate — its internal geography does not obey normal rules.
- The Crystal Caverns underlie most of the valley and have access points at multiple surface locations.
