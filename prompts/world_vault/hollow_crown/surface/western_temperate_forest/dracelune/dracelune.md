# Dracélune

The realm's western trade capital and the threshold city between ordinary Drakenvale life and the untamed Feywood beyond. Dracélune sits at the edge of the western temperate forest where the land softens toward liminal Fey-touched terrain, its streets and plazas built across stilted platforms, stone causeways, and terraced riverside districts. It is the first and last true city the Silver Scale Trading Company's caravans pass through on their inbound and outbound routes, the primary realm-side staging point for any expedition into the Feywood, and the most culturally porous settlement in the Hollow Crown. Dracélune is lively, music-thick, commercially fast, and quietly permissive in ways that make it feel less like the rest of Drakenvale and more like itself.

## Scene Texture

Dracélune is built where the western temperate forest meets the low river country that runs toward the Feywood threshold. The terrain is uneven — stands of old-growth forest, braided waterways, rising and falling ground, and the occasional stretch of marshy flat where the water table pushes up through the soil. The city has adapted to its geography rather than flattening it. Buildings rise on stone pilings where the ground is wet, on terraced platforms where it slopes, and on ordinary foundations where the land is dry and stable. The result is a settlement that feels organic, layered, and impossible to fully grasp from a single vantage.

The approach from the central realm follows the SSTC trade road westward through the temperate forest. The first glimpses of Dracélune are rooflines between the trees — pitched slate, copper-green weathered, decorative ridge ornaments in styles that reflect the city's mixed architectural heritage. Then the outer market gates, then the broad promenade along the eastern river crossing that marks the city's inbound threshold. A traveler crossing into Dracélune is crossing from the realm's interior into its threshold zone, and the shift in character is immediate.

The city smells of river water, pine, and smoke from the hearths of a hundred taverns and cookhouses. Music is nearly constant — street musicians in the plazas, small bands playing outside the better taverns, formal Sapphire Choir performances scheduled at the civic amphitheater, and the background of someone practicing an instrument from an upstairs window. Not every part of the city is loud, but no part of the city is quiet.

Architecture is eclectic. The older buildings follow a mixed western-temperate style with heavy timber framing, steep roofs for rain shedding, and carved wooden facades that bear decorative motifs drawn from forest fauna, river imagery, and the occasional Fey-touched pattern that local artisans have adapted from materials brought in from the Feywood. Newer construction borrows from across the realm — Drakenvale City stonework, Zarkharath metalwork accents, touches of Hall of Scales academic formality in the civic buildings. The effect is varied but not chaotic. Dracélune has absorbed influences and made them its own.

The population is the realm's most culturally mixed. Every species present in Drakenvale is present here, in closer-to-equal proportions than anywhere else. Beyond species diversity, Dracélune attracts the realm's travelers, the itinerant, the temporarily-between-somewhere crowd: traders who spend half their year on the road, guides who lead Feywood expeditions, scholars researching cross-border phenomena, performers on circuit, craftspeople drawn by the city's willingness to buy unusual goods. The social fabric is denser and faster than elsewhere. Friendships form quickly and sometimes briefly; business arrangements are made over shared meals; rumors travel well.

The river running through the city's heart is called the Dracé, and the city's name derives from it — "moon over the Dracé," loosely, from the old western-temperate dialect. The river is the spine of the settlement's layout and its primary commercial artery. SSTC barges move goods upriver and downriver; smaller craft carry local trade; the eastern bank is the formal civic side and the western bank is the rougher, more permissive trade side. Both banks are bridged by six stone crossings, each with its own character.

## Districts

Dracélune organizes around its geography and its trade function:

- **The Inbound Quarter** — the eastern-side commercial district where SSTC caravans arrive and unload. Harbor-style warehouses, customs offices, the Silver Scale Trading Company's regional headquarters, the formal market plaza for bulk trade, and the inns and lodgings oriented toward inbound travelers.
- **The Civic District** — the administrative and cultural core on the eastern bank. The Mayor's Hall (Dracélune's civic administration, answerable to the broader realm), the Civic Amphitheater, the Sapphire Choir's western chapter hall, and the formal residences of the city's senior officials and merchant families.
- **The Bridge Markets** — the middle zone between the two banks, built across the six river crossings and the reclaimed river-flat land between them. Retail markets, smaller guild workshops, specialty craft shops, traveler-oriented services, and the majority of the city's music venues. The most vibrant part of the city during daylight and early evening hours.
- **The West Bank** — the rougher, more permissive commercial zone. Lower rents, less regulation, more willingness to trade in goods of uncertain provenance. The city's most interesting taverns, its shadow-trade interfaces (however quietly), and the Feywood Outfitters district.
- **The Feywood Outfitters** — a specialized sub-district on the West Bank near the road out to the Feywood threshold. Specialized gear shops, guide services, information brokers who know the Feywood's current conditions, and the Hall of Guides headquarters. A traveler preparing for a Feywood expedition comes here for everything.
- **The Dracé Lowlands** — the marshy peripheral districts where the river spreads into shallow reaches before the settlement thins. Older residential neighborhoods, fishing operations, and the city's poorer working population. Not dangerous by civic standards, but unmonitored in ways the central city is not.

## Functions

- **Trade hub.** Dracélune is the primary realm-side interface for the Silver Scale Trading Company's inbound and outbound caravan routes. Virtually all trade goods moving between the Hollow Crown and the controlled external world pass through this city. The Inbound Quarter's infrastructure is built for this volume.
- **Feywood threshold.** Dracélune is the realm's main staging point for Feywood expeditions. Outfitters provide specialized gear; guides provide route knowledge and escort services; information brokers provide current Feywood conditions and warnings; the Hall of Guides coordinates the guide profession. Every credible expedition into the Feywood begins (and, if successful, ends) here.
- **Cultural crossroads.** The realm's most culturally porous settlement. Travelers from across Drakenvale and the controlled handful of external contacts converge here, and the city's music, food, crafts, and conversation carry influences from throughout the realm and, in subtler ways, from beyond it.
- **Civic administration.** The Dracélune Mayor's office handles local governance, coordination with the Administrative Quarter in the Stronghold, and the specialized legal and customs work that a border trade city requires. Civic authority here is more commercially focused than in Drakenvale City proper.
- **Shadow trade reality.** Every major trade hub hosts an underworld economy. Dracélune is no exception. The shadow trade handles smuggling, information brokering beyond the licensed tier, the movement of Feywood-origin materials that would be questioned through legitimate channels, and the occasional personal-escape traffic. The Dragon Guard and Wardens work against this, but the shadow economy persists. It stays genuinely hidden — the city's visible face is clean, the underground is underground, and those who cross back and forth between the two do so carefully.

## Vetra Ebony

Vetra Ebony is the canonical senior figure for trade-adjacent, transport-adjacent, and Silver Wing–adjacent matters in Dracélune. Her role is quieter than it seems — she handles routes, permissions, introductions, and the threshold politics that a border trade city requires to function. She is not the Mayor, not the senior SSTC authority in the city (that is Kaerys's deputy), not the Guild of Guides' head. She is the *broker* who knows everyone, has favors out with most of them, and can move things that the more visible authorities cannot quietly arrange.

Characters seeking passage, permissions, quiet introductions, access to specific people or places, information that is not publicly available, or the resolution of threshold matters that do not fit cleanly in any institution's jurisdiction — those characters end up talking to Lethira, one way or another.

She operates from an office on the eastern bank, in a building that is neither imposing nor hidden but somehow rarely noticed by people who do not know where to look.

## The Hall of Guides

The Hall of Guides is Dracélune's specialized professional body for Feywood expedition guidance. Membership is by examination, demonstrated experience, and sponsorship. A certified Hall of Guides guide carries the professional credibility necessary to lead paid expeditions, handle insurance and liability through standardized contracts, and coordinate with the Feywood-adjacent institutions on both sides of the threshold.

The **Master of Guides** is the Hall's senior authority. Tier-3 generative role. Typically a veteran of many successful expeditions (and, by definition, no fatal unsuccessful ones) who has earned the trust of the profession. The Master handles guide certification, dispute resolution, and the interface with the Mayor's office and the Wardens on expedition-related matters.

## The Silver Scale Trading Company Presence

Kaerys Emberclaw's primary residence is Drakenvale City, but the SSTC's largest operational presence is in Dracélune. The SSTC's western regional headquarters occupies a substantial warehouse complex in the Inbound Quarter, handling caravan receipt and dispatch, the administrative work of international trade, and the specialized commerce that flows between the realm and its approved external contacts. Tazrik Flameweaver is in Dracélune more often than anywhere else, managing caravan logistics from the company's operational center here.

## Character and the Feywood Disposition

Dracélune has a distinctive cultural disposition. Decades of sitting at the threshold of the Feywood — where people go in and sometimes do not come back — have shaped the city's response to uncertainty. Residents are pragmatic about risk. They respect the wild because they have watched what happens to people who do not. They are quick to laugh, quick to form connection, quick to enjoy what the day offers, because a significant fraction of the people they love have left for the Feywood and not returned.

The local saying — *the world rolls on* — captures it. It is not nihilism. It is acceptance with grace. Things happen. The wild is the wild. What you can do is live well in the time you have, respect what deserves respect, and keep moving with whatever comes.

This disposition colors every aspect of Dracélune's social life: the easier friendships, the more frequent music, the food and drink treated as genuine pleasures rather than perfunctory needs, the casual generosity toward strangers who might not be around tomorrow. Visitors often describe Dracélune as *warm* in a way Drakenvale City is not. The warmth is real, and it is partly a response to how regularly the city loses people.

## Access

Dracélune is fully open to travelers. The SSTC road from the central realm arrives from the east; the Feywood threshold road departs westward. Six river crossings connect the banks; the city's gates are formal but permissive, inspecting for contraband and registering new arrivals without restricting access.

Dragon Guard presence is substantial at the city's perimeter and in the Inbound Quarter, handling customs, caravan security, and the formal interface with external trade. Wardens maintain a liaison station focused on Feywood-related anomaly response and the monitoring of shadow-trade activity that crosses institutional thresholds.

Foreign visitors arriving via SSTC caravan are processed at the Inbound Quarter's customs offices. Most are cleared for city access; some with specific expedition credentials may receive Feywood-threshold permits; a few are turned back for reasons the city does not make public. Once inside the city proper, travelers move freely.

The Feywood threshold is a controlled boundary. Passage across it is by permit, by sanctioned guide escort, or by the unauthorized-crossing path that anyone with functioning curiosity eventually learns exists and usually should not take.
