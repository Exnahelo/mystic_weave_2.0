# Dewhollow

The realm's cultivator of Fey-influenced flora, set in the western temperate forest where the ordinary forest gradually softens toward the Feywood threshold. Dewhollow grows and harvests the plants that exist only in this liminal zone — the morning-bell flowers, the silverleaf herbs, the glasswort reeds, the various Fey-adjacent specimens that do not survive transplantation to ordinary terrain. Its residents are herbalists, cultivators, alchemists, and the occasional scholar on field assignment, drawn to the village by the specialized work rather than born to it. The village is small, quiet, and more technically skilled than any settlement of its size has any reason to be. Its harvests move to Dracélune for realm-wide distribution and to the Arcane Conservatory for specific commissions.

## Scene Texture

Dewhollow sits in a shallow forested hollow — the geographic feature that gives the village its name — where morning mist collects and holds through most of the day. The mist is the village's defining atmospheric signature. Residents call it the dew; it is heavier than ordinary morning fog, persists longer, and carries the faintly sweet scent of the Fey-adjacent flora that grows throughout the hollow. A visitor arriving in the early morning sees the village through the dew first and the village itself second.

The approach from Dracélune follows forest paths that gradually diverge from the main Silverwood Trail, turning westward into less-traveled terrain. The transition is subtle — the forest becomes quieter, the light changes character slightly, the air takes on a different quality. By the time a traveler reaches Dewhollow's outer boundaries, they have crossed from ordinary temperate forest into terrain that is still on the realm's side of the Feywood threshold but is demonstrably affected by the proximity. This is where the specialized flora grows. This is why Dewhollow is here.

The village itself occupies the hollow's floor and gentle surrounding slopes. Buildings are modest — single-story, timber-framed with deep eaves for the persistent damp, thatched or slate-roofed. The central clearing holds the common hall, the drying sheds where harvested flora is cured, the preparation workshops where herbalists process specimens into stable forms for transport, the small inn (the Morning Bell), and two taverns — one frequented by locals, one by visiting scholars and traders.

The cultivation gardens extend outward from the village in carefully tended plots. Unlike Silvercut's minimal-footprint forest management, Dewhollow actively shapes its growing spaces — the plots are cleared and maintained, soil is amended, moisture is managed, sun exposure is controlled through strategic canopy thinning. The cultivation is intensive because the plants are demanding. Morning-bell flowers will not bloom where the ground has been disturbed recently. Silverleaf herbs require specific moisture cycles. Glasswort reeds grow only in damp soil that has been ritually prepared with a particular mineral infusion. Each specialty requires its own protocol.

The residents working the gardens look different from residents of other supply villages. Fewer generational family patterns, more visibly individual — a young tiefling cataloging specimens in a leather-bound field book, an older half-elf directing apprentice cultivators through a silverleaf harvest, a dragonborn herbalist grinding dried material into preparation powders with the attention of someone who has spent decades perfecting the technique. The village's workforce is drawn from across the realm rather than born to the place. Nearly every adult here arrived as an apprentice, studied under a senior practitioner, and either stayed because the work suited them or moved on to somewhere else.

The sounds of Dewhollow are subdued. The cultivation work is quiet by necessity — loud disturbance affects certain specimens. Conversation in the gardens is pitched low. Even the common hall tends toward quieter evenings, as residents who spend their days in concentrated work prefer their rest to match. The village's humor is dry and specific to the herbalist's temperament — jokes about botanical frustrations, running commentary on the plants' personalities, the kind of specialized wit that only makes sense to people inside the work.

## Functions

- **Fey-influenced flora cultivation.** The village's primary work. Species grown include the morning-bell flowers (a mild sedative in infusion, a component of certain sacred rites), silverleaf herbs (a digestive aid and a common alchemical base), glasswort reeds (used in specific Conservatory ward-work and in the finer craft of Dracélune's glass artisans), dreamroot (a rare specimen used in controlled doses for certain meditative practices), and a dozen other Fey-adjacent species of varying economic importance.
- **Raw material preparation.** Dewhollow processes its harvests to stable forms for transport — dried, pressed, tinctured, or otherwise stabilized so that the materials retain their properties during the journey to market. This is specialized work. Improperly prepared Fey-flora can lose potency, become unstable, or in rare cases develop unintended effects during storage.
- **Conservatory commissions.** The Arcane Conservatory maintains standing commissions with Dewhollow for specific research and practical needs. A commission might call for a rare specimen at a particular developmental stage, a specific preparation protocol, or an unusual harvest timed to a particular season. The Conservatory's ongoing relationship with the village is significant and mutually respectful.
- **Dracélune market supply.** Beyond the Conservatory commissions, most Dewhollow output moves to Dracélune for broader market distribution. The village's goods feed Dracélune's alchemical trade, the Feywood outfitting economy (travelers preparing for Feywood expeditions often stop for specific herbs), and the higher-end culinary and apothecary markets of the realm.
- **Feywood-adjacent expertise.** The village's residents know the liminal zone between the realm and the Feywood better than almost anyone outside the Dracélune Hall of Guides. Informal knowledge-sharing occurs between Dewhollow and the Hall when relevant; Dewhollow residents occasionally accompany Hall of Guides expeditions as specialist advisors on Fey-touched terrain.

## Civic Character

The village is run through the Cultivar Warden's office. The **Cultivar Warden** is the village's senior civic authority, traditionally a senior herbalist of significant experience who has demonstrated both technical mastery and community-building skill. Tier-3 generative role. The role is semi-elected — senior practitioners nominate candidates among themselves, and community consensus confirms the appointment. Tenure runs indefinitely, typically until the Warden chooses to step back.

The **Senior Herbalist** is the village's technical authority — the senior-most practitioner on active cultivation work, consulted on difficult specimens, responsible for training apprentices in the more advanced preparation techniques, and the coordinator of Conservatory commissions. Tier-3 generative role. The Senior Herbalist's authority is craft-based and runs parallel to the Cultivar Warden's civic authority.

The village's social fabric differs from other supply villages in its structure. Dewhollow has a genuine apprenticeship tradition — most residents arrived as trainees, worked under senior practitioners for several years, and either integrated into the community as permanent residents or departed as trained herbalists to work elsewhere. The village exports trained practitioners nearly as much as it exports plant material. A Dewhollow-trained herbalist working anywhere in the realm carries a meaningful professional credential.

Inter-generational family patterns exist — some families have stayed for multiple generations — but they coexist with the constant flow of new apprentices and departing journeymen. The village's character is more dynamic than most places its size. Population turnover over a decade is substantial.

## The Feywood Relationship

Dewhollow's proximity to the Feywood Glade shapes the village's operating reality. The Feywood is dangerous terrain. Its effects bleed into the surrounding forest in subtle gradations — the further from the threshold, the less pronounced; the closer, the stronger. Dewhollow sits in the zone where the bleed is strong enough to support the cultivation of Fey-adjacent flora but not so strong that ordinary life becomes impossible.

Residents adapt to the proximity. They pay attention to the specific weather patterns that precede heavier Feywood bleed. They notice when the morning dew becomes denser than usual — a sign the threshold is in one of its more active phases. They know which flora respond to these phases by blooming early, changing color, or becoming temporarily more potent. They know which days are best for which work.

The village also accepts a baseline level of ambient weirdness. Plants sometimes move slightly in ways that wind does not explain. Certain harvested specimens occasionally need to be held down during preparation. A new cultivator who has not yet calibrated to the village's working reality occasionally has a short crisis of confidence that experienced residents helped them through with dry humor and a cup of tea. These are ordinary occurrences. The village has learned to absorb them.

Crossing into the Feywood proper from Dewhollow is discouraged but not physically prevented. The threshold is close; an unwise traveler could reach it in an afternoon's walk. The village's standing advice to visitors is simple: don't. The Hall of Guides at Dracélune handles sanctioned Feywood expeditions. Unsanctioned crossing from Dewhollow into the Feywood produces occasional disappearances that the village notes with regret but does not attempt to rescue.

## Character

Dewhollow is quiet, focused, and deeply specialized. Residents are technical, observant, and generally patient — qualities the work demands and selects for. A traveler visiting the village on legitimate business (a commission, a consultation, specific trade) is received helpfully and efficiently. A traveler visiting without clear purpose finds the experience pleasant but limited; there is not much for a casual visitor to do in Dewhollow beyond walk the paths and admire the gardens, and the village's social life is largely closed to outsiders who do not share the work.

Hospitality is professional rather than warm. The inn is clean and well-run. Meals are excellent — cooking traditions benefit from access to the village's specialty flora, and Dewhollow's kitchens produce food that is better than a village of its size should be able to produce. Conversation is available but selective; residents engage on topics where the visitor has something useful to say and retreat from chatter that does not serve a purpose.

The village has a distinctive relationship with knowledge. Information is shared readily with those engaged in the work. Information is guarded closely from those whose interests are unclear. A scholar from the Hall of Scales arriving with a proper introduction from a known practitioner receives full cooperation. The same scholar arriving unannounced with vague questions receives polite but limited response.

## Access

The village is open to travelers with purpose. The forest paths from Dracélune bring commissioned purchasers, visiting scholars, apprentice candidates, and the occasional traveler with specific business. The path to the Feywood Glade Border departs westward from the village and is used by sanctioned expedition traffic passing between Dracélune and the Feywood. Dewhollow sits on this route and sees regular expedition-related visitors.

Accommodation is at the Morning Bell inn or in the handful of small guesthouses operated by senior families. Rooms are clean, meals are substantial, and rates are reasonable. Extended stays (for apprentices in training, scholars on field assignment, or commissioned purchasers awaiting specific harvest windows) are negotiated through the Cultivar Warden's office.

Dragon Guard presence is absent. Wardens visit occasionally for Feywood-adjacent anomaly response but maintain no resident presence. Local order is handled by the Cultivar Warden's office and the village's own social norms.

Travelers wandering off the village's managed paths into the Feywood-adjacent zone face real risk. The plants are occasionally unfriendly. The terrain shifts subtly in ways that can disorient an unprepared visitor. The village provides guides for legitimate need and declines to rescue those who venture without purpose.
