# Silvercut

The realm's principal timber village, set in the western temperate forest north of Dracélune among the silverwood stands that give the settlement its name. Silvercut's economy is forest work — logging, milling, hunting, trapping, and the specialty craft of cutting and curing silverwood for the realm's finer construction and fine-craft needs. The village itself is small and compact, its buildings clustered tightly to minimize forest clearing, and its residents keep the long quiet rhythm of people whose work is done under canopy rather than under sky. Finished timber and hunting goods travel south to Dracélune for market distribution; the village feeds its own workforce and keeps a modest surplus.

## Scene Texture

Silvercut sits in a small clearing surrounded by old-growth western temperate forest. The clearing is not natural — it was cut by the village's founders generations ago and has been maintained at its current size through careful management ever since. Expansion into the surrounding forest is deliberate and slow. The village's cultural principle, carried down through the logging families, is that the forest exists first and the village exists with its permission.

The approach from Dracélune follows the Silverwood Trail northward through the forest. The trail is well-maintained but narrow — wagons pass but two wagons cannot pass each other without one pulling into a designated wayside. The forest crowds close on either side. Travelers arriving at Silvercut emerge from canopy into the village's central clearing with the suddenness that forest travel always produces.

The village is compact. A central square holds the common hall (a broad single-story structure built from silverwood beams and local fieldstone), the mill (water-powered, fed from a diverted stream that runs through the village's eastern edge), the smithy, the inn (the Silver Axe), and the two small taverns. Residences cluster tightly around the square, built in the local timber idiom with pitched roofs for shedding forest debris and deep eaves against the forest's frequent rain.

The silverwood trees themselves are the village's defining sight. Tall, straight-trunked, their bark has a subtle silver-gray coloration that catches light differently than ordinary forest timber. Stands of silverwood extend in every direction from the village, their distinctive canopy creating patches of the forest where the light has a faint silver quality on clear days. A visitor walking into a silverwood stand notices the light before they notice the trees.

Beyond the central clearing, the village's working infrastructure extends into the forest along managed paths: the primary logging camps where active cutting happens, the smaller hunting cabins distributed along game trails, the skinning stations, the smokehouses for preserving game, and the apiaries that produce the village's distinctive forest-flower honey. The paths are well-worn but not obvious to outsiders. A visitor wandering off the central trails can lose orientation quickly.

The sounds of Silvercut are forest sounds — wind through canopy, birdsong at varying distances, the rhythmic chop and saw of active logging work in the surrounding stands, the occasional crack of a falling tree in the distance. The village itself is quiet. Residents speak at moderate volume, move without hurry, and attend to their work with the focused concentration that forest labor requires.

## Functions

- **Silverwood harvesting.** The village's specialty crop. Silverwood is valued across the realm for its combination of light weight, structural strength, and subtle luminous quality when polished. It is used in fine construction (civic buildings, upper-tier residences, the occasional instrument), furniture work, specialty tools, and — rarely — in enchanted craft where the wood's natural properties complement magical work. Harvesting is selective; a mature silverwood is identified, inspected, and cut only when the stand's overall health permits.
- **General timber production.** Beyond silverwood, the village harvests the forest's other timber species for ordinary construction, fuel wood, and specialty work. Pine for construction, hardwoods for furniture, soft deadwood for kindling. The mill processes everything to realm-standard lumber sizes.
- **Hunting and trapping.** The forest holds substantial populations of deer, boar, smaller game, and the distinctive western-forest predators that require experienced hunters to take safely. Hunting is licensed through the Hunts-Master's office and tracked against sustainable yield estimates. Hides, furs, smoked meat, and specialty products (antler for craft use, certain glands for alchemy) all move south to Dracélune.
- **Honey and forest specialty products.** The village's apiaries produce forest-flower honey with a distinctive complex flavor drawn from the silverwood blossoms and the forest's other flowering species. Modest production, high value. Certain medicinal mushrooms, rare berries, and other forest specialties are also harvested in small quantities.
- **Craft support for Dracélune.** Silvercut supplies a significant portion of the raw material used in Dracélune's craft economy. The connection is economically central for both communities — Silvercut depends on Dracélune's market access, and Dracélune's craft shops depend on Silvercut's material supply.

## Civic Character

The village is run informally through the Woodwarden's office. The **Woodwarden** is the village's senior civic authority, traditionally a senior logger or forester with deep knowledge of the forest's patterns and the village's sustainable-yield practices. Tier-3 generative role. The Woodwarden handles dispute resolution, forest management decisions (which stands are cut in which seasons, which game populations require rest periods, how logging crews are rotated), and the formal interface with Dracélune and the broader realm.

The **Hunts-Master** is the senior hunting authority — licensing, coordination, training of new hunters, and the specialist's voice in forest matters involving wildlife. Tier-3 generative role. The Hunts-Master's authority is parallel to the Woodwarden's rather than subordinate; the two offices coordinate on overlapping concerns.

The village's social fabric is tight and family-based. Logging families, hunting families, and the smaller group of craft families supporting both have intermarried for generations. Most Silvercut residents can trace their ancestry to the village's founding period. Outsiders marrying into the village integrate slowly; those who stay become residents by demonstrated commitment over years rather than by ceremonial acceptance.

## The Forest Principle

Silvercut's operating ethic deserves explicit description because it shapes everything the village does. The founding generations established a principle that has carried forward: the forest is a living system that predates the village, and the village's continued existence depends on respecting that system's carrying capacity. In practical terms:

- **Selective harvest.** No clear-cutting. Mature trees are selected for cut; younger stands are left to develop; specific areas are rested for years at a time.
- **Game population monitoring.** Hunting quotas are set seasonally based on observed population health. When populations drop below thresholds, specific species are protected until they recover.
- **Minimal clearing.** The village expands its permanent clearing only when absolutely necessary and only after extensive consultation among senior residents. Most growth happens within the existing footprint.
- **Quiet practice.** Noise, light, and general disturbance are kept low in the surrounding forest. Logging operations follow protocols that minimize impact on adjacent stands.

This principle is not enforced by external law. It is internalized culture. A logger who violated the principle would face social consequences within the village — not formal punishment, but a steady withdrawal of cooperation and trust that would make their work untenable. The Woodwarden's office formalizes the practice but does not create it.

The Arcane Conservatory has studied Silvercut's forest-management practices more than once over the years, and the Hall of Scales includes them in certain curriculum modules. The village takes quiet pride in this recognition but does not advertise it.

## Character

Silvercut is quiet, hardworking, and traditional. Residents are neither unfriendly nor especially outgoing. A traveler asking for directions gets direct help; a traveler asking for conversation gets conversation that flows only as long as the traveler's contribution warrants. The village's hospitality is real but measured. Travelers passing through on legitimate business are treated well; travelers lingering without purpose find the experience politely unrewarding.

Humor runs dry and understated. Residents tell long jokes that take three days to reach their punchlines if the traveler sticks around that long. Craft pride is visible everywhere — the quality of the silverwood work, the mill's output, the smokehouse's products, even the village's own buildings all show craftsmanship that goes beyond what mere function requires.

The village's relationship to Dracélune is affectionate but asymmetric. Silvercut residents consider Dracélune crowded, noisy, and occasionally silly — but also necessary, and many Silvercut young people spend years in Dracélune during their late adolescence before returning home with a broader perspective. The reverse is less true; Dracélune's residents think of Silvercut as quaint and rarely visit.

## Access

The village is open to travelers. The Silverwood Trail from Dracélune brings steady traffic — caravans for timber and hunting goods, occasional craftspeople coming to select specialty materials, the occasional Silver Wing envoy passing through on forest-edge business. The western temperate forest's other internal trails connect to Silvercut from several directions; overland travel from the central realm passes through this region on its way toward the Feywood threshold.

Visitors are lodged at the Silver Axe or in the small private rooms offered by two of the village's senior families. Accommodation is spare but clean. Meals are simple and substantial.

Dragon Guard presence is absent — the village is deep within the safe realm interior and has never required formal garrison. Wardens visit occasionally for forest-edge anomaly response (rare incidents of Feywood-adjacent irregularity that reach Silvercut's outer hunting range) but maintain no resident presence. Local order is handled by the Woodwarden's office and the village's own social structure.

The forest beyond the village's managed paths is genuinely dangerous. Travelers who wander off the trails without guidance face real risk — disorientation, predator encounters, exposure during sudden weather. The village provides guides for travelers who need to venture into the deeper forest for legitimate reasons. Travelers who venture alone without cause are not rescued.
