# Feywood Glade Border

The realm-side threshold to the Feywood Glade, where the road from Dracélune ends and the western temperate forest gives way to the Glade's liminal territory. From outside, the Border shows as an ordinary forest edge — a line of older, taller, stranger trees standing somewhat differently than their realm-side neighbors, the ground beyond them shifting in quality in ways that a traveler notices without being able to name. There is no wall, no gate, no physical barrier. The Border is marked by stone waymarkers set in place over generations and by the small Hall of Guides station that manages sanctioned expedition traffic from here. The realm honors an ancient alliance with the Feywood Glade: safe passage at the Border is reserved for those with guide sanction or elven welcome; unsanctioned entry is tolerated but genuinely consequential. Travelers who cross without preparation rarely return as they departed, and some do not return at all.

## Scene Texture

The approach from Dracélune follows a forest road westward from the city's edge. The road is well-maintained for the first several miles — this is a realm-civic route like any other — and then gradually, as it nears the Border, its character shifts. The road's edges are less sharply defined. The surrounding forest grows quieter in subtle ways. The sounds of realm wildlife diminish while the light under the canopy takes on a slightly different quality. A traveler on the road can feel the Border approaching for perhaps a quarter mile before reaching it.

The Border itself is a specific physical zone rather than a line. The realm-side forest's familiar oaks, beeches, and pines give way to trees of similar species but subtly different bearing — older, taller, standing slightly farther apart, their canopies reaching higher. The trees at the Border are recognizably the same kinds of trees as grow elsewhere in the realm, but they carry themselves with a quality that realm-side trees do not. A Dewhollow cultivator describing the Border's trees once observed that realm trees grow; Feywood trees watch.

The ground at the Border changes character. Realm-side forest floor gives way to ground that is somewhat more densely carpeted with moss and leaf litter, with ferns appearing more thickly, and with occasional patches of bioluminescent fungi that glow faintly even in daylight. The transition is not sharp. A traveler walks forward and finds themselves in terrain that has become the Border without any specific moment of crossing.

The **stone waymarkers** are the Border's most explicit marker. These are upright stones, roughly twice the height of a person, placed at intervals along the known sanctioned crossing points. The stones are ancient — some date to the sanctuary's founding period, and all have been in place for generations. They are carved with symbols that are not realm-common script; the markings are elven, recognizable to those familiar with Feywood work but not otherwise legible. The waymarkers indicate where sanctioned crossing has traditionally occurred. Crossing the Border at a waymarker is recognized by both realm and Glade conventions as a proper crossing.

The **Hall of Guides station** sits a short distance from the primary waymarker pair — a modest wooden-and-stone structure maintained by the Hall of Guides from Dracélune. The station is not heavily staffed; typically one or two guides are present, sometimes the Border Steward specifically, sometimes a rotating assignment from the Hall's senior ranks. The station provides: expedition registration, guide assignment for sanctioned crossings, briefing materials about current Glade conditions, emergency shelter for travelers who arrive at the Border in distress, and the coordination point for returning expeditions re-entering the realm. It is not an inn — travelers bound for the Glade or returning from it overnight at Dracélune, not here.

The realm-side air at the Border is different from ordinary forest air. It carries a faint sweetness — similar to but distinct from Dewhollow's morning dew — and a quality that travelers describe as "awaiting." The air is not unpleasant; it is present in a way that ordinary air is not.

Visitors standing at the Border can often see into the Glade's realm-facing edge — the first several paces of Feywood terrain are visible from the threshold zone. What they see looks like ordinary forest continuing beyond. The trick is that the ordinary forest they see does not extend as far as it appears; a few paces in, the geometry of the space begins to do whatever the Glade has decided the geometry of the space will do that day.

## The Crossing

Crossing the Border is the event that matters. Travelers crossing with sanction — accompanied by a Hall of Guides guide, or carrying an elven welcome-token, or under formal diplomatic protocol — cross safely. The crossing itself is experienced as a specific moment: a shift in the air, a change in the quality of sound, a brief sensation of passage through something that is more than ordinary air. Sanctioned travelers arrive at their intended destination within the Glade — typically the realm-facing elven settlement that handles external trade, or further if their expedition has approved business deeper in.

Travelers crossing without sanction have a different experience. The crossing is the same initial moment — the shift, the change, the brief passage-sensation — but what happens next is not reliable. The Glade's liminal geometry does not treat unsanctioned entrants kindly. They may:

- Find themselves in the Glade but not where they expected, with no clear path back
- Walk for hours or days through terrain that does not resolve into recognizable features
- Exit the Glade eventually through an exit they did not choose, emerging into the realm at some location potentially far from where they entered
- Meet Feywood residents whose dispositions toward unsanctioned visitors vary from curious to unfriendly
- Experience time passage that does not match the time that has passed in the realm
- Not return at all

This is not hostility from the Glade in an ordinary sense. The elves of the Glade do not seek out unsanctioned visitors to harm them. The Glade's own nature simply does not accommodate those who enter without proper relationship to it — in the same way that a wilderness does not accommodate someone who enters it without appropriate preparation. Those who respect the wood, the wood respects. Those who enter without respect, the wood does not protect.

The realm's Hall of Guides and the elven Feywood Council both take the same position on this: the Border is open, the crossing is available, the consequences of unsanctioned crossing are the traveler's own.

## The Hall of Guides Presence

The **Hall of Guides Border Steward** is the senior Hall figure regularly stationed at the Border. Tier-3 generative role. The Steward handles expedition registration, assignment of guides to specific expeditions, briefing travelers on current Glade conditions, coordination with elven traders arriving at the Border for mercantile exchanges, and the practical work of maintaining the Hall's Border operations. The Steward typically rotates in and out of Border duty on multi-week schedules; continuous Border presence is required, but individual Stewards spend most of their working time in Dracélune.

Expedition traffic through the Border is modest but steady. Scholars with approved Glade research commissions. Traders conducting the established mercantile exchanges. The occasional diplomatic courier on realm-Glade coordination matters. Rarely, Council-sanctioned representatives traveling to the elven Council on formal business. Each expedition is guided; none cross without a Hall of Guides member accompanying.

Guides who specialize in Glade expedition work carry specific knowledge of the crossing, the current safe paths once inside, and the techniques for re-exiting the Glade at the intended Border location rather than somewhere else in the realm. This knowledge is professional, carefully maintained, and canonically the Hall of Guides' most valuable expertise.

## Mercantile Exchange

The realm-Glade mercantile relationship is structured through periodic **exchange windows** conducted at or near the Border. During an exchange window — typically scheduled in advance, lasting several days — elven traders bring Feywood goods to the Border zone and realm traders (primarily from Dracélune, with SSTC coordination) bring the realm's contributions. The exchange happens on the realm side of the Border, in the cleared area near the Hall of Guides station where temporary trading stalls are erected.

Feywood goods traded include: fey-crafted herbal preparations, certain enchanted woods and plant materials unavailable in the realm, specific artifacts of elven craftwork, and occasionally magical items whose provenance the elves choose to share. Realm goods provided include: magical crystals from Deephollow (a significant and valued contribution), magical materials brought in via SSTC from beyond the realm, specialty metals from Cinderpit, and certain manufactured goods the Glade residents value.

An **Elven Trade Envoy** is the senior Feywood figure who presents at exchange windows. Tier-3 generative role. The Envoy is typically a long-tenured elven trader with established realm contacts — not a Heartwarden or Council member, but a skilled mercantile figure empowered to conduct the exchange on the Glade's behalf. The Envoy does not live at the Border; they cross from the Glade for the exchange window and return afterward. Realm residents who interact regularly with the Glade's mercantile relationship often know specific Envoy-figures by face if not by full name; the Envoys rotate, but slowly.

Between exchange windows, the Border is quieter. Casual traffic is minimal. The Hall of Guides station operates its normal coordination work. The stone waymarkers stand where they have always stood. The air continues to await.

## Functions

- **Realm-side sanctioned threshold.** The Border is the realm's recognized entry point to the Feywood Glade. Sanctioned crossing occurs here; unsanctioned crossing also occurs here, and the realm's institutional response is calibrated accordingly.
- **Expedition infrastructure.** The Hall of Guides station, the stone waymarkers, and the adjacent cleared terrain serve as the realm's operational infrastructure for Glade expedition traffic. The Hall of Guides from Dracélune handles the institutional work.
- **Mercantile exchange site.** Periodic exchange windows conduct the realm-Glade mercantile relationship at the Border. Elven traders come here; realm traders come here; the exchange happens on realm soil but in the Border's liminal atmosphere.
- **Elven diplomatic contact point.** Formal diplomatic matters between the realm and the Feywood Glade typically route through the Border initially. The elves do not typically travel into the realm's interior; the realm sends delegations to the Border (or, for very formal matters, the realm's delegation is escorted into the Glade by elven welcome). The Border is the neutral meeting ground.
- **Return point for returning expeditions.** Travelers returning from the Glade aim to re-exit through the sanctioned Border. Guides accompanying expeditions manage this exit carefully; the Glade's liminal geometry means that improper exit can deposit travelers anywhere in the realm. Exiting through the proper Border, under guide supervision, is the reliable way to return.

## Character

The Border has a specific atmospheric character that distinguishes it from the realm's other threshold zones. It is not frightening in the Rift Edge's way; it is not corrupted in the Hollows Approach's way; it is not militarily serious in the Platinum Oath Approach's way. The Border is *awaiting*. The space attends to what happens at it without malice and without particular benevolence. Travelers passing through it for sanctioned crossing often feel the attention but find it neutral. Travelers approaching for unsanctioned crossing sometimes feel the attention shift character, but this is subtle and easily overlooked.

The stillness at the Border is different from the Hollows' listening quiet. The Hollows watches the traveler. The Border awaits the crossing. The distinction is real for those sensitive enough to notice it, though many travelers do not consciously perceive it.

Hall of Guides staff stationed at the Border develop a specific relationship with the zone over time. Border Stewards who have spent years in the work describe the Border as having moods — there are days when the crossing feels easier, days when it feels more charged, days when the Glade's interior seems closer to the threshold and days when it feels further away. These moods are not predictable from realm weather or calendar; they are Feywood phenomena experienced at the Border. Senior Stewards pay attention to the moods and sometimes reschedule non-urgent crossings based on them.

The Border carries a cultural significance in the realm's broader consciousness. It is widely known — every realm resident knows there is a Feywood Border and what it means — but rarely visited. Most realm residents will never see the Border in person. Those who have seen it often describe the experience in terms that do not fully translate to those who have not. This is appropriate. The Border does not resolve into ordinary description.

## Access

The Border is accessible to any realm resident with road access from Dracélune. There is no formal checkpoint at the realm-side approach; the road simply leads to the Border, and travelers arrive when they arrive. The Hall of Guides station does not gate the Border; it provides services for those who want them and registers sanctioned crossings.

**Sanctioned crossing** requires:
- A Hall of Guides guide accompanying the traveler, OR
- An elven welcome-token (rare but real — certain realm figures carry tokens from specific elven contacts that permit individual crossing with Glade recognition), OR
- Formal diplomatic protocol for Council-level delegation

Unsanctioned crossing is not physically prevented. Travelers who choose to cross the Border without sanction are permitted to do so. The Hall of Guides station staff will observe the crossing, record the traveler's description, and note the time — these records are kept against the possibility that the traveler's family may later inquire whether they returned. The Hall does not attempt to stop unsanctioned crossings; the order's position is that adults may make their own choices regarding the Glade, and the Glade itself enforces consequences more effectively than any realm-side intervention could.

**Exchange window attendance** is open to realm residents who have legitimate mercantile interest — typically SSTC-sanctioned traders, specific craft figures, and occasionally individual purchasers seeking specific Feywood goods. Arrangements are made through Dracélune's Hall of Guides or directly with SSTC coordination.

**Dragon Guard presence** at the Border is minimal — the realm-Glade alliance is not a security matter requiring garrison, and an overt military presence at the Border would be inappropriate to the diplomatic relationship. The Hall of Guides handles operational coordination entirely.

**Wardens** are not resident at the Border and rarely visit. The Border is not a corrupted zone; it does not fall within Warden anomaly-response jurisdiction in the ordinary sense.
