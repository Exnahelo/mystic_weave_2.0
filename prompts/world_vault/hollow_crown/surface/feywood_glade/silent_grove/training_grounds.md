# The Training Grounds

Where Heartwardens are made. Seven years; sustained work; shaped by elder trees that have watched generations of this training and are themselves part of the teaching.

## Scene Texture

The Training Grounds occupy a portion of the Silent Grove's working interior — a set of worked spaces integrated with the elder-tree landscape rather than carved out of it. Training happens beneath canopy that is older than any other in the Glade; the ambient field is continuously present and specifically attentive; the register is disciplined in the quality that gives the Grove its name.

The Grounds are not a military compound. There are no barracks in the realm sense; trainees in residence during their cycle occupy quiet housing clusters elsewhere in the Grove. There are no parade grounds. The training spaces are integrated with the forest — a long running-path through specific terrain for endurance and mobility work; sparring circles in clearings of moderate size; archery lanes that thread through older growth at measured distances; quiet spaces for druidic instruction where movement is still and the work is interior.

Sound during active training is purposeful: weapons meeting, feet on worked ground, occasional instructor voices giving direction at conversational volume, the shift of canopy that the Grove always carries. Between training periods, the Grounds are as quiet as the rest of the Grove.

## The Seven-Year Cycle

Heartwarden training runs on a seven-year cycle. Trainees enter together in small cohorts (typically eight to sixteen), progress through the cycle together, and emerge as sworn Heartwardens at the end. The cycle includes:

**Years 1-2: Foundation.** Elven martial fundamentals — combat forms with Silverbark Ash spear and Longbough bow, movement in forest terrain, endurance development, basic druidic literacy. Trainees sleep in their Grove quarters and spend daylight hours under instruction.

**Years 3-4: Integration.** Combat becomes integrated with field-reading and ambient-field awareness. Druidic practice deepens. Trainees begin extended solitary postings — weeks or months alone in specific terrain — as their judgment develops.

**Years 5-6: Specialization.** Trainees develop toward specific Heartwarden roles within the order — Watch rotation work, combat coordination, liaison with specific Glade institutions, deep-wild competence. Individual instruction increases as the cohort's shape settles.

**Year 7: Sworn Preparation.** The final year integrates the cohort's work, prepares the trainees for their sworn oaths, and involves the senior Heartwardens and the Elder Druid of the Heartwood in the transition. Oaths are sworn at the Heartwood itself, not at the Training Grounds; the final work prepares the candidates for that moment.

Not all candidates complete the cycle. The Glade does not moderate training to accommodate the unsuited; those whose path is elsewhere find themselves directed there, with dignity and without shame. Most who enter finish; some do not, and their other paths are respected.

## Working Spaces

Specific training spaces within the Grounds include:

- **The Long Path.** A running and endurance course threading through the Grove's terrain over several miles. Used throughout the cycle; specific segments are reserved for specific skill levels.
- **The Sparring Circles.** Three clearings of moderate size for armed and unarmed practice. Paired work dominates; free-for-all practice is rare.
- **The Archery Lanes.** Long lines of sight through older canopy, worked at measured distances for shortbow and Longbow practice. Targets are natural — marked trees, specific stones, occasional moving targets when instructors work with advanced trainees.
- **The Quiet Spaces.** Smaller clearings and canopy-enclosed rooms for druidic instruction, ambient-field practice, and the still work that Heartwarden training requires in equal measure with the active.
- **The Ritual Ground.** A specific clearing reserved for Heartwarden observance — oath-practice (not the final oaths, which occur at the Heartwood), preparation ceremonies, the shared work of the cohort's cohesion.

## Instructors

Training is delivered by senior Heartwardens rotating through the Grounds on multi-year instructor postings, coordinated by the Heartwarden Captain (interim, with Lethariel in Captain-emeritus). Druidic instruction comes from Silent Grove druids — typically the Elder Druid and specific senior druids with Heartwarden-adjacent experience.

The Heartwarden Instructor role is the senior daily-instruction figure — a Heartwarden with sustained training experience, handling cohort-level oversight and direct instruction for specific skill areas. Multiple Instructors serve at any given time, each with specialty areas.

## Visitors

The Training Grounds do not receive casual visitors. Heartwarden order business can bring sworn Heartwardens through for specific purposes; Silent Grove druids move through as part of their broader Grove work; occasional Council business requires specific access. Realm visitors are not received here as a matter of canonical practice.

Observing a training session without being part of the instruction is unusual; most visits are substantive working visits.

## Authoring Notes

The seven-year Heartwarden training cycle is canonical (druidic_grove.md, now silent_grove.md). Any scene involving a Heartwarden trainee should respect this timeline.

Oaths are canonically sworn at the Heartwood, not at the Training Grounds. GPT scenes involving Heartwarden oath-taking should locate at the Heartwood.

Training is canonically integrated with druidic practice. GPT should avoid realm military-academy tropes; Heartwardens are not produced by drilling and shouting.

Cohort size is approximately 8-16, canonically. Specific cohorts can be invented at scene time.

The Heartwarden Captain is currently interim (Lethariel is emeritus, resuming on Sylvara's departure). Specific interim Captain NPCs can be invented; the register is senior but provisional, with awareness that Lethariel will return to the role.

Threat level 1; training is serious work with real physical risk but not combat-level danger under ordinary conditions.
