# Heartwater Basin

The Basin is the Feywood's water. A central lake, irregular in outline, sits across terrain that shapes Thornveil's geography and the deeper interior beyond. Rivers feed in from higher ground in the Outer Old-Growth Wild and exit through channels that eventually find their way out to the Border's drainage. The water is consistently cool, clear enough to see a wading heron's legs to the knee, and carries the same faint ambient quality the rest of the Feywood does — a freshwater brightness that realm-side water does not quite replicate.

## Scene Texture

The lake's edges are reeded and irregular. Stands of tall reed alternate with patches of open shore, and the shore itself varies — in some stretches it is clean gravel that boats can put in against, in others soft mud under a carpet of moss, in others a ragged transition through willow-analog stands and riparian thicket. There are no docks in the realm-built sense; there are elven landing places, worked into the shore with the same organic restraint that characterizes Thornveil's architecture.

Sound in the Basin is its own register. Heartpond frog chorus at dusk is one of the Feywood's canonical ambient sounds; on a Standing Green evening it carries for great distances. Cranemoor heron calls come across the water sporadically. Silverstream trout rise in the shallows when the light is right. Under canopy where the reeds thin, the quiet is deeper than anywhere else in the Feywood — water-silence, not forest-silence, distinct in character.

Boats are present but restrained. Elven craft tend toward narrow, shallow-draft designs worked from fallen wood; they move under paddle, not under sail. The Basin is not the sort of place where a trader's galley would make sense. Realm visitors who ask about boat traffic learn quickly that the water is a working ecology, not a transit lane.

## Ecological Role

The Basin is the second zone in the Feywood's seven-stage decline order. Thornveil's cultivated plots register Heartwood stress first; the Basin's aquatic life follows. Specific signals druids watch for:

- **Fish populations.** Silverstream trout thin in Turning. Absence of visible trout in the shallows when conditions should support them is an early warning.
- **Amphibian chorus.** Heartpond frogs reduce their evening chorus, then cease. Silence in warm-season evenings where chorus should be is a significant signal.
- **Waterfowl behavior.** Cranemoor herons depart early, moving to the outer drainages or beyond the Border entirely.
- **Surface behavior.** The water's surface holds a specific quality in Standing Green — a fineness of movement, a consistency of response to wind. In Turning it becomes flatter and more metallic. Druids reading water can distinguish Standing Green from early Turning by surface alone.

These signals are closely monitored. A rotation of druids from the Silent Grove attends the Basin on a schedule that varies by season and by ambient-field conditions; between scheduled attendance, local elves living around the Basin's margins carry the observation load informally.

## Fauna

Major species:

- **Silverstream trout** — primary protein source for the Feywood; steady population under Standing Green.
- **Lakepike** — apex fish of the lake; large, ambush, rare but known. Older elven fishers speak of specific pike holding specific water for generations.
- **Feywood otter** — common along shallows and reed-margins; small family groups; handled gently by the elves who share their water.
- **Cranemoor heron** — wading species; large but light-framed; canonical sentinel of the waterline.
- **Heartpond frog** — chorus species; health-indicator by sound as much as by sight.
- **Silverback marten** — mustelid along river-banks and outer drainage.
- **Briar-stinger** — biting insects in warm-season evenings. Nuisance but localized.

Occasional **oldroot bear** visits during seasonal fish runs. The bears work specific pools on a schedule elven fishers know and accommodate.

## Flora

Riparian species dominate the margins:

- **River Reed Onion** — culinary plant, tended in elven riparian plots.
- **Reedbeds** — habitat structure, seasonal indicator, material for Thornfiber cane work.
- **Watermeadow grasses** — margin species; tolerant of seasonal inundation.
- **Dewberry bramble** at drier margins — berry shrub feeding birds, otters, bears, and elven gatherers alike.

Shade margins carry the full canopy mix of the surrounding forest — Elderoak, Silverbeech, Grey Beech, stands of Resinheart Pine at higher banks.

## Human Use

Elven riparian plots handle the Basin's cultivated work — reed onion tending, watermeadow management, aquaculture in the loose sense (no farmed ponds, but active encouragement of trout spawning habitat). Thornfiber cane is gathered seasonally for basketry, binding, and training apparatus. Fishing is a working practice, not a sport; nets are used more than rods; the catch is small and consistent rather than large and irregular.

Realm visitors to Vaelmere occasionally request access to the Basin for scholarly purposes — realm freshwater ecology specialists, in particular, have sometimes been granted escorted access over decades. Access for trade purposes is rare; the Basin's produce does not move in volume.

## Travel Notes

- From **Thornveil**: the Basin borders the city on several sides. Elves walk to it routinely.
- From **Heartwood Estate** (Outer Old-Growth Wild): several hours' walk to the nearest regular access point, or less depending on which drainage one follows.
- The Basin has no sanctioned crossing from the Border; realm traders do not pass through it.

## Authoring Notes

The Basin is canonically the Feywood's second decline-register zone (Phase 1 §VII health-register order). GPT narration of Basin scenes should weight the health-register lightly in Standing Green and more visibly in Turning. Stillfall-era Basin narration is severe — surface darkened, choruses silent, water withdrawn from margins.

The absence of any sacred-site designation is intentional. The Basin is ecologically central but not ritually designated. Druidic attendance is practical observation, not ceremonial service.

Fishing and aquaculture are both canonically restrained practices. The catch is small. No commercial-scale harvest. Realm visitors should not be allowed to fish the Basin without elven host approval regardless of how reasonable the request seems.

Boats exist but are small. No realm-style galleys, barges, or watercraft-with-sails appear in the Basin.

The Basin connects Thornveil, Heartwood Estate (reachable by following the drainage), and the broader Feywood region. It does not directly connect to the Silent Grove, the Heartwood itself, or the Border — those are reached through other pathways.
