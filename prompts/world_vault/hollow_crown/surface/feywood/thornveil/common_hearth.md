# The Common Hearth

Where most of Thornveil lives. Where most of its daily work happens. Where the city's ongoing relational life accumulates, cluster by cluster, year by year, generation by generation.

## Scene Texture

The Common Hearth reads, on first impression, more like a well-integrated forest village spread over considerable ground than like a realm city district. The canopy is continuous but lower than the Noble Groves — Thornveil-managed mature growth rather than old-growth individuals of extraordinary age. Understory is more varied, with cultivated stretches and working plantings mixed into the residential pattern. Paths are more developed than in the Approach or the Groves: not paved, but well-worn, consistently marked by use, sufficient to carry routine daily traffic.

Cluster dwellings dominate the residential fabric. Each cluster is a grouping of four to twenty dwellings, arranged around shared garden spaces, kitchen-hearths, and common-use buildings. The buildings themselves are elven-architectural throughout — grown and woven and dressed with restraint — but the register is domestic rather than ceremonial. Children move through the district at the pace and volume children always do. Neighbors call across gardens. Work in progress lies about where work is happening.

Sound in the Common Hearth is the fullest of any Thornveil district. Not loud by realm urban standards, but continuous and layered — conversation, craft sounds, children, the hearthgoats that many households keep, the songbirds that canopy the district supports. The ambient field is present but moderated by all this life; the Hearth is where Thornveil's register is most domestic and least ceremonial.

## Markets

The district holds Thornveil's primary markets — plural, because each is a cluster-scale or sub-district-scale affair, not a single civic market square. Exchange happens mostly by barter and relationship. Coin sees limited use internally (elves do not coin their own commerce; Drakes appear in Thornveil mostly in the context of realm-import goods passed from Vaelmere). The markets serve the Hearth itself primarily, with goods moving between clusters, between the Hearth and the Crafthall, and between the Hearth and Vaelmere's trade flow.

Typical market goods include:

- **Food.** Hearthnut products, Summerpear and Goldcap fruit and preserves, Dewberry preserves, River Reed Onion, Heartmorel in season, honey and wax, the small-scale venison and boar that local hunters bring in, Silverstream trout from the Heartwater Basin, hearthgoat dairy.
- **Drink.** Forest wines, Goldcap cider, honey mead, herbal infusions. All produced in the Glade; some traded outward from here to Vaelmere.
- **Daily goods.** Thornfiber basketry, Silverleaf Balm preparations, Dawnmint, candles of honey wax, domestic textiles.
- **Exchange items.** Goods traded back from Vaelmere's realm-import flow — metal tools, specific craft materials, realm books and scrolls for those who want them.

## Community Life

Cluster life is the Common Hearth's backbone. Most residents identify first with their cluster, then with the district, then with the city. Clusters are semi-permanent; dwelling transfers between clusters happen (children marrying into a neighbor's cluster, families growing or contracting, occasional cluster dissolution and reformation) but the pattern is stable over long elven periods.

Shared spaces bind each cluster. Every cluster has a shared hearth of some kind — a central kitchen-commons where residents cook collectively or individually in a shared space, depending on the cluster's habits. Every cluster has a shared garden. Every cluster has a gathering-space, indoor or outdoor, for observances, conversation, craft work, and the accumulated small ceremonies of daily life.

Observances in the Common Hearth follow the Glade's lunar-and-event rhythm. Full-moon gatherings, every-third-moon rites, every-seventh, every-thirteenth. Births, deaths, bondings, oaths, departures, returns. These are cluster-scale and sub-district-scale, not district-wide; the Hearth's rhythm is plural and layered rather than singular.

## Work

Junior craftwork and ordinary service-work happens in the Hearth rather than in the Crafthall proper. Apprentices in the first years of a craft work from their cluster space or from small Hearth-district workshops before moving to Crafthall training if their path requires it. Routine maintenance of dwellings, gardens, and shared spaces is household and cluster work. Cooking, tending, teaching, child-care, elder-care — the continuous labor of collective domestic life — happens in the distributed way elven households handle such things.

The Hearth also holds several small civic functions: a **dispute-hearing commons** where small inter-cluster conflicts are worked out before they reach any formal Council-adjacent process; a **teaching commons** where younger elves receive the shared education that precedes specialized training; a **records commons** where house-independent civic records (population, health, work-rotations) are kept by informally designated elders.

## Visitors

Realm visitors approved for access beyond the Outer Bounds are most likely to find themselves in the Common Hearth. Visiting scholars spend time at the teaching commons; visiting traders attend specific markets; visiting diplomats at the cluster level occasionally stay in the Hearth's guest spaces. Integration of long-term non-elven residents begins here; most non-elves in Thornveil live in specific clusters in the Hearth (or in the Outer Bounds).

Visitors are expected to operate at the Hearth's register — domestic, warm, attentive, unhurried. The register is more approachable than the Approach's or the Groves', but it is not a tourist district; visitors without purpose do not acquire one by wandering.

## Authoring Notes

The Common Hearth is canonically Thornveil's largest district and holds most of the city's residential population. GPT scenes set "in Thornveil" without further specification often belong here.

Cluster structure is canonical. Any scene involving an elven household in the Hearth should be set within a specific cluster, even if the cluster is invented at scene time. Clusters are named (often informally) and persistent.

Markets are plural and distributed, not a single market square. GPT should avoid realm-city market tropes (central plaza, vendor stalls in rows); the Hearth's market layer is cluster-threaded and organic.

The `known_npcs` list is empty because the Hearth's population is distributed. Named Hearth NPCs should be invented at scene time within the canonical character register (domestic, warm, attentive, unhurried).

Threat level 0; the Hearth is among the safest places in the city. Social risk from serious disrespect exists but physical risk does not.
