# The Council of Elders

The governing body of the Feywood. The Council holds the political authority of the elven realm — the entity that authorizes major deployments, sets policy on matters of state, conducts diplomatic relations with the Drakenvale Council under the Oath of the Fallen, and resolves the questions that exceed any single institution's authority. The Council does not rule in the realm sense. It deliberates, decides, and authorizes; the working life of the Feywood is conducted by the orders, the Halls, the druidic community, and the houses themselves, each with substantial day-to-day autonomy.

The Council's character matches the Feywood's broader pace. Deliberation is slow. Months are common for substantive matters; years for the most consequential. This is not bureaucratic drag — it is the doctrinal stance that decisions affecting a millennia-old elven realm require time appropriate to the depth of what is being decided.

## Doctrine and Purpose

The Council exists to make decisions for the Feywood as a whole. The Feywood's institutional structure — orders, Halls, houses, druidic community — handles most of what needs handling without Council involvement. The Council deals with the residue: matters that exceed any single institution's purview, decisions that bind the Feywood together rather than its parts separately, periodic rebalancing of relationships among institutions, and the rare moments when the Feywood as a sovereign realm must act or speak.

The doctrinal frame is **considered governance**. Decisions are reached through deliberation that takes time appropriate to their weight. Consensus is sought where it can be earned; majority is acceptable where consensus cannot be reached but the matter must be resolved; matters that cannot be resolved either way are deferred until they can be. The Council does not feel pressure to decide quickly because quick decisions are what realm-side governments produce; it feels pressure to decide rightly.

Specific matters that fall to the Council:

**Major institutional decisions.** Selection of the Heartwarden Captain, the Greenshield Commander, the Trade Master, the Master of Guides, and other senior figures across the Feywood's institutions. The Council does not ordinarily intervene in junior appointments or operational matters.

**Treaty and external relations.** All formal relations with the Drakenvale Council under the Oath of the Fallen. Authorization of significant cross-realm engagement. Decisions on whether and how the Feywood responds when the Oath is invoked or might be.

**Major deployments.** Authorization of military deployments beyond the Feywood's borders — vanishingly rare, with the Discordant War as the canonical instance. Authorization of Heartwarden order arms-deployment beyond the Heartwood and Silent Grove.

**Matters of land-weight.** Decisions whose implications touch the Feywood as a living system rather than its institutional affairs. Convened in the Council Chamber beneath the Heartwood (see Working Sites) and proceed in the specific register that location requires.

**Inter-institutional questions.** Disputes between institutions, decisions about how institutions' work should coordinate, and the periodic clarification of authority boundaries when they become unclear.

**Recognition of new institutions or new senior roles.** When the Feywood's life produces work requiring institutional formalization, the Council recognizes the arrangement.

The Council does not handle: ordinary administration of any institution; disputes within institutions (those resolve internally); matters of common life — agriculture, craft, daily settlement order — which the elven community handles through ordinary civic process; most ceremonial and religious matters, which fall to the Elder Roots and the relevant houses; most diplomatic interactions below treaty level, which the Halls handle.

## Composition

Approximately twenty members at any given time, plus the Elder Druid of the Heartwood as recognized counselor on matters of land (not technically a Council member, but present and heard at all relevant deliberations).

Composition breaks roughly into three categories:

**House representatives** — one elder per major house, plus rotating positions for minor houses. Approximately ten to twelve members. The seven major houses (Heartwood, Vaelaryn, Silverbough, Thornmere, Dawnveil, Greybranch, Ironsap) each typically maintain one Council seat at any given time; specific houses occasionally hold a second seat for periods when their work warrants. Remaining house seats rotate among smaller and less prominent houses on schedules each house determines internally.

**Common-origin elders** — elves not affiliated with the noble houses, recognized for sustained civic contribution in specific functions. Approximately six to eight members. Drawn from across the Feywood's life: druidic practitioners of recognized standing, senior craftfolk whose work has shaped specific traditions, elders from smaller settlements whose long stewardship has produced civic weight, occasional figures from Vaelmere's integrated community whose contributions warrant recognition. Common-origin standing is a recognized category, not a courtesy seat.

**Specialized roles** — a small number of seats for specific institutional positions when their occupants meet Council-elder threshold. Currently typically one or two senior figures whose role-derived weight warrants Council membership independently of house or common-origin path. Such seats are rare and depend on the specific configuration of senior figures at any given time.

Total Council size varies between roughly seventeen and twenty-three depending on rotations and recognitions in progress; twenty is the canonical typical size.

The Elder Druid of the Heartwood is not a Council member but is recognized as the Feywood's senior land-counselor. The Elder Druid attends Council deliberations on matters touching the land, speaks when their counsel is sought or when their judgment requires it, and holds particular authority in Council Chamber convenings of land-weight. This is a structural arrangement of long standing, not a formal seat.

## Selection and Membership

### House Representatives

Each house determines its own Council representative through processes specific to its culture. House Heartwood selects through senior consultation among the family elders. House Thornmere holds extended family discussions that converge on consensus through reputation-weighted deliberation. House Vaelaryn's process involves consultation with the family's senior trade and diplomatic figures and produces representatives with significant external-relations experience. Other houses' processes vary similarly.

What is consistent across houses: representatives are senior, deeply experienced, and chosen for capability rather than birth-order or formal claim. A house's Council seat is held by the elder the house judges most fit for the work, without strict succession rules.

### Common-Origin Members

Common-origin Council members rise through demonstrated civic contribution. The path is slower than house representation — common-origin candidates must accumulate the standing that warrants recognition, which typically takes a century or more of substantial work in a specific function. Recognition occurs through:

**Council recognition.** Existing Council members identify candidates they consider Council-worthy and propose recognition during ordinary deliberation. The proposing member typically has worked alongside the candidate for decades.

**Community concurrence.** The candidate's home community (settlement, neighborhood, working community) is informally consulted; significant community objection would block recognition, though such objections are rare given the slow accumulation of standing that precedes recognition.

**Self-presentation.** The candidate is consulted about whether they will accept recognition. Refusal is real and not unusual — Council membership is substantial work, and elves with significant ongoing commitments sometimes decline.

The process produces recognition rather than election. Common-origin members are not voted in; they are recognized as having the standing the position requires.

### Tenure

Council membership is open-ended. Members serve until they choose to step down, until their house chooses a different representative, or (very rarely) until the Council itself decides their continued presence is unworkable. Most Council members serve for centuries; some serve for the better part of a millennium. Stepping down is honorable.

### The Speaker

A specific role within the Council: a member chosen by their fellow members to handle the practical work of deliberation. The Speaker presides over meetings, manages the order of speaking, ensures that matters move through the Council's deliberative process appropriately, and coordinates with external institutions on Council communications. The Speaker is a working role, not a status symbol; the Council's authority remains collective.

The Speaker is typically a member of considerable Council seniority chosen through informal Council recognition. Tenure is open-ended. House Greybranch members hold the Speaker role disproportionately given the family's procedural and memory-keeping specialty, but the role is not Greybranch-bound and other houses' members have served capably.

## Procedural Patterns

### Deliberation Pace

Substantive matters typically take months from first introduction to resolution; major matters take years; the most consequential can take longer. This is doctrinal: decisions affecting an elven realm should be made at elven timescales.

Within that overall pace, specific deliberations move at the rhythms appropriate to them. Routine matters resolve more quickly. Matters with clear consensus from the start resolve quickly even when substantive. Matters with deep disagreement resolve slowly even when operationally simple.

Deliberation typically proceeds across multiple sessions over the relevant duration. A given session may address multiple matters at different stages of consideration. Matters develop until they are ready, and then they resolve.

### Speaking Patterns

Council deliberation operates by accumulated speaking rather than by fixed agenda. Members speak when their judgment bears on the matter, in roughly the order their thinking has reached completion. The Speaker manages the flow without imposing artificial structure on it. Senior members typically speak after extensive listening; younger members speak when their specific knowledge bears; common-origin members and house representatives speak with equal weight regardless of category.

Silence is canonical and meaningful. Members may attend a deliberation for considerable duration without speaking if they have no specific contribution; their attentive presence is itself a contribution. Silence does not indicate disengagement.

Cross-talk is rare. Disagreements emerge through competing presentations of judgment rather than through direct argumentation. The Council's culture resists realm-side debate-as-combat patterns and produces a deliberative texture that realm visitors sometimes find frustratingly placid.

### Decision-Making

Decisions are reached through deliberation rather than through formal voting. The Council's preference is consensus — a position that all members can accept, even if not all members would have chosen it as their first preference. Consensus is sought through deliberative refinement: members who initially disagreed are heard; their concerns are addressed; the proposed position is refined until it reflects something all can accept.

When consensus cannot be reached, the Council can resolve through majority. Formal votes are rare but real. The Speaker calls the vote when deliberation has clearly converged on majority direction without producing consensus and when the matter requires resolution. Members vote according to their judgment; outcomes are recorded; the resulting decision is binding.

Matters that cannot be resolved either way are sometimes deferred until they can be. Deferral is honorable. A matter deferred for years is the Council recognizing that the time has not come.

### Records

Decisions and major deliberations are recorded — by Council Speaker oversight, in oral tradition primarily, with written records for treaties, formal proceedings, external communications, and matters whose precedential weight warrants written form. The Speaker's office maintains the records; House Greybranch's procedural-memory tradition supports the work.

## Working Sites

### The Council Chambers in the Heartwood Approach

The Council's primary working location. Located in Thornveil's Heartwood Approach district, in a grove where several ancient trees form a natural meeting space enhanced over generations of careful elven craft. The chambers accommodate the full Council when plenary meetings convene, smaller breakout spaces for sub-committee work, and the small archive of written records the Council maintains.

Routine deliberation — most of the Council's substantive work — occurs in these chambers. The chambers' character is the Heartwood Approach's broader register: ancient, attentive, weighted, quiet.

Access is restricted. Council members enter through specific paths; sanctioned visitors (such as an institutional figure summoned for consultation) through others; casual traffic does not enter at all.

### The Council Chamber Beneath the Heartwood

The Council's working seat for matters of land-weight. Located beneath the Heartwood's base, accessed through the natural opening between the buttress roots, the Chamber is the space where the Council convenes when matters touching the Feywood as a living system require the Heartwood's direct presence in the deliberation.

Land-weight convenings are rare — perhaps a handful in a century, sometimes fewer. The decision to descend to the Chamber rather than meet in the Heartwood Approach is itself part of the deliberation's meaning. A matter convened beneath the Heartwood is acknowledged by the Council as touching the Feywood at depth.

The Heartwarden order provides ceremonial security for Council Chamber convenings. The Elder Druid is present without exception; their counsel carries particular weight in this register. The Pool ritual may coincide with Council deliberation if the Heartwood drops a branch — Lethariel's vision during the Discordant War's deployment deliberation is the canonical instance (see `heartwardens.md`).

## Relationship to Other Groups

### The Heartwardens

The Heartwarden Captain answers to the Council on matters of order policy, deployment authorization, and significant ceremonial work. The Council selected the current Captain (and historical predecessors). The Heartwardens provide ceremonial security at Council Chamber convenings of land-weight.

### The Greenshields and Ranger Branch

The Greenshield Commander answers to the Council on the same terms. Major Greenshield deployments require Council authorization; routine operations are the Commander's authority. Coordination at the leadership level is continuous between the Council Speaker, the Commander, and the Heartwarden Captain. The Captain of the Ranger Branch reports through the Greenshield Commander.

### The Hall of Trade

The Trade Master reports to the Council on trade policy, significant exchange events, realm-condition awareness, and Hall institutional matters. House Vaelaryn members serving on the Council carry trade-related awareness into Council discussions naturally.

### The Hall of Guides

The Master of Guides reports to the Council on Hall policy, significant escort matters (rare; most escort is routine), and institutional matters.

### The Elder Roots

The Elder Druid of the Heartwood holds a structural counselor role to the Council on land matters. The Druid Master is consulted on Hall-internal matters when relevant. Druidic interpretation of land signals informs Council deliberation when the signals warrant.

### The Noble Houses

House representatives hold their Council seats; the Council and the houses are interwoven at the membership level. House heads consult their Council representatives on matters affecting their houses; Council representatives carry house perspectives into deliberation while exercising independent judgment.

The Council does not direct house affairs. Houses are sovereign in their own internal life; the Council's authority extends to matters where the Feywood as a whole has stake.

### The Drakenvale Council

The Feywood Council and the Drakenvale Council coordinate at Council-to-Council level under the Oath of the Fallen. Such coordination is rare in normal times — most realm-Feywood matters are handled at the Hall and order level — but is canonically the channel for treaty matters, significant diplomatic developments, and the rare events that invoke or might invoke the Oath's mutual-aid clause.

Relations are correct, formally respectful, and substantively distant. The two Councils are sovereign equals; neither directs the other; each governs its own realm under shared compact. Direct meetings between the two Councils are rare events, typically conducted at neutral or jointly-sanctioned sites.
