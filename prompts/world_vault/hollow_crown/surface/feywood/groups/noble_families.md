# Noble Families of the Feywood

The Feywood's civic life is shaped by its noble families — elven houses whose multi-generational presence has carried specific traditions, specializations, and stewardships across millennia. The houses are not aristocracy in the realm sense. There are no titles of nobility, no inherited authority, no ranks of precedence. A house is a family line that has, across centuries, accumulated enough reputation, work, and recognized contribution that the broader Feywood treats them as a distinct civic presence. The houses' standing is earned and renewed across generations; a house that ceases to contribute would, over time, simply cease to be one.

Seven houses are the most prominent in the present age: **Heartwood**, **Vaelaryn**, **Silverbough**, **Thornmere**, **Dawnveil**, **Greybranch**, and **Ironsap**. Each is associated with a characteristic civic specialty. The Council of Elders includes representatives from these seven and from additional houses; the seven are the most notable, not the only ones.

## The Common-but-Not-Exclusive Pattern

Each house's civic association is **common but not exclusive**, and the mechanism is real.

Houses become known for their specialties because they produce, generation after generation, candidates who are genuinely better suited to the associated work. The mechanism is straightforward: accumulated knowledge that gets passed down, ceremonial and practical exposure from childhood, family-culture transmission of the bearing and habits the work requires, and the dense informal mentorship that comes from growing up around elves who have done the work for centuries. A House Heartwood child grows up in proximity to the Heartwood, with Heartwarden practice woven into the texture of family life from earliest memory. A House Thornmere child grows up around defensive-vinework cultivation and the founding-mythic stories that make the Greenshield's work feel like family work. A House Vaelaryn child learns to read realm signals at the dinner table.

The result is real overrepresentation. House members appear in associated structures at substantially higher rates than baseline at all levels of the structure, and disproportionately at senior levels where the depth of experience and accumulated bearing matters most. House Heartwood members serve as Heartwardens at substantially higher rates than other houses; House Heartwood holds the Heartwarden Captaincy more often than any other house across the order's history. The same pattern holds for House Thornmere and the Greenshield Commandership, House Vaelaryn and the Trade Master role, House Silverbough and the Master of Guides.

The pattern is common but not exclusive because the structures themselves are not house-bound. There are no inherited posts. There are no formal preferences. There is no restricted eligibility. Selection at every level — Council selection of senior figures, instructor recommendation for novices, peer recognition for advancement — is conducted on capability, not affiliation. House members serve at higher rates because the candidate pool that develops includes more of them at the relevant capability level. Individual exceptions are real and ordinary: a House Ironsap member who becomes Greenshield Commander, a House Dawnveil member who becomes Heartwarden Captain, a common-origin Trade Master. The structures' culture is explicitly anti-nepotistic; family service in a line does not advance a candidate's standing.

GPT scenes involving questions of house association should preserve both halves of this. Houses are not guilds with monopolies. They are also not interchangeable cultural badges. They are families whose generational traditions actually produce the candidates the associated work requires.

## The Seven Major Houses

### House Heartwood

**Specialty:** Sacred proximity, Heartwood stewardship, oldest legitimacy.

**Common civic association:** Heartwarden Captaincy and senior Heartwarden service.

House Heartwood takes its name from the Heartwood — not the other way around. When the Heartwood was first discovered by an elf adjacent to the Silent Grove community in the deep past, the broader elven community offered the finder's family the name *Heartwood* as a mark of respect, not ownership. The house's standing across the millennia since rests on its accepted role as the family most closely associated with the Heartwood's ongoing stewardship: the most likely to produce Heartwardens, the most likely to hold Captaincy when it is open, the most active in the ceremonial work that surrounds the tree. The mechanism is generational — children grow up adjacent to Heartwarden practice and develop the sensitivity to the tree, the habits of attention, and the cultural fluency that produce capable candidates.

The house maintains two seats — the **Outer Wild Estate** in the Outer Old-Growth Wild (the wild-heritage ancestral seat where the family's deepest roots sit and where the proving-wild tradition operates) and the **Thornveil seat in the Heartwood Approach** (the political-ceremonial center near the Heartwood itself).

**Lethariel Heartwood** is the current head of the house and Heartwarden Captain emeritus; **Sylvara Heartwood** is the current Proving candidate.

### House Vaelaryn

**Specialty:** Trade-facing diplomacy, external relationships, spycraft.

**Common civic association:** Trade Master role and senior Trade Envoy positions; senior posts at Vaelmere; the Feywood's external information-gathering culture.

House Vaelaryn is the Feywood's most realm-facing house. Members are the elves most likely to spend their lives engaged with realm trade, realm diplomacy, and the careful work of knowing what is happening on the other side of the Border. Closely tied to **Vaelmere** as both institutional and emotional center; Vaelaryn estates and family establishments are concentrated there.

The spycraft dimension is real but understated. The Feywood does not maintain a formal intelligence service; what intelligence it has comes through the steady accumulated awareness of its realm-facing residents, with House Vaelaryn at the center of this work. The work is not adversarial in framing — the Feywood does not spy on its sovereign-equal ally — but it is attentive in ways the realm rarely is in return. Vaelaryn children learn to read realm signals from family life; the cultural transmission produces members who are realm-aware to a degree pure trade work would not require.

**Caelthir Vaelaryn**, currently in residence at Vaelaryn's estate, is the father of Sylvara and Aelarion (linking House Heartwood and House Vaelaryn through marriage).

### House Silverbough

**Specialty:** Wild-harvest, harts, old-growth estate stewardship.

**Common civic association:** Master of Guides role and senior Hall of Guides service; stewardship of specific Outer Wild zones; Silverbough Hart and Silverbough White Hart lineage.

House Silverbough's identity is anchored in the Outer Wild and its specific cervid populations. The Silverbough Hart and the much rarer Silverbough White Hart are tied by name and ancient bond to the house, and the house's traditional work concerns the careful stewardship of these populations alongside the broader wild-harvest practice that defines life in the Outer Old-Growth Wild. The house maintains estates and working sites distributed across several Outer Wild zones rather than concentrated in any single seat.

Silverbough members commonly serve in Greenshield patrol units assigned to outer-range work, in Hall of Guides roles where the family's accumulated geometry-reading capability aligns with the work, and in the specific druidic practice that intersects with wildlife management. The house's character is quieter than most — Silverbough members are often the elves least visible at Thornveil's civic events and most present in the woods themselves.

### House Thornmere

**Specialty:** Defensive vinework, Greenshield tradition, Thornveil founding myth.

**Common civic association:** Greenshield Commandership and senior Greenshield service.

House Thornmere is the parallel to House Heartwood — common to the Greenshields as Heartwood is common to the Heartwardens. The house's defensive-vinework tradition (cultivated thorned vines that grow as natural defensive barriers around specific sites in the Feywood, requiring centuries of careful tending to mature into effective form) is distinctively Thornmere; the house's role in the founding myth of Thornveil itself (the elven city's name and protective character both reflect Thornmere influence in the deep past) anchors its standing.

Thornmere members are well-represented across Greenshield service at every level, and the Commandership has come from the house more often than from any other across the order's history. The house's character is plainspoken and practical — Thornmere members tend toward the kind of competent, grounded presence the Greenshields' culture rewards.

### House Dawnveil

**Specialty:** Ceremonial flowers, festival custodians, observance calendar.

**Common civic association:** Stewardship of the Feywood's ceremonial calendar; cultivation and care of flowers used in ritual practice; coordination of major seasonal observances.

House Dawnveil's work is the Feywood's ritual texture. The house cultivates and tends the specific ceremonial flowers used in elven observances (Oathbloom, Verdant Lilies, the Dawn-Blooms used in solstice rites, and several others); maintains the calendar of major observances across centuries; and coordinates the practical organization of festival-scale events. Dawnveil estates include flower-gardens of significant scale, maintained as living infrastructure rather than ornament.

Dawnveil members are common in druidic practice (particularly the calendar-attentive branches), in ceremonial roles at major festivals, and in the working coordination that holds large gatherings together. The house's character is warm and outward-facing in ways that differentiate it from Silverbough's quieter wild-stewardship register or Greybranch's reserved formal-procedure register.

### House Greybranch

**Specialty:** Oral law, memory-keeping, Council procedure.

**Common civic association:** Council of Elders procedural roles, including the Speaker disproportionately; preservation of legal-precedent oral tradition; the Reading Glade's documentary work where it intersects with civic memory.

House Greybranch is the Feywood's institutional memory. Elven civic life relies heavily on oral transmission of law, precedent, and procedural form, and Greybranch members are the elves whose centuries of focused practice make this transmission reliable. A Greybranch elder consulted on Council procedure can often state, from memory, the relevant precedent and its application in cases reaching back over a thousand years.

The house maintains close working relationships with the Reading Glade tradition (where written records exist and are coordinated with the oral) and with the Council directly. Greybranch members are common in formal Council procedural roles — clerks, advisors, witnesses to formal proceedings, and the Speaker disproportionately — though these positions are not house-restricted. The house's character is reserved and precise.

### House Ironsap

**Specialty:** Sanctioned timber, tools, bow-staves, restraint in material culture.

**Common civic association:** Sourcing and crafting of specific elven tools, weapons, and materials; oversight of which tree-specimens may be cut and which must be preserved.

House Ironsap is the Feywood's principal craft-material house. The Feywood's relationship with its trees is too intimate for ordinary timber-harvesting practice; specific specimens may be cut, others must not, and the distinction requires the kind of accumulated specialist knowledge a single life cannot accumulate. House Ironsap holds this knowledge across its generations, identifies sanctioned specimens, oversees the careful work of cutting and curing, and produces or supplies the materials from which Longboughs, ceremonial implements, the order weapons, and the elves' more specialized tools are made.

The house's "restraint in material culture" association reflects its broader doctrinal stance: that the Feywood's craft tradition should produce less than it could, more carefully than it would otherwise, with each piece justifying the relationship that produced it. Ironsap-supplied bow-staves are particularly notable; most Longboughs in current Greenshield and Heartwarden service trace their material sourcing through Ironsap channels.

## Other Houses

The seven houses listed above are the most prominent and the most likely to appear in narrative. They are not the only houses. Several minor or specialized houses exist — older lines that have receded, newer lines that have not yet reached prominence, houses whose specialties are too narrow to register in broader civic life, and houses that simply prefer quiet existence. The Council of Elders includes representatives from a wider field than these seven.

GPT scenes may reference other houses as narrative requires. New named houses should fit the pattern (multi-generational elven families with specific civic associations) and should not contradict the seven above.

## The Council of Elders

The Council of Elders is the Feywood's governing body, composed of representatives drawn from the noble houses and from common origins as well. Council membership is not house-restricted; representatives are selected through processes specific to the constituency they represent, and common-origin elves of significant standing serve alongside house representatives.

The seven major houses each typically maintain a representative or two on the Council at any given time, contributing to (but not dominating) the broader composition. Smaller houses contribute fewer or rotate their representation. Common-origin Council members typically rise through demonstrated work in specific civic functions and often serve alongside their house-affiliated counterparts on issues their expertise touches.

See `council_of_elders.md` for full Council structure and procedure.
