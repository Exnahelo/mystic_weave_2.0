# Noble Families of the Feywood

The Feywood's civic life is shaped by its noble families — elven houses whose multi-generational presence has carried specific traditions, specializations, and stewardships across millennia. The houses are not aristocracy in the realm sense. There are no titles of nobility, no inherited authority, no ranks of precedence. A house is a family line that has, across centuries, accumulated enough reputation, work, and recognized contribution that the broader Feywood treats them as a distinct civic presence. The houses' standing is earned and renewed across generations; a house that ceases to contribute would, over time, simply cease to be one.

Seven houses are the most prominent in the present age: **Heartwood**, **Vaelaryn**, **Silverbough**, **Thornmere**, **Dawnveil**, **Greybranch**, and **Ironsap**. Each is associated with a characteristic civic specialty. The Council of Elders includes representatives from these seven and from additional houses; the seven are the most notable, not the only ones.

## The Common-but-Not-Exclusive Pattern

Each house's civic association is **common but not exclusive**. Members of the associated house are well-represented in the corresponding civic structure, often disproportionately so at senior levels, but the structures themselves are open to all elves of the Feywood and (in principle) to integrated non-elven residents. House Heartwood members commonly hold the Heartwarden Captaincy; House Thornmere members commonly hold the Greenshield Commandership; but neither position is house-bound, and individual exceptions are real and not unusual.

The pattern is sustained through **cultural alignment**, not institutional preference. Houses raise their members in traditions that fit specific civic work. Members so raised tend to gravitate toward that work and to perform it well. The civic structures, recognizing the steady supply of capable candidates, draw from the houses that produce them. None of this involves formal preference, inherited posts, or restricted eligibility. A House Ironsap member could become Greenshield Commander; a House Dawnveil member could become Heartwarden Captain; the cultural patterns simply make these outcomes less common than the alternatives.

GPT scenes involving questions of house association should preserve this distinction. Houses are not guilds with monopolies. They are families with traditions.

## The Seven Major Houses

### House Heartwood

**Specialty:** Sacred proximity, Heartwood stewardship, oldest legitimacy.

**Common civic association:** Heartwarden Captaincy and senior Heartwarden service.

House Heartwood takes its name from the Heartwood — not the other way around. When the Heartwood was first discovered by an elf adjacent to the Silent Grove community in the deep past, the broader elven community offered the finder's family the name *Heartwood* as a mark of respect, not ownership. The house's standing across the millennia since rests on its accepted role as the family most closely associated with the Heartwood's ongoing stewardship: the most likely to produce Heartwardens, the most likely to hold Captaincy when it is open, the most active in the ceremonial work that surrounds the tree.

The house maintains two seats — the **Outer Wild Estate** in the Outer Old-Growth Wild (the wild-heritage ancestral seat where the family's deepest roots sit and where the proving-wild tradition operates) and the **Thornveil seat in the Heartwood Approach** (the political-ceremonial center near the Heartwood itself). The two seats are complementary; the family's life moves between them according to which work each member is doing.

**Lethariel Heartwood** is the current head of the house and Heartwarden Captain emeritus; **Sylvara Heartwood** is the current Proving candidate.

### House Vaelaryn

**Specialty:** Trade-facing diplomacy, external relationships, spycraft.

**Common civic association:** Senior posts at Vaelmere; Trade Envoy roles at Border exchanges; oversight of the Feywood's external information-gathering.

House Vaelaryn is the Feywood's most realm-facing house. Its members are the elves most likely to spend their lives engaged with realm trade, realm diplomacy, and the careful work of knowing what is happening on the other side of the Border. The house is closely tied to **Vaelmere** as both its institutional and emotional center; Vaelaryn estates and family establishments are concentrated there, and the house's character carries Vaelmere's specific cosmopolitan ease.

The spycraft dimension is real but understated. The Feywood does not maintain a formal intelligence service; what intelligence it has comes through the steady accumulated awareness of its realm-facing residents, with House Vaelaryn at the center of this work. Vaelaryn members often serve in the Greenshield's Vaelmere outer-rotation or in less formal observational capacities. The work is not adversarial in framing — the Feywood does not spy on its sovereign-equal ally — but it is attentive in ways the realm rarely is in return.

**Caelthir Vaelaryn**, currently in residence at Vaelaryn's estate, is the father of Sylvara and Aelarion (linking House Heartwood and House Vaelaryn through marriage).

### House Silverbough

**Specialty:** Wild-harvest, harts, old-growth estate stewardship.

**Common civic association:** Stewardship of specific Outer Wild zones; Feywood-internal hunting and game-management traditions; Silverbough Hart and Silverbough White Hart lineage.

House Silverbough's identity is anchored in the Outer Wild and its specific cervid populations. The Silverbough Hart and the much rarer Silverbough White Hart are tied by name and ancient bond to the house, and the house's traditional work concerns the careful stewardship of these populations alongside the broader wild-harvest practice that defines life in the Outer Old-Growth Wild. The house maintains estates and working sites distributed across several Outer Wild zones rather than concentrated in any single seat.

Silverbough members often serve in Greenshield patrol units assigned to outer-range work, in Ranger postings, and in the specific druidic practice that intersects with wildlife management. The house's character is quieter than most — Silverbough members are often the elves least visible at Thornveil's civic events and most present in the woods themselves.

### House Thornmere

**Specialty:** Defensive vinework, Greenshield tradition, Thornveil founding myth.

**Common civic association:** Greenshield Commandership and senior Greenshield service.

House Thornmere is the parallel to House Heartwood — common to the Greenshield as Heartwood is common to the Heartwarden. The house's defensive-vinework tradition (the cultivated thorned vines that grow as natural defensive barriers around specific sites in the Feywood, requiring centuries of careful tending to mature into effective form) is distinctively Thornmere; the house's role in the founding myth of Thornveil itself (the elven city's name and protective character both reflect Thornmere influence in the deep past) anchors its standing in Feywood civic life.

Thornmere members are well-represented across Greenshield service at every level, and the Commandership has come from the house more often than from any other across the order's history. The house's character is plainspoken and practical — Thornmere members tend toward the kind of competent, grounded presence the Greenshield's culture rewards.

### House Dawnveil

**Specialty:** Ceremonial flowers, festival custodians, observance calendar.

**Common civic association:** Stewardship of the Feywood's ceremonial calendar; cultivation and care of flowers used in ritual practice; coordination of major seasonal observances.

House Dawnveil's work is the Feywood's ritual texture. The house cultivates and tends the specific ceremonial flowers used in elven observances (Oathbloom, Verdant Lilies, the Dawn-Blooms used in solstice rites, and several others); maintains the calendar of major observances and ensures its accuracy across centuries; and coordinates the practical organization of festival-scale events. Dawnveil estates include flower-gardens of significant scale, maintained as living infrastructure rather than ornament.

Dawnveil members are common in druidic practice (particularly the calendar-attentive branches), in ceremonial roles at major festivals, and in the working coordination that holds large gatherings together. The house's character is warm and outward-facing in ways that differentiate it from Silverbough's quieter wild-stewardship register or Greybranch's reserved formal-procedure register.

### House Greybranch

**Specialty:** Oral law, memory-keeping, Council procedure.

**Common civic association:** Council of Elders procedural roles; preservation of legal-precedent oral tradition; the Reading Glade's documentary work where it intersects with civic memory.

House Greybranch is the Feywood's institutional memory. Elven civic life relies heavily on oral transmission of law, precedent, and procedural form, and Greybranch members are the elves whose centuries of focused practice make this transmission reliable. A Greybranch elder consulted on Council procedure can often state, from memory, the relevant precedent and its application in cases reaching back over a thousand years.

The house maintains close working relationships with the Reading Glade tradition (where written records exist and are coordinated with the oral) and with the Council of Elders directly. Greybranch members are common in formal Council procedural roles — clerks, advisors, witnesses to formal proceedings — though these positions are not house-restricted. The house's character is reserved and precise; Greybranch members are the elves most likely to interrupt a casual conversation to correct a misstatement of historical fact.

### House Ironsap

**Specialty:** Sanctioned timber, tools, bow-staves, restraint in material culture.

**Common civic association:** Sourcing and crafting of specific elven tools, weapons, and materials; oversight of which tree-specimens may be cut and which must be preserved.

House Ironsap is the Feywood's principal craft-material house. The Feywood's relationship with its trees is too intimate for ordinary timber-harvesting practice; specific specimens may be cut, others must not, and the distinction requires the kind of accumulated specialist knowledge a single life cannot accumulate. House Ironsap holds this knowledge across its generations, identifies sanctioned specimens, oversees the careful work of cutting and curing, and produces or supplies the materials from which Longboughs, ceremonial implements, the order weapons, and the elves' more specialized tools are made.

The house's "restraint in material culture" association reflects its broader doctrinal stance: that the Feywood's craft tradition should produce less than it could, more carefully than it would otherwise, with each piece justifying the relationship that produced it. Ironsap-supplied bow-staves are particularly notable; most Longboughs in current Greenshield and Heartwarden service trace their material sourcing through Ironsap channels.

## Other Houses

The seven houses listed above are the most prominent and the most likely to appear in narrative. They are not the only houses. Several minor or specialized houses exist — older lines that have receded, newer lines that have not yet reached prominence, houses whose specialties are too narrow to register in broader civic life, and houses that simply prefer quiet existence. The Council of Elders includes representatives from a wider field than these seven, and the Feywood's full noble structure is broader than any single file's enumeration.

GPT scenes may reference other houses as narrative requires. New named houses should fit the pattern (multi-generational elven families with specific civic associations) and should not contradict the seven above. The Feywood does not have a fixed roster of houses; the houses that matter at any given time are the houses whose work is currently visible in civic life.

## The Council of Elders

The Council of Elders is the Feywood's governing body, composed of representatives drawn from the noble houses and from common origins as well. Council membership is not house-restricted; representatives are selected through processes specific to the constituency they represent, and common-origin elves of significant standing serve alongside house representatives. Council deliberations, particularly those of land-weight that descend to the Council Chamber beneath the Heartwood, are conducted in the careful, slow, attentive manner the Feywood considers appropriate to decisions that will affect the land.

The seven major houses each typically maintain a representative or two on the Council at any given time, contributing to (but not dominating) the broader composition. Smaller houses contribute fewer or rotate their representation. Common-origin Council members typically rise through demonstrated work in specific civic functions — druidic practice, Reading Glade scholarship, exceptional Greenshield or Heartwarden service, distinctive cultural contribution — and often serve alongside their house-affiliated counterparts on issues their expertise touches.

## Authoring Notes

This file is the canonical source for the seven major Feywood noble houses. Order files (Heartwarden, Greenshield, future Ranger) and location files (Thornveil, Heartwood Approach, Vaelmere, Heartwood Estate, future house seats) reference this file rather than duplicating its content. When a scene needs the structural canon of "common but not exclusive" house-civic association, this file is the source.

**The seven houses' specialties are canonical:**
- Heartwood: sacred proximity, Heartwood stewardship, oldest legitimacy → Heartwarden Captaincy
- Vaelaryn: trade-facing diplomacy, external relationships, spycraft → Vaelmere senior posts
- Silverbough: wild-harvest, harts, old-growth estate stewardship → Outer Wild stewardship
- Thornmere: defensive vinework, Greenshield tradition, Thornveil founding myth → Greenshield Commandership
- Dawnveil: ceremonial flowers, festival custodians, observance calendar → festival coordination
- Greybranch: oral law, memory-keeping, Council procedure → Council procedural roles
- Ironsap: sanctioned timber, tools, bow-staves, restraint in material culture → craft-material oversight

**The "common but not exclusive" pattern is canonical** across all civic structures. Houses produce candidates well-suited to their associated work; civic structures draw from those candidates more often than from less-suited alternatives; but no civic structure is house-restricted, and individual exceptions are real and ordinary.

**The Council of Elders includes members from these seven houses, additional houses, and common origins.** The seven major houses are notable, not exhaustive, and not exclusive to Council composition.

**Per-house authoring at greater depth is a future deliverable.** Each house could in principle support its own location nodes (estates, working sites), its own NPC anchors (heads, notable members), and its own dedicated detail file. This file is the structural canon; deeper per-house authoring will follow as narrative needs require.

**The houses are not aristocracy in the realm sense.** No titles, no inherited authority, no ranks of precedence. A house is a family line of accumulated standing across generations. GPT scenes should resist any framing that depicts the houses as analogous to realm noble families with formal political power.

**Marriage and cross-house family relationships are canonical.** The current PC's family — Sylvara Heartwood, daughter of a Heartwood mother and Caelthir Vaelaryn (House Vaelaryn) — is a canonical example. Cross-house relationships are not unusual; they bring families into broader webs of mutual relationship that further sustain the Feywood's civic interconnection.

**House Heartwood and House Thornmere have explicit canonical parallels** with the Heartwarden and Greenshield orders respectively. Other house-order or house-civic-function pairings exist but are less institutionally formal — Vaelaryn with Vaelmere, Silverbough with Outer Wild stewardship, Dawnveil with festival coordination, Greybranch with Council procedure, Ironsap with sanctioned craft. The Heartwood-Heartwarden and Thornmere-Greenshield pairings are the most formalized; the others are more loosely-held cultural associations.
