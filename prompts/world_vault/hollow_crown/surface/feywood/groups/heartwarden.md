# The Heartwarden

The elite sacred-guardian order of the Heartwood and the Silent Grove. Sworn at the Heartwood's base in the elven manner — once, for a century, again — the Heartwarden are the Feywood's standing answer to the question of who attends the land's living center when the land's living center requires attending. They are not a large order; they are not meant to be. They are sized to the work: continuous watch at the Heartwood, sustained presence at the Silent Grove, training and renewal cycles that keep the order whole across centuries, and reserve depth sufficient to deploy in genuine crisis without abandoning the home posts. The order's name reflects the tree, not a family. The order's authority comes from the Heartwood, not from any house. The order's oath is the deepest standing commitment in elven civic life, and those who take it are understood by their community to have given themselves to a service that does not, except by their own request, end.

## Doctrine and Purpose

The Heartwarden exists to guard what the Feywood cannot lose. The Heartwood and the Silent Grove are not protected because they are valuable. They are protected because the Feywood is the Heartwood, and the Silent Grove is where the elves first settled when they gave their immortality to repair the land — and these are not assets that admit of replacement. To lose either is to lose what makes the elves of the Feywood themselves. The Heartwarden is the order that takes that fact seriously enough to organize its entire life around it.

The order's doctrine is simple in expression and exacting in practice. The Heartwarden does not hold offensive doctrine. It does not project force outward. It does not pursue, expand, or campaign. It guards. Specifically, it guards the Heartwood's inner sanctums (the surface clearing, the Council Chamber beneath the tree, the Pool, the cardinal stones), the Silent Grove (its ancient trees, its training and ceremonial sites, its druidic community), and the rituals bound to both. When the Heartwarden does deploy in arms — as during the Discordant War — it is because the threat has come close enough to the order's charges that defense at the source is the necessary expression of guardianship.

The doctrinal framing within the order is that everything the Heartwarden does is service to a relationship that predates them and will outlast them. They are not the Heartwood's army. They are the latest hundred elves in a continuous chain who have chosen this work, and they will hand it to the next hundred when their century closes. Continuity is the doctrine. Presence is the discipline. The relationship is the point.

## Structure

The order is small enough to be known to itself. Most active Heartwardens recognize most other active Heartwardens by face, name, and lineage of service. Hierarchy is real but light; the work is the same work whether one is the Captain or a first-year novice on Watch, and the order's culture preserves that.

### The Captain

A single Captain leads the order. The Captaincy rotates among the senior sworn at intervals determined by the Council of Elders in consultation with the Elder Druid of the Heartwood, typically every two to three centuries. Captains step down at their own request, by the Council's request, or upon entering emeritus standing. The Captain coordinates with the Council, the Elder Druid, and the Greenshield's senior leadership; commands the order's deployment in crisis; oversees the rotation schedule; presides at oath-takings; and carries the weight of decisions that exceed any single Watch's authority. **Lethariel Heartwood currently holds Captaincy in emeritus standing**, supporting the active Captain through the preparation phase and resuming the active Captaincy when her family's current Proving candidate departs.

### Sworn Heartwardens

The body of the order. One hundred elves at any given time, distributed across active service (approximately ninety) and the renewal cycle at the Silent Grove (approximately ten). Sworn Heartwardens hold equal standing among themselves; differentiation is by experience, specialization, and the particular work each is suited to. Senior sworn — those several centuries into renewed service — typically hold instructor postings at the Silent Grove during their renewal years, and may rotate through specific high-skill posts (Watchpost duty, communication-ritual witnessing, escort of rare ceremonies) during active service.

### Novices

At any given time, the order has approximately fifteen to twenty novices in training at the Silent Grove. Novices are not yet sworn; their decade of initial training precedes the oath. Some will not complete training. Some will complete training and decline to swear. The novitiate is understood as the period in which both the candidate and the order test whether the relationship will hold, and either party may end the path.

### Emeritus Standing

Heartwardens who have completed at least one full century of service may, at the close of their term, accept emeritus standing rather than renewing. Emeritus members retain their bond to the order, their oath of remembrance to the Heartwood, and their counsel to the Captain and the active body. They are not on the Watch rotation; they do not deploy in arms; they are honored attendees at major ceremonies and trusted advisors when their experience is sought. Lethariel's current state is emeritus pending her resumption of the active Captaincy — an arrangement specific to her circumstance.

### Heartwood-Sworn, Not House-Sworn

This distinction is doctrinally central and worth repeating. The Heartwarden order shares its name with House Heartwood because both take their name from the Heartwood. They are not otherwise connected. House Heartwood does not control, command, fund, or constitute the order. Heartwardens come from every elven house of the Feywood and from common origins as well; the order's rolls across millennia show no house dominance and no inheritance pattern. House Heartwood members serve as Heartwardens at the same rate as members of any other house — Lethariel being the most prominent current example — and serve as members of the order, not as representatives of their family. The shared name is a fact of common origin, not a claim of shared authority.

## The Oath

The oath is taken at the Heartwood's base in the surface clearing, in the presence of the Elder Druid of the Heartwood, the Council of Elders' representatives, the assembled Heartwarden community, and any family or community members the candidate has invited as witnesses. The candidate places their hand on the trunk and speaks the oath in Elven; the words are not transcribed in this file, by the order's own preference. The oath commits the candidate to one full century of service to the Heartwood — ten years of training and renewal at the Silent Grove, ninety years of active service — and to the work of guardianship as the order practices it.

The oath is renewable. At the close of each century, the Heartwarden may renew for another century, accept emeritus standing, or release the bond and return to private life. Most elven Heartwardens renew across multiple centuries; some serve five or six centuries before stepping into emeritus standing; a small number have served longer than ten centuries in living memory.

For an elf, the oath in practice is lifelong. A century is a substantial fraction of an elven adult life but not most of it; the choice to renew or step down is real and meaningful at each cycle, and the order's culture treats both renewal and emeritus standing as honorable outcomes. The century structure preserves the dignity of completion — every Heartwarden, regardless of how many centuries they serve, has at least one moment of formally completing their oath rather than only ending it.

For a non-elf, the oath would in principle be takable but in practice unprecedented. The order's official position, which is the canonical Feywood position, is that the oath is open to any individual the Heartwood trusts. The Heartwood's trust is not species-bound. But the path to becoming a Heartwarden requires Heartwood trust + the elven community's trust + sufficient lifespan to commit to a century of service + the time and capacity to complete training. No non-elf in recorded Feywood history has yet aligned all of these requirements. The order does not consider this circumstance a closure; it considers it an alignment that has not yet happened.

## Training

### The Ten-Year Cycle

Training takes ten years. Every Heartwarden — novice or returning — spends a decade at the Silent Grove. Novices complete their initial decade before swearing. Sworn Heartwardens return for a decade at the start of each renewed century.

The structural elegance of this arrangement is that the Silent Grove always holds active Heartwardens in renewal alongside novices in initial training. Renewing Heartwardens carry the order's accumulated experience into the Grove and pass it to the next generation through ordinary daily presence — not as formal teaching, though that exists, but as the texture of who lives and works at the Grove during the renewal year. A novice who completes training has, across their decade, lived alongside ten different cohorts of returning Heartwardens, each carrying a different century's lessons.

### Novice Selection

The order accepts novices on a rolling basis. Selection is conducted by the Captain, the senior instructors at the Silent Grove, and the Elder Druid of the Heartwood, with consultation from the Council of Elders when a candidate's circumstances warrant. The order does not advertise. Candidates either present themselves for selection or are recommended by Heartwardens, druids, Council members, or community members of standing.

Eligibility is open to elves who have reached at least young adulthood — the age at which an elf has completed the equivalent of House Heartwood's wild-upbringing or a comparable formative period in their own family or community. In practice, the order rarely accepts novices in their young-adult years. Most candidates accepted into training are mature adults — typically in their second century or later — who have lived enough of their adult lives to know their own measure and to commit to the work without illusion. Young-adult Heartwardens do exist, and have served well, but they are uncommon.

The order looks for: composure under pressure, sustained discipline, demonstrated relationship with the land, willingness to serve where needed rather than where preferred, and the specific quality elves call *steadiness in waiting* — the capacity to do nothing for long periods without losing edge. The work is mostly waiting. Candidates who cannot wait well do not become Heartwardens.

### Curriculum

The novice decade is structured but not regimented. Time is allocated across:

- **Combat training.** Bow as primary, close-combat secondary. Elven martial tradition, refined for the order's specific work — defense rather than offense, sustained vigilance rather than tactical maneuver, action calibrated to context rather than to doctrine.
- **Land-knowledge.** Druidic foundations, ecological reading, the specific signs the Heartwood and the broader Feywood produce when stressed, the canopy and weather signals, the wildlife patterns. Not at druidic depth, but sufficient for a Heartwarden to read the order's primary signal sources without needing translation.
- **Ceremonial knowledge.** The rites the order witnesses or supports, including the rare communication ritual at the Pool. The order does not officiate; it attends and protects. Knowing what is being protected matters.
- **Order history and lineage.** Generations of Heartwarden service, the recorded events, the lessons preserved in oral tradition. The Discordant War is studied carefully. So are the older crises in oral tradition that have not been written down.
- **Stillness practice.** The contemplative discipline that supports steadiness in waiting. Daily, throughout the decade.
- **Watch participation.** From mid-decade onward, novices serve at the Heartwood under direct senior supervision. They do not stand Watch alone; they accompany sworn Heartwardens and learn the work in place.

The renewal decade for sworn Heartwardens is differently structured. Combat skills and ceremonial knowledge are refreshed rather than learned. Time is spent on instruction of novices, contemplative practice, the specific updating of land-knowledge for changes the renewing Heartwarden may have missed during their ninety years of active service, and the personal work of preparing to renew the oath or step into emeritus standing.

## Service

The active ninety years of each century are spent across several rotating duties.

### Continuous Heartwood-on-Watch

The Heartwood's surface clearing is guarded at all times. At any given moment, between two and four sworn Heartwardens are present at the clearing — typically two on direct watch and two in nearby support. Watch shifts rotate on an eight-hour cycle, with overlap at shift change to maintain continuous coverage and to allow brief consultation between rotations. The Heartwarden on Watch is the position that any visitor to the Heartwood, sanctioned or not, will encounter first.

Watch is not high-tempo work. Most shifts pass without incident. The discipline is in the attention itself — sustained presence at the order's most consequential post for hours that are mostly uneventful. Heartwardens who cannot maintain attention through quiet hours do not last in the order; the selection process and the training cycle filter for this trait early.

### Silent Grove Presence

The Silent Grove holds a permanent Heartwarden presence beyond the renewal-year population. A small number of sworn Heartwardens — typically three to five at any time — are posted to the Grove for instruction, ceremonial support, and coordination with the Druid Master and the broader druidic community. These postings rotate among senior sworn members, usually on multi-decade cycles within active service.

### Council Convenings

When the Council of Elders descends to the Council Chamber beneath the Heartwood for matters of land-weight, the Heartwarden order provides ceremonial security — guarding the chamber's access through the buttress roots, supporting the Council's transit, and witnessing the working without intruding on its substance. Council convenings of land-weight are rare; when they occur, they involve the Captain or a designated senior sworn directly.

### Communication Ritual Witnessing

When the Heartwood drops a branch with a flower and the recipient comes to the Pool to perform the ritual, the order witnesses. At least one Heartwarden — typically a senior sworn or the Captain when present — attends the ritual alongside the Elder Druid of the Heartwood. The Heartwarden's role is integrity of the working, not its substance. They observe that the ritual is conducted properly; they do not participate in the recipient's vision; they do not relay what they witness.

### Response to Land-Stress

When the Feywood enters its stress signals — autumn beginning, plants dying, animals turning aggressive — the Heartwarden order coordinates with the druids and the Council on the appropriate response. Specific responses are calibrated to the specific stress. The order does not deploy in arms unless the stress source is hostile and reachable. More commonly, the response is increased presence, deeper attention, and tighter coordination with the Elder Druid's interpretation of what the land is signaling.

### Crisis Deployment

In genuine crisis — the Discordant War being the canonical instance — the order deploys in arms beyond the Heartwood and Silent Grove. Such deployment is rare and is decided by the Council of Elders in consultation with the Captain. During the Discordant War, approximately twenty Heartwardens deployed alongside the larger Greenshield contingent through the Hollow Crown's western threshold; the remaining eighty maintained the home posts at full strength, with the renewal cohort at the Grove ready to return to active service if the deployment had extended or escalated.

## Equipment

Heartwardens carry traditional elven martial gear refined for the order's specific work.

The primary weapon is the bow. Most Heartwardens carry a Longbough or comparable family bow earned in their pre-novitiate years. Senior sworn sometimes carry distinctive bows — heirlooms from prior service, gifts from the Feywood community, or rare pieces that have come into the order's keeping over centuries. Lethariel's Heartwood bow, cut from the branch the Heartwood dropped to her during the Discordant War, is the most consequential singular weapon in the order's history; she carries it during active Captaincy and keeps it at Heartwood Estate during her current emeritus standing.

Close-combat secondary weapons are individually chosen — typically a sword of moderate length, occasionally a paired set of shorter blades, occasionally a polearm for Heartwardens whose preference and training run that direction. The order does not standardize close-combat equipment; it standardizes competence with whatever the individual carries.

Armor is light. The order's work is sustained presence and rapid response in environments where heavy armor would impede both. Heartwardens wear elven layered cloth-and-leather appropriate to weather and shift, with light bracers and minimal hard pieces. Defense is mobility, awareness, and skill, not encumbrance.

Heartwardens carry no insignia in the realm sense. The order is recognized by elves of the Feywood without need for badge. Realm visitors who interact with Heartwardens recognize them by context (Watch presence at the Heartwood, ceremonial role at observances) and by the specific bearing the order's training produces.

## Eligibility

Eligibility is open to any individual the Heartwood trusts who can meet the order's other requirements — training capacity, century-of-service commitment, and the elven community's recognition of their fitness for the work. The Heartwood's trust is not species-bound, and the order's official position, which is the canonical Feywood position, is that no species barrier exists in principle.

In practice, the order has been entirely elven across recorded Feywood history. The reasons are practical rather than doctrinal. Most non-elven species do not live long enough to commit to a century of service. Most non-elven individuals do not live in the Feywood long enough to develop the relationship with the Heartwood that earns the tree's trust. The few non-elven residents of the Feywood who have earned full integration over the centuries have not, in the cases recorded, sought Heartwarden service — and the order has not recruited them, on the principle that the work is for those who present themselves to it.

The order does not consider its all-elven character a closure. It considers it an alignment that has not yet happened. If a non-elf candidate were to present themselves with the Heartwood's trust established, the elven community's recognition secured, and the lifespan or willingness to commit to a century, the order would conduct the same selection process it conducts for any candidate. The result might or might not be a sworn Heartwarden. The path would not be barred.

## Relationship to Other Orders

### The Greenshield

The Greenshield is the Feywood's primary defensive military order — settlement defense, daily security, patrols, the realm-to-realm border. The Greenshield is much larger than the Heartwarden and carries the bulk of the Feywood's standing arms.

The two orders are parallel, not nested. Neither commands the other. Coordination occurs at the leadership level — the Heartwarden Captain and the Greenshield Commander consult routinely on matters that touch both orders' work — and at the operational level, primarily at the Feywood's borders and during deployments where both orders are engaged. During the Discordant War, the Heartwarden contingent and the Greenshield contingent operated as one force under combined command but retained their separate identities and chains within the larger structure.

Heartwardens are sometimes drawn from former Greenshield members; this is honored in the order's culture but is not a typical pathway. Most Heartwardens come to the order from other backgrounds.

### Druidic Practice

The order works in continuous coordination with the druidic community of the Feywood, particularly the Elder Druid of the Heartwood and the Druid Master of the Silent Grove (the latter is the Reading Glade tradition's working seat). Druids interpret the land's signals; Heartwardens act on those interpretations when action is needed. Druids officiate ceremonies; Heartwardens witness and protect them. The relationship is operationally close and culturally distinct: druids attend to the land's voice, Heartwardens attend to the land's safety. Both serve the same relationship.

### The Council of Elders

The order serves the Council of Elders in the structural sense — the Council holds the political authority of the Feywood, and the Heartwarden operates within that authority. The Captain answers to the Council on matters of order policy, deployment, and significant ceremonial work. Day-to-day operations are not Council-directed; the order runs itself in the work the Council has established it to do.

When the Council convenes in the Council Chamber beneath the Heartwood, the order provides security and ceremonial support. When the Council deliberates in its primary chambers in the Heartwood Approach, the order does not attend in formal capacity; the work there is civic and political rather than land-weight, and the order's presence is not required.

## Notable Heartwardens

### Lethariel Heartwood

Captain emeritus. Discordant War commander. Currently in residence at Heartwood Estate in House Heartwood's Outer Old-Growth Wild seat, supporting the family's current Proving candidate through the preparation phase. Will resume active Captaincy when the candidate departs for the Proving.

Lethariel is the only Heartwarden in the order's recorded history to have received a Heartwood communication via the branch-and-Pool ritual during her service. The branch fell at the Council Chamber convening that deadlocked on the Oath of the Fallen's mutual-aid clause; Lethariel performed the ritual, received the vision, and the vision overrode the deadlock. The Feywood deployed. She led the contingent through the Hollow Crown's western threshold and fought alongside the Platinum Warden through the war's final days. She was at the pit's edge when Vindrael gave his life. The Heartwood-branch bow she carries was cut from the branch the Heartwood had dropped to her — the only heirloom of its kind in the order's history.

Her current emeritus standing is a particular case rather than a general pattern. She has not stepped fully into emeritus retirement; she has stepped aside temporarily to support her family's preparation phase, with the Captaincy waiting for her return. The order recognizes the arrangement as valid under the Captain's discretion and the Council's concurrence.

## Authoring Notes

The Heartwarden is a Feywood-internal order, parallel to the Greenshield and connected to the Ranger (which is a specialist branch of the Greenshield). The realm-side `prompts/groups.md` carries pointer entries for all three orders; this file is the canonical source for Heartwarden doctrine, structure, and operations.

**Size is canonical at 100 active sworn**, distributed approximately 90 in active service and 10 in renewal at the Silent Grove. Plus approximately 15-20 novices in training at any given time. Plus emeritus members in honored standing. Total order presence including all categories: approximately 120-140 individuals at any time.

**The century-long oath structure is canonical.** Ten years of training/renewal at the Silent Grove + ninety years of active service. Renewable indefinitely. The structural consequence — a continuous flow of active Heartwardens cycling through the Silent Grove for renewal alongside novices in initial training — is the elegant explanation for how the Grove maintains both its training function and its connection to the active force.

**Lethariel's Captain emeritus standing is a particular case**, not a general pattern. She has stepped aside to support the family's Proving candidate; she resumes active Captaincy when the candidate departs. Other emeritus Heartwardens are typically post-retirement rather than mid-arrangement.

**The Heartwood-sworn / not House-sworn distinction is canonical** and important. The order's name reflects the Heartwood (the tree), not House Heartwood (the family). House Heartwood members serve as Heartwardens at the same rate as any other house. The shared name is a fact of common origin, not a claim of shared authority. GPT scenes should resist any framing that depicts the order as House Heartwood's household force.

**Cross-species eligibility is canonical** but unprecedented in practice. The order's official position is that the path is open to any individual the Heartwood trusts. No non-elven Heartwarden has yet existed because the alignment of trust + lifespan + commitment + recognition has not yet occurred for any non-elven candidate. GPT scenes involving discussion of order eligibility should preserve both the principled openness and the practical absence.

**The 20-Heartwarden Discordant War deployment** is canonical. The remaining ~80 active sworn maintained home posts at full strength during the deployment; the renewal cohort at the Silent Grove was held ready to return to active service if needed.

**The Heartwarden on Watch** at the Heartwood is a continuous canonical presence. Specific Heartwardens on Watch can be instantiated for scenes; the position itself is always filled.

**Equipment is bow-primary, close-combat-secondary, light armor.** Lethariel's Heartwood bow is the order's only canonical heirloom of supernatural origin; other Heartwardens carry ordinary elven martial gear of varying personal character.

**The order does not standardize insignia.** Heartwardens are recognized by context and bearing rather than by visible badge. Realm scenes involving Heartwarden identification should reflect this — visitors recognize a Heartwarden by where they are and how they carry themselves, not by a uniform or sigil.

The relationship to House Heartwood, the Greenshield, the druidic community, and the Council of Elders is canonical as described.

`discovered: false` at the canonical level. Most realm residents know the order exists in concept; very few have direct knowledge of its structure, training, or operations. Characters who have observed the order in operation (sanctioned visitors at the Heartwood, scholars with research permissions, Discordant War-era figures) have rare standing in this regard.
