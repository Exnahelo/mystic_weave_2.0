# The Elder Roots

The druidic community of the Feywood — the practitioners whose work is to attend the Feywood as a living system, to read what the land communicates, and to keep the elven community in relationship with the broader ecology that sustains it. The Elder Roots are a continuous community of practice, organized around shared work, distributed across the Feywood's primary sites, and led by senior figures whose authority comes from accumulated capability rather than from formal office. The community's name reflects its central canon: the Heartwood's roots reach across the Hollow Crown, and the elder druids' attention runs through that network alongside it. To attend the land is to attend along the elder roots.

## Doctrine and Purpose

The Elder Roots exist to maintain the elven community's relationship with the Feywood. The Feywood is a living, responsive system. The Heartwood is conscious. The land communicates. Most of this happens regardless of whether anyone is paying attention — the system is self-sustaining at its baseline. But the elven community's life within the Feywood is itself part of the system, and the elven side of that relationship requires conscious attention. The Elder Roots hold that attention on behalf of the community.

The work breaks into several pillars:

**Reading the land.** Continuous attentive observation of the Feywood's signals — canopy behavior, weather patterns, wildlife movement, vegetation health, the ambient magical field, the specific signs the Heartwood produces. Druids accumulate the depth of attention that ordinary life cannot sustain.

**Heartwood stewardship.** Direct attention to the Heartwood itself — daily presence at the surface clearing, ceremonial attendance at the cardinal stones, support for the rare communication ritual at the Pool, and the long quiet work of being in relationship with the tree.

**Ceremonial practice.** Conducting the elven calendar's seasonal observances, presiding at significant community moments (births, comings-of-age, oath-takings, funerals), and holding the smaller individual ceremonies that thread elven daily life with sacred attention.

**Land-stress response.** When the Feywood enters its stress signals — autumn beginning, plant die-off, animal aggression — the druids interpret what is happening and coordinate the community's response. The Heartwarden order acts on those interpretations when defensive action is needed; the druids hold the interpretation itself.

**Knowledge transmission.** The continuous work of teaching apprentices, preserving oral tradition across generations, and ensuring that druidic capability persists in unbroken continuity. The Reading Glade is the community's primary site for sustained practice and transmission.

The community is non-political in institutional posture but not naive about the Feywood's broader entanglements. Druids advise the Council of Elders on matters touching the land; they coordinate with the Heartwarden order on Heartwood and Silent Grove protection; they consult with the Greenshields on patrol-route ecology and outer-range conditions. They do not seek political authority and do not exercise it directly, but their judgment carries weight in the matters they are asked to interpret.

## Leadership

### The Elder Druid of the Heartwood

The community's senior figure. The Elder Druid is the head druid of the Feywood — the practitioner whose authority runs across the broader community and whose specific stewardship attaches to the Heartwood itself. The title reflects the Heartwood-specific work; the role's full scope encompasses the entire community's leadership.

The Elder Druid's organizational base is the Silent Grove. They hold council with the Druid Master on operational matters, set doctrine for the broader community, oversee training and apprenticeship cycles, represent the community to the Council of Elders, and coordinate with the Heartwarden Captain on shared concerns. The role is conducted from the Silent Grove for community-leadership work and from the Heartwood for direct stewardship — the Elder Druid spends substantial continuous time at the Heartwood, often holding personal practice at the clearing or attending Council Chamber convenings when they occur.

The Elder Druid is not selected through formal process. The role passes by recognition rather than by election or appointment. When the current Elder Druid steps down, the community's senior druids consult, the Druid Master coordinates the conversation, and a successor is recognized — often someone who has been operating informally as the senior figure-in-waiting for years or decades. Council of Elders concurrence is conventional but not constitutive.

Tenures vary widely. Some Elder Druids serve for centuries; some for less.

### The Druid Master of the Silent Grove

The community's second-in-command. The Druid Master oversees the Silent Grove's day-to-day druidic work, coordinates training and apprenticeship, manages the Reading Glade's practice, and serves as the community's working interface for matters that do not require the Elder Druid's specific weight. The two roles are partnered: the Elder Druid sets direction, the Druid Master executes; the Elder Druid attends the Heartwood, the Druid Master attends the Grove; the Elder Druid speaks to the Council, the Druid Master speaks to the broader community.

The Druid Master is selected by the Elder Druid in consultation with the senior druids. The role is functionally permanent during the Elder Druid's tenure — Druid Masters typically serve until the Elder Druid steps down, at which point a new arrangement is established (sometimes the Druid Master succeeds to Elder Druid; sometimes a different senior druid is recognized).

The Druid Master is the figure most realm visitors with druidic business will encounter. The Elder Druid is more reserved by virtue of their work; the Druid Master conducts more of the community's outward-facing activity.

### Senior Druids

Veterans of long practice — typically at least a century of active druidic work, often substantially more. Senior Druids hold informal authority within their specialties (land-reading at the Reading Glade, Heartwood attendance, ceremonial work at specific seasonal observances, regional stewardship at outer-Feywood sites) and serve as the community's primary instructors for apprentices. Approximately twenty to thirty Senior Druids active at any given time.

### Working Druids

The body of the community. Approximately one hundred fifty active druids hold sustained practice at any given time, distributed across the Feywood's primary sites and outer-range stewardships. Working Druids hold equal standing among themselves; differentiation is by specialty, experience, and the specific work each is suited to.

### Apprentices

At any given time, approximately twenty to thirty apprentices in various stages of training at the Silent Grove. The apprenticeship is long — typically twenty to thirty years of formal training plus extended subsequent practice before an apprentice is recognized as a working druid. The community's standards are high; not every apprentice completes the path, and those who do not are not stigmatized.

## Working Sites

### The Silent Grove

The community's organizational center. Where the Elder Druid and Druid Master are based, where apprentices train, where most ceremonial work is prepared, and where the community's day-to-day coordination happens. The Grove's broader role as the elven civilization's origin point and the Heartwarden training site means that druidic work here is conducted alongside other functions; the Grove is shared, not exclusive to the druids.

Approximately eighty active druids stationed at the Silent Grove at any given time, including the leadership cohort, instructional staff, apprentices in training, and supporting roles.

### The Heartwood

Direct attendance at the Feywood's living center. A small cohort of druids — typically eight to twelve at any given time — maintains continuous presence at the Heartwood clearing, supporting the Heartwarden on Watch, conducting daily observance, attending the cardinal stones, and serving as the practical interface between the community and the tree itself. The Elder Druid is present at the Heartwood for substantial periods; other senior druids rotate through extended postings here.

### The Reading Glade

The community's primary site for sustained land-reading practice. Where druids interpret the Feywood's signals at depth — observation circles for learning the practice, sustained working spaces for individual long-form land-reading, and the oral-tradition seats where senior druids transmit the accumulated knowledge of generations. Approximately thirty to forty druids work at the Reading Glade at any given time, including specialists in land-reading whose careers are spent largely in this work.

The Reading Glade's character is distinctively quiet and attentive. The work is patient, slow, and deeply contextual; druids working here often spend years on specific questions about the Feywood's patterns. See the Reading Glade location node for the site's full character.

### Distributed Presence

Beyond the three primary sites, druids are present in smaller numbers across the broader Feywood — at outer-range stewardships, at smaller settlements, at specific zones whose ecological conditions warrant continuous druidic attention. Approximately fifty druids serve in distributed posts at any given time. Some are tied to House Silverbough's wild-stewardship work in the Outer Old-Growth Wild; some are dedicated stewards of specific water-features (the Heartwater Basin's drainage, certain stream zones); some maintain individual research presences at specific Feywood-edge zones.

## Practice

### Daily Stewardship

The community's continuous baseline work. Druids present at any of the working sites maintain continuous attention to the Feywood's daily condition. Most of this is uneventful; the value is in the continuity itself, the unbroken thread of attention across centuries.

### Land-Reading at the Reading Glade

Specialist interpretation of Feywood signals. Druids working at the Reading Glade — both apprentices learning the practice and senior druids continuing it across decades — read the land at depth. The work involves long-form observation of specific patterns, comparison across seasons and generations, the slow accumulation of understanding that ordinary attention cannot reach. Reports flow from the Reading Glade to the Elder Druid, and through the Elder Druid to the broader community and the Council when warranted.

### Heartwood Stewardship

The Elder Druid's specific work, supported by the Heartwood-stationed druid cohort. Direct attention to the Heartwood — daily observance, ceremonial attendance at the cardinal stones, support for the rare communication ritual at the Pool, witnessing of Council convenings of land-weight in the Chamber. The work is conducted in the Heartwood's specific intense register and requires druids whose practice can sustain that intensity.

### Ceremonial Work

The Feywood's seasonal calendar runs continuous observances at the equinoxes, solstices, and the specific phase-turns the elven year recognizes. Druids preside at major ceremonies — held at the Heartwood for the most significant, at the Silent Grove for community-scale observances, at smaller sites for neighborhood and family practice. The ceremonial work includes the smaller observances threaded through elven daily life: birth blessings, comings-of-age, oath markings, funerals.

### Land-Stress Response

When the Feywood enters its stress signals, the community's interpretation drives the broader response. The Elder Druid coordinates the community's reading; the Druid Master coordinates with the Heartwarden Captain and the Greenshield Commander on operational implications; the Council of Elders is informed and may convene to deliberate. Druids are not the actors when defensive response is required, but they are the interpreters of what response is needed. In recorded memory, the Feywood has reached the plant-die-off stage twice; both events were navigated through druidic interpretation as the central interpretive resource.

### Knowledge Transmission

Apprenticeship is the community's primary mode of knowledge continuity. Senior druids work directly with apprentices over decades. The Reading Glade's oral-tradition seats are the formal site for major transmissions; informal teaching threads through every aspect of community life. Written records exist in the elven idiom but are marginal to druidic practice; the bulk of the community's knowledge moves orally and through embodied teaching.

## Training and Membership

### Apprenticeship

Druidic training begins by the apprentice presenting themselves to the community, typically through introduction by an existing druid or a recognized member of the broader Feywood community who has identified the candidate's aptitude. The Druid Master conducts initial evaluation in consultation with senior druids; promising candidates are accepted into apprenticeship at the Silent Grove.

The apprenticeship runs twenty to thirty years of formal training, structured as long-form work under one or more senior druids' direct instruction. Years are spent on observation, ceremonial practice, ecological knowledge, the specific arts of land-reading, and the ongoing relationship-building with the Heartwood and the Feywood that defines druidic identity. Training is patient and unhurried; an apprentice who needs more time receives it without stigma.

After formal training, the new druid serves an extended period of supervised practice — typically another decade or more — before being recognized as a working druid. The transition is gradual and the recognition is by community consensus rather than by ceremonial moment.

### Membership

Membership is open to elves of the Feywood who present aptitude and pass the apprenticeship.

Non-elven membership is technically possible under the same Heartwood-trust framework that applies to the Heartwarden and the Greenshield orders. In practice, the apprenticeship's length and the depth of relationship-with-land that druidic practice requires make non-elven membership exceptionally rare. A handful of integrated non-elven Feywood residents have entered apprenticeship across the millennia; some have completed the path. Their numbers are small, their work is genuine, and the community recognizes them as full druids without qualification once recognized.

Membership comes from no particular house. House Silverbough has a slightly elevated rate of druidic apprenticeship among its members (the house's wild-stewardship tradition produces candidates with relevant aptitude), but the rate is not pronounced and the community's composition cuts broadly across all houses and common origins. See `noble_families.md`.

## Boundary with Realm-Side Druidic Practice

The Elder Roots are Feywood-specific. They do not maintain institutional connections with druidic traditions elsewhere in the world, and they do not assume alignment with realm-side druids by virtue of shared practice category.

This is canonical. Druidic practice is not innately good or innately aligned. Druids operate from specific ecological-philosophical positions; different positions produce different practitioners; agreement is not automatic. A druid working in the Mystic Wetlands serving that ecology's specific dynamics is not automatically a friend or ally of an Elder Roots druid; they may have profoundly different views on what "respecting the land" means in their respective contexts. A druid working with realm-side institutions on ecological questions may have functional working relationships with realm authorities that an Elder Roots druid would never consider. A druid serving a corrupted or hostile ecology — in principle possible, though rare — would be in active opposition to Elder Roots principles.

The Elder Roots' general posture toward unknown druids is **respectful caution**. They recognize the practice category and treat practitioners with the courtesy due to fellow attenders of land. They do not extend institutional trust without specific demonstrated alignment. Tension is canonically possible — particularly on questions of relationship with sentient land, on the role of magical intervention in ecological dynamics, on the proper limits of druidic action vs druidic restraint.

GPT scenes involving cross-tradition druidic interaction should preserve this. Two druids meeting are not automatically allies. The Feywood's druids would not attack a foreign druid without cause, but they would not assume alignment without demonstration.

## Relationship to Other Groups

### The Heartwardens

The closest operational relationship in Feywood civic life. Druids interpret signals; Heartwardens act on those interpretations when action is required. The Elder Druid and the Heartwarden Captain consult routinely. Heartwardens witnessed at the Pool (during communication rituals) work alongside the Elder Druid as joint witnesses. Heartwarden training at the Silent Grove includes druidic foundations sufficient for the order's reading work, taught by Reading Glade druids during the Heartwarden cycle.

### The Greenshields and the Rangers

Operational coordination on outer-range work, patrol routes, and ecological reading. Druids embedded in outer-range stewardships work alongside Ranger reconnaissance teams when their zones overlap; Greenshield patrol units consult druids on specific zone conditions; the Druid Master and the Captain of the Ranger Branch coordinate on matters that touch both communities' work.

### The Council of Elders

The Council includes druidic representation through the Elder Druid (who serves as a recognized counselor on matters of land) and through the Druid Master in their absence. Druids do not hold formal Council seats in the same constitutional sense that house representatives do, but their advisory presence on land-related deliberations is canonical and continuous. When the Council convenes in the Chamber beneath the Heartwood for matters of land-weight, the Elder Druid is present and is heard.

### Realm-Side Druidic Practice

Separate. See above. No institutional connections. Cross-tradition interaction is possible but not assumed to be friendly.

### Realm-Side Forces (Dragon Guard, Wardens, etc.)

No formal relationship. The Elder Roots are a Feywood-internal community and do not engage realm-side military or institutional forces in their normal work. Council-mediated contact may occur when ecological matters touch cross-realm concerns, but such contact is rare and conducted through Council channels rather than directly.
