# Feywood

The liminal forest realm that exists within the Hollow Crown's geography but does not obey its ordinary rules. The Feywood appears from outside as a contained patch of the western temperate forest — a pocket perhaps half a day's walk across at its widest, bordered clearly by the realm-side Feywood Border. Inside, the Feywood is larger than any realm-side geography can account for. Its forests extend in ways that are not measurable from outside. Its interior holds the elven city of Thornveil around the Heartwood that is the land's living center, the Silent Grove at the elven origin point, secondary settlements including Vaelmere, sacred groves, and the wild terrain between them — all occupying space that, by any ordinary reckoning, should not fit within the Feywood's external footprint. The Feywood is not hostile. It is not corrupted. It is magical land sustained by the Heartwood's continuous working, protected by the elves who have lived here since before the realm existed, and by the land itself in return.

## Scene Texture

Crossing the Border into the Feywood is immediate and gentle. The moment of crossing carries the shift described at the Border — the change in air, the altered quality of sound, the brief passage-sensation — and then the traveler is in the Feywood. The initial terrain looks like a continuation of the western temperate forest that lies behind them: tall trees, dappled canopy, forest floor with leaf litter and ferns. But within the first few minutes of walking, the geometry begins to be the Feywood's geometry rather than the realm's.

Distances do not behave consistently. A tree that appears twenty paces away may take thirty paces to reach. Another tree may be reached in fewer paces than its distance suggested. Paths that seemed to curve one way resolve into different curves when walked. The effect is disorienting for first-time visitors; it is ordinary to elves who live here and to the Hall of Guides members who know the Feywood professionally. What seems like a trick is a feature: the Feywood's geography responds to the traveler's intent and attention through the Heartwood's continuous reading of those who cross into the field, and those who walk with respect and purpose find their way more easily than those who do not.

The forest itself is denser and older than realm-side forest. The trees are of familiar species — oaks, beeches, the broader hardwoods of the western temperate biome — but they grow to ancient proportions. Individual trees reach heights and trunk diameters that no realm-side tree achieves. The canopy above reaches staggering heights, and the understory beneath it is itself a rich ecosystem — layered growth, flowering vines, the occasional specimen of plant life that grows only here and that the realm's herbalists would recognize but never find outside the Feywood.

Light in the Feywood behaves with its own character. Dappled canopy light falls in patterns that are more complex than ordinary sun-through-leaves, as though the canopy is thicker or the light is passing through more than leaves. Clearings within the Feywood hold specific qualities of light — some are bright and warm, some are cool and blue-green, some hold a pale gold quality that realm-side light does not produce. Night in the Feywood is not merely darkness: bioluminescent flora glows faintly throughout the understory, providing ambient illumination even under heavy canopy. Moonlight, when the canopy admits it, has a silvering effect on everything it touches.

The sounds of the Feywood are rich and layered. Birdsong from species that do not exist in the realm. The calls of animals that are recognizable and animals that are not. Wind through canopy producing a kind of choral effect when it moves through the taller trees. Occasional whispers that are not from any identifiable source — not voices, exactly, but sounds the mind tries to interpret as language and cannot quite resolve. Travelers learn to let these sounds pass without attending to them too closely.

The air carries a specific quality — cooler than realm-side forest, slightly more humid, faintly sweet in ways that echo Dewhollow's ambient character but at greater intensity. The Feywood's air also carries an ambient magical presence that sensitive individuals can feel directly and most people register as a background sensation without being able to name. Long exposure to Feywood air produces subtle effects comparable to but distinct from Crystalhaven's lake water practice — elves who live here all their lives carry the Feywood's signature in their bearing and skin quality, and long-term human residents eventually develop something of the same.

Wildlife is abundant and varied. Deer, boar, smaller game — many species shared with realm-side forest, some specific to the Feywood. Predators exist in the deeper terrain — larger cats, occasional bear species, rarer predators that realm-side biologists would classify as mythic but are simply local fauna here. The Feywood's wildlife is not directed by the Heartwood; the animals are not its agents and the trees are not its servants. What the Heartwood does is communicate with the broader ecosystem, and the ecosystem responds from its own nature. A wolf that moves toward a disrespectful traveler does so as a wolf would move toward a threat in its territory, prompted by the Heartwood's reading of the visitor but acting on its own pattern. Respectful travelers find the wildlife indifferent or accommodating; disrespectful travelers find it watchful or actively unfriendly. The shift is not ordered; it is communicated.

## The Liminal Geometry

The Feywood's most distinctive feature is its internal geometry. Understood properly, this is not a trick or a hazard but a simple fact of the place: the Feywood's interior contains more space than its external footprint should allow, and the space responds to travelers.

The geometry is the Heartwood's working. The protective field that holds the Feywood's larger-on-the-inside character is sustained continuously by the Heartwood — a sentient, conscious tree at the Feywood's living center whose deeper canon is treated in the `heartwood` node. The field reads visitors as they cross into it, and the geography responds accordingly. This is not magic the elves invoke or maintain; it is the Heartwood's own continuous presence, the shape the tree has given the land it lives in.

**Internal scale.** The Feywood contains the city of Thornveil, the Heartwood at its sacred center, the Silent Grove at the elven origin point, the secondary settlement of Vaelmere at the Border-facing edge, elven noble family estates scattered through the old forest, and wild terrain extending between these locations. Travel between major Feywood locations — Thornveil to Vaelmere, the Heartwood to the Silent Grove, any location to the Border — takes hours or days depending on the journey and the traveler's relationship with the land. None of this fits within the Feywood's external dimensions as observed from the realm side. This is simply how the Feywood is.

**Path-finding.** Travelers moving through the Feywood with clear intent and appropriate disposition find their way. The land accommodates them. Paths appear where paths are needed. Distances become traversable. Landmarks orient. This is not magical guidance in a sense that can be deliberately invoked; it is the Heartwood's reading of the traveler combined with the natural cooperation of a land that responds to communication. Elves experience this as ordinary. Hall of Guides members experience this through their professional training. Inexperienced travelers experience it as lucky wayfinding.

**Displacement.** Travelers moving through the Feywood without clear intent, or without respect, or while actively disoriented, experience the opposite. Distances become uncertain. Paths do not resolve. Landmarks repeat in impossible configurations. The traveler becomes lost not because the Feywood is hostile but because the land is not extending itself to accommodate them. Extended lost-wandering in the Feywood is genuinely dangerous — and the danger is not only navigational. The Heartwood's roots have linked over millennia with plant root systems throughout the Hollow Crown, and travelers expelled from the Feywood may emerge through that network rather than at the Border. The exact mechanics and outcomes of displacement — proper exit, improper neutral exit, detected ill will, and the rare favor case — are detailed in the `heartwood` node under The Root Network. The short summary: respectful exit returns the visitor to the Border; harmful intent may deposit them anywhere the network reaches, which is essentially anywhere with vegetation in the Hollow Crown.

**Time.** Time in the Feywood is usually consistent with time in the realm, but not always. Short visits typically produce no time discrepancy. Longer visits sometimes do — travelers spending days in the Feywood occasionally emerge to find that more or fewer days have passed in the realm. The effect is minor in most cases; travelers rarely lose or gain more than a day or two. The phenomenon is not predictable from any observable cause; it is another feature of the Feywood's liminal character, and one of the lesser-understood aspects of the Heartwood's continuous working.

## Weather and the Heartwood's Health

Within the Feywood, weather is linked to the Heartwood's health. Under healthy conditions the Feywood holds in spring and summer continuously, as though the seasons that turn outside the Feywood do not turn within it. When the Heartwood is troubled, threatened, or stressed, the weather shifts — autumn beginning is a signal that something is wrong, winter starting is a signal that something is very wrong, plant die-off indicates the Heartwood is drawing on the land's life to sustain its working, and animal aggression follows from ecological stress downstream of plant die-off. The full weather-as-health-signal canon is treated in the `heartwood` node.

GPT narration of Feywood scenes should reflect the appropriate state for the narrative moment. Default state is Standing Green — healthy, in spring or summer, with the broader ambient field intact. Other states (Turning, Stillfall, etc.) are drawn from canonical Heartwood-stress conditions and should be used only when the narrative reflects them.

## The Elven Homeland

The Feywood is the elves' homeland and has been since the founding. Canonical elven origin: after the Cataclysm — the meteor that crashed into the valley and threatened to be a world-killing event — the elves of the age gave up their immortality to repair the land. The first trees of the valley rose from that sacrifice. The elves settled among those first trees, at the site that is now the Silent Grove. From that founding the elves have lived in the Feywood continuously. The Heartwood was discovered later, by an elf adjacent to the Silent Grove community; House Heartwood took its name from that discovery. Both deeper canons are treated in the `silent-grove` and `heartwood` nodes.

The elven population is the Feywood's majority demographic. Ancient noble families trace their lineage back through generations whose total span is measured in millennia. Common families, craftfolk, druidic practitioners, warriors, traders — the full social spectrum of elven life is represented within the Feywood. Elves of the Feywood are generally taller and slighter of build than realm-side humans, with the specific physiology that magical ambient exposure produces over generations. Their hair and eye coloration runs across a range that includes colors realm-side species do not produce — silver-white, deep forest green, luminous amber.

Non-elves live in the Feywood as well, but their presence is conditional on demonstrated respect. Humans, dwarves, drakari, orcs, and members of other realm species can become Feywood residents if their conduct proves acceptable over time. The integration is not ceremonial — there is no oath-taking, no acceptance ritual — but it is real. A non-elf who has lived in the Feywood for years, treated the land well, formed relationships with elven neighbors, and demonstrated through conduct that they belong becomes a resident in every meaningful sense. Such residents are uncommon but not rare. The Heartwood's protection extends to anyone the Heartwood trusts, regardless of species — an elf-deep relationship with the land is possible for any individual willing to live it, and the Heartwood recognizes the relationship rather than the species. The elves' founding bond is the deepest, but it is not exclusive.

The elves' relationship with the Feywood is not one of ownership. The Feywood is not property held by the elves; it is a place the elves live in and protect. The elves speak of being the Feywood's stewards rather than its owners. This framing is meaningful to them and shapes their politics, economics, and religion.

## Governance

The Feywood is governed by a **Council of Elders** — a consensus-based body drawn from elven noble families, druidic practitioners, and recognized elder figures whose wisdom has accumulated over centuries. The Council does not have a single leader. Deliberations move at a pace that realm-side observers often find remarkable — decisions may take weeks or years to reach, as the Council considers implications across longer time-horizons than human politics accommodates.

The Council's primary responsibilities are:

- **Heartwood stewardship.** Protection of the Heartwood at the Feywood's sacred center is the Council's foremost responsibility and the reason the Council exists.
- **External diplomatic coordination.** Relationships with the realm, the Oath of the Fallen's ongoing obligations, and the rare extra-realm contact all route through Council deliberation.
- **Internal dispute resolution.** The Feywood's settlements and noble families operate with considerable autonomy; disputes between them or matters affecting the broader elven community come to the Council.
- **Military coordination.** The Feywood's formal military answers to the Council. In ordinary times the military trains and prepares; in the rare crisis, the Council directs.
- **Resource and mercantile oversight.** The elven end of the realm-Feywood mercantile relationship is managed through Council-appointed Trade Envoys with Council-approved authority.

**Noble families** hold specific portfolios and responsibilities within the broader Council structure. Certain families have traditionally produced warriors; others druids; others diplomats. These traditions run deep but are not rigid — families evolve, new roles emerge, children born into one tradition sometimes find themselves drawn to another.

The Council's primary chambers are in Thornveil's Heartwood Approach district, where routine Council business is conducted. Council convenings of land-weight — decisions whose implications touch the Feywood itself rather than merely its institutional affairs — are held in the Council Chamber beneath the Heartwood, accessed through the natural opening between the tree's buttress roots. Such convenings are rare and are documented in the `heartwood` node. The descent into the Chamber is itself part of the deliberation's meaning.

## Military Structure

The Feywood maintains three distinct martial orders, organized as parallel institutions rather than nested hierarchies. Each has a specific scope and a specific operational profile.

### Heartwardens

Elite sacred guardians of the Feywood's inner sanctums. The Heartwardens' primary charge is the protection of the Heartwood itself, the Silent Grove, and the most sensitive druidic and ceremonial sites. They serve as the highest martial expression of elven sacred duty: disciplined, selective, and rarely deployed for ordinary security work.

Core duties:

- Protect the Heartwood and its immediate sacred approaches.
- Guard the Silent Grove and its training grounds.
- Defend major ritual sites, rites, and sacred persons.
- Escort druids or ceremonial processions when threat level warrants it.
- Respond to existential threats against the Feywood's spiritual core.
- Serve as a rare crisis-response force in exceptional wartime conditions.

Heartwardens are stationed primarily at sacred-core locations rather than spread broadly across settlements. Their training is conducted at the Silent Grove on a seven-year cycle (see `silent-grove` for the detailed cycle). Selection is by honor — noble families sometimes send their children through specific coming-of-age trials with the aim of Heartwarden service, and some non-noble elves earn selection through distinguished conduct. Heartwarden oaths are sworn at the Heartwood's surface clearing, in the presence of the Elder Druid, the Council of Elders' representatives, and the assembled Heartwarden community.

The Heartwarden order is currently led by a Captain. Lethariel Heartwood holds Captain-emeritus standing during the current Proving cycle of her successor; the interim Captain handles day-to-day order business. Lethariel's Heartwood bow — cut from a branch the Heartwood dropped during the Discordant War, when her vision in the Council Chamber overrode the Council's deadlock and mandated the Feywood deployment against the Mordraxian Rebels — is the order's most consequential historical artifact and is kept at Heartwood Estate during her emeritus period.

### Greenshields

The main practical defense and security force of the elven settlements. Greenshields are the visible, day-to-day protectors of civic life, trade movement, sanctioned routes, and Border approaches. They are the body most people encounter in ordinary matters of safety, patrol, and order.

Core duties:

- Maintain security in Thornveil and Vaelmere.
- Patrol roads, trails, Border thresholds, and sanctioned crossings.
- Protect trade movement and escort travelers or caravans when needed.
- Respond to local threats, disturbances, and missing-person incidents.
- Handle routine lawkeeping and public-order concerns.
- Watch for poaching, smuggling, trespass, and minor hostile incursions.

Greenshields are broadly distributed and publicly visible. They are practical, reliable, and rooted in everyday service, structured for regular patrol, rapid response, and territorial familiarity. The Greenshield Commander operates from headquarters in Thornveil's Noble Groves district. Greenshield training is conducted across the Feywood's settlements rather than centralized at a single site; recruitment draws broadly from the elven civic population and from the rare non-elven residents who have earned the standing required.

Every adult elf of certain noble families undergoes Greenshield training; many non-noble elves do as well. The result is a civilian population that is, by realm standards, unusually combat-capable — a feature of elven civic life rather than evidence of militarization.

### Rangers

A specialized branch of the Greenshields focused on outer-range fieldwork, small-team operations, and low-visibility response beyond the main settlements. Rangers are scouts, trackers, observers, and precision responders who work in the greater wilds, along remote borders, and in places where a larger visible force is inefficient or counterproductive.

Core peacetime duties:

- Reconnaissance of the greater wilds and remote approaches.
- Trail-watch and remote patrol along low-traffic routes.
- Quiet information gathering and disturbance checks beyond ordinary patrol range.
- Tracking intruders, hostile movement, corruption signs, or anomalies.
- Observation missions for druids, the Council, or Greenshield command.
- Maintaining awareness of distant conditions before threats reach settlements.
- Selective interdiction or tactical strikes when small-team action is warranted.

Wartime function expands to scouting and forward observation, guerrilla tactics, harassment and disruption, sabotage, precision strikes, and deep-range reconnaissance behind hostile movement.

Rangers operate in small teams or as lone operators. They are mobile, discreet, and information-driven; less visible than ordinary Greenshields, chosen for fieldcraft, independence, and judgment. Rangers are best used where observation, mobility, and timing matter more than force concentration. They are administratively part of the Greenshields and report through Greenshield command, but their operational profile is distinct enough that the Ranger designation is recognized as its own role within the broader order.

### Order Relationships

The three orders coordinate but do not subordinate to one another. Heartwarden and Greenshield commands consult on matters involving both orders; Rangers operate within the Greenshield structure but with significant autonomy in the field. Crisis deployments may bring all three together, as occurred during the Discordant War when Heartwardens, Greenshield contingents, and Ranger scouts deployed jointly under Lethariel's command.

The pre-existing canonical name "the Greenshield" (singular, used as the order's institutional name) refers to the same body as "Greenshields" (plural, used for individual members). Earlier framing of Heartwardens as "a prestige formation within the Greenshield" is superseded by this canon; Heartwardens and Greenshields are parallel orders.

## Religion and the Land

The elves' religious practice is not centered on deities. It is a practice of respect for the land, the animals, the trees, the Heartwood, and the deeper life the Feywood itself represents. The practice is druidic in form — seasonal observances, ceremonies tied to natural cycles, meditative and ritual practices performed at specific sites, the deep study of the Feywood's ecology and magical character as spiritual knowledge rather than mere biology.

The Heartwood is the central sacred site, but the elven understanding of what the Heartwood actually is remains canonically multiple. Three traditions persist (remnant magic, celestial-who-remained, ordinary-plant-and-collected-energy), and the elves do not require a single answer. Both deeper canon and the traditions' details are treated in the `heartwood` node.

The Silent Grove is the secondary sacred site and is canonically the elves' origin point in this world. The first trees of the valley rose at the Grove from the elven sacrifice that healed the land after the Cataclysm. Smaller sacred groves and shrines exist throughout the Feywood, often associated with specific elven noble family traditions or with particular natural features.

Elves speak of the land as a relationship rather than as a resource. The practice of respect — giving back what is taken, leaving what should be left, attending to what requires attention — is not a religious obligation in the ordinary sense. It is the ecological reality of living in a place that responds. Disrespect is not a religious sin; it is a practical error that the Feywood corrects.

This worldview is incompatible with the realm's Solveris monotheism in some deep philosophical senses, but the two traditions have coexisted for the full span of the realm's existence without requiring reconciliation. Elves understand that the realm's species have their own spiritual arrangements, including the dragon gods and the Oath religious structure. Realm species visiting the Feywood are not required to adopt elven practice; they are required to behave in ways that the Feywood finds acceptable, which is a different matter and does not require religious conversion.

## Relationship with the Realm

The realm-Feywood relationship is specific, ancient, and durable:

- **Oath of the Fallen alliance.** The Feywood swore alliance to the nascent realm during the Oath of the Fallen's founding period. The alliance acknowledges the Feywood's sovereignty, commits the realm to non-interference in Feywood affairs, and commits the Feywood to defensive cooperation if the realm faces existential threat. The alliance has been honored since. The Discordant War's Feywood contingent was a fulfillment of this alliance — the elves sent fighters because Mordrax's release would have threatened the Heartwood and the Feywood by extension, and the Oath's terms justified the deployment.
- **Mutual non-interference.** The realm does not send armies, officials, or formal representation into the Feywood without invitation. The Feywood does not send forces or formal representation into the realm beyond sanctioned diplomatic contact at the Border. Each operates in its own territory.
- **Mercantile exchange.** The primary ongoing contact between realm and Feywood is mercantile — the exchange windows conducted at the Border, where elven goods and realm goods are traded. This relationship is economically significant for both sides and provides the practical reason the Feywood tolerates occasional foolish realm visitors crossing without sanction.
- **Diplomatic contact.** Formal diplomatic matters between realm and Feywood route through the Council of Elders. The Council's diplomatic approach is patient — realm timelines feel rushed to Council members, and the Council's deliberations feel glacial to realm officials. Both sides have learned to accommodate the mismatch.
- **Cultural distance.** Elven and realm cultures remain distinct after a millennium of proximity. The elves are not partially-assimilated realm residents; they are residents of a different polity that shares geography. Elves who visit the realm (rare but not unknown) are clearly foreign. Realm residents visiting the Feywood (rare but not unknown) are clearly guests.

## Character

The Feywood has a specific atmospheric character that distinguishes it from every other region in the realm. It is magical at a continuous background intensity — not dramatic magical effects, but ambient magic that colors light, sound, wildlife, weather, and the traveler's own sense of self. The Feywood does not feel like a realm with magic in it; it feels like magic manifesting as a realm.

The elves' bearing contributes to the Feywood's character. They move through their own territory with a specific quality of attention that realm-side species rarely match. They know where they are, they know what is around them, they know what the land is doing at any given moment. This knowledge is not heightened awareness in an alarming sense; it is baseline competence for the environment. Realm visitors often find elven presence calming rather than intimidating — there is security in being near someone who clearly understands the place.

The Feywood's trickster reputation in realm folklore is partial truth. The Feywood does respond to disrespect in ways that realm visitors sometimes find cruel — lost travelers wandering in circles, promising paths that loop back to where they began, apparent help that turns out to be no help. The elves understand these responses not as tricks but as the land's natural self-regulation through the Heartwood's continuous reading of those who cross into the field. Respect is not complicated. The Feywood teaches respect through consequence, which is how wild places always teach respect.

The elves themselves have a sense of humor that realm visitors can miss at first. It is dry, observational, long-running. Jokes that began decades ago continue among certain family groups. Ironies accumulate. Wordplay in the elven tongue produces layers that realm translations cannot preserve. Elven gatherings with realm visitors sometimes produce moments where the elves are quietly enjoying something that realm visitors do not register. These are not jokes at the realm visitors' expense; they are simply layers the visitors are not equipped to perceive.

## Access

The Feywood is accessible through the Feywood Border. Sanctioned entry — Hall of Guides guide, elven welcome-token, or formal diplomatic protocol — is the reliable method. Unsanctioned entry is technically possible and produces the consequences documented at the Border.

**Navigation within the Feywood.** Sanctioned visitors are guided by their Hall of Guides companion or their elven host. Experienced solo visitors (rare — typically scholars with Feywood-specific expertise) navigate through established personal relationships with the land and with specific elven contacts. Unsanctioned entrants navigate on their own and typically do not navigate successfully.

**Lodging.** Visitors do not lodge "in the Feywood" in any general sense; they lodge at specific Feywood locations (Vaelmere for most realm visitors, Thornveil for higher-level diplomatic visits, occasionally noble family estates for specific guests, very rarely at the Silent Grove for specific purposes). Arrangements are made through elven hosts or through the Council's administrative infrastructure.

**Rights and protections.** The Feywood's laws and customs apply within Feywood territory. Realm law does not apply in the Feywood. Realm visitors agree to this when they cross the Border, though the realm's population understands this with varying degrees of depth. The realm does not intervene if a realm visitor runs into trouble within the Feywood; the Feywood handles its own affairs.

**Exit.** Proper exit through the Border they entered by, accompanied by guide or host. Improper exit means emerging somewhere along the Heartwood's root network at the Feywood's selection rather than the traveler's. The full canon of displacement outcomes is in the `heartwood` node.
