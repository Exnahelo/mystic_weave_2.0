# Scalemere

The Southern Lake's principal fishing village, set on the eastern shoreline where the grasslands slope gently into the water. Scalemere's harbor holds the realm's largest working fishing fleet — broad-bottomed nets-craft and smaller line-boats crewed by families whose ancestors have fished these waters since the realm's early generations. The daily catch moves north along the lakeshore road to Drakenvale City's Lakefront Market, reaching the capital before the fish lose their morning freshness. The village is modest, functional, and quietly prosperous. Its residents know the lake's moods, its seasonal patterns, and the specific grace its waters carry, and they do their work with the long steady rhythm of people whose livelihood is reliable.

## Scene Texture

Scalemere sits where the Draconic Grasslands' southern edge softens into the Southern Lake's shoreline. The transition is gentle — grassland gives way to scattered reed beds, reed beds to shallow water, shallow water to the broad open lake. The village is built at the point where the shoreline is stable enough for permanent construction and the water is deep enough for a working harbor. The result is a settlement that feels more lake than land, though its back is firmly set in the grasslands.

The approach from Drakenvale City is the lakeshore road — a well-maintained civic route running along the northern and eastern edges of the lake between the capital and the fishing grounds. The road is busy in the early morning hours, when fish carts move the daily catch north, and quiet most of the rest of the day. Travelers arriving at Scalemere see the harbor first — boats at moorings, the low shapes of warehouses along the water, the village proper rising gently above the waterline in layered terraces.

The harbor is the village's organizing feature. A stone-built quay extends into the lake in a broad curve, with the village's working fleet moored along its inner face. The broad-bottomed nets-craft — the realm's distinctive lake-fishing boats, designed for stability while hauling large nets — dominate the fleet. Smaller line-boats and the occasional specialty craft for deeper-water work fill out the mooring spaces. The boats themselves are painted in the traditional Scalemere idiom: dark hulls for water-resistance, pale decks, each family's craft marked with a specific combination of colored bands that identifies the boat at lake distance.

The village rises from the harbor in three rough tiers. The lowest tier holds the working infrastructure: the fish warehouses, the ice cellars carved into the embankment, the nets-repair shops, the cooperage, and the single stone cold-room that keeps the highest-grade catch during the brief summer months when the lake's upper waters warm. The middle tier holds the village's commercial and civic spaces: the central market, the common hall, the Harbor Reeve's office, the inn (the Reed and Scale), the three taverns, and the small craft workshops that support the fishing economy — rope-makers, net-menders, small-boat builders, ice-cutters. The upper tier holds residences, stretching back into the grassland rise above the shoreline.

The village's distinctive sound is water against wood — boats moving in the harbor, nets dragging across decks, the low slap of small waves against the stone quay. Gulls and lake-birds provide a continuous background chorus. In the early morning before the fleet departs, the harbor is loud with preparation; by mid-morning when the boats are out on the water, Scalemere becomes one of the quietest working villages in the realm. The boats return in the late afternoon, and the harbor comes back to life for the evening processing work.

## Functions

- **Primary lake fishing.** Scalemere is the Southern Lake's principal fishing operation. The fleet works most of the lake's productive waters, rotating grounds across seasons to allow recovery. The catch includes the lake's various freshwater species, with the most economically important being the lake-salmon that run seasonally, the bottom-feeding stock that supplies steady commodity fish, and the specialty species caught for Drakenvale City's finer markets.
- **Supply to the capital.** Daily catch moves by cart along the lakeshore road to Drakenvale City's Lakefront Market. The trip is short enough that morning catches reach the city's late-morning market still fresh. Scalemere's steady supply is a meaningful contributor to the capital's food economy and a staple in city residents' daily meals.
- **Ice and preservation work.** The village's ice cellars, cut into the lake embankment, support both the local fleet's preservation needs and the broader realm's demand for ice during summer months. Ice is cut in winter from the lake's frozen shallows, stored underground, and sold throughout the year.
- **Small-scale boat-building.** The Scalemere boatwrights produce the realm's working lake craft — the broad-bottomed nets-craft that are the village's signature — and occasionally specialty craft for customers elsewhere in the realm. The boats are not the realm's fine craft tradition (that belongs to Mirefall's wetland boatwrights and the Dracélune builders), but they are competent, reliable, and well-suited to Southern Lake conditions.
- **Tertiary lake observation.** Scalemere's fleet is on the water daily. The crews notice what happens on the lake — unusual currents, unusual visitors, unusual phenomena. This informal observation is not a formal function of the village, but the Harbor Reeve's office maintains quiet coordination with Drakenvale City's Administrative Quarter on any noteworthy lake activity.

## Civic Character

The village is run informally through the Harbor Reeve's office. The **Harbor Reeve** is the village's senior civic authority, elected from among the established fishing-family heads by a rotating council of senior captains, serving indefinite tenure subject to community confidence. Tier-3 generative role. The Reeve handles dispute resolution, coordination with Drakenvale City's civic offices, and the formal interface between the village and the broader realm.

The **Fishmaster** is the senior professional authority within the fleet — master fisher, coordinator of seasonal grounds rotation, mentor to new captains, and the traditional voice of the fishing community in civic matters. Tier-3 generative role. The Fishmaster's authority is craft-based rather than civic, but her word carries significant weight in village decisions that affect the fleet.

The village's social fabric is built around extended fishing families. Most residents are born into Scalemere, grow up working the lake alongside parents and siblings, and either continue fishing professionally or move into the village's supporting crafts. Marriage in and out of the village happens but is not the norm; Scalemere marriages tend to be within the village or to adjacent grasslands communities. The village is stable, slow-changing, and tightly-knit.

## Character and the Lake

Scalemere's residents have a specific relationship with the Southern Lake. They drink from it daily (the lake's waters, as residents know, carry a subtle beneficial quality from the Sacred Pools that feed it). They swim in it as children. They bury their dead at designated lakeshore sites where the sacred water can wash the graves. They observe small private rituals at significant life moments — a pour of lake water at a newborn's naming, a final taste of lake water at a deathbed, the hands of a returning fisher washed in the lake before entering the family home.

This relationship is personal rather than institutional. Scalemere is not a religious village in the formal sense — there is no temple, no dedicated Acolyte, no ceremonial structure beyond what the common hall accommodates. But the village takes the lake's sacred character seriously in its own quiet way, and the residents' collective sense of themselves is inseparable from the water they live on.

Festival observances coordinated with Drakenvale City (the yearly lake blessing, seasonal observances tied to the fishing calendar) bring Acolytes to the village at appointed times. Between those visits, Scalemere attends to its own quiet practice.

## Access

The village is fully open to travelers. The lakeshore road brings steady traffic from Drakenvale City; the agricultural road from Ashfield connects to Scalemere via the southeastern grasslands; lake traffic from Crystalhaven (the contemplative village on the opposite shore) occasionally brings visitors by boat.

Visitors are welcomed. Scalemere does not see the volume of traffic that the major settlements do, but its inns and taverns serve travelers regularly — traders buying fish in bulk, caravan crews resting overnight, occasional tourists from Drakenvale City coming to see the lake, Hall of Scales students on weekend expeditions who make the longer walk down from the north.

Dragon Guard presence is minimal — a small patrol station on the lakeshore road handles routine observation. Wardens are functionally absent. Local order is handled by the Harbor Reeve's office and the village's own social fabric.
