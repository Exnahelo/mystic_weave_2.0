# Ashfield

The realm's principal agricultural town, set in the southern Draconic Grasslands amid the wide fields that feed most of the Hollow Crown. Grain, root vegetables, livestock, dairy, and orchard fruit all move through Ashfield on their way to the city markets, and the town's character is shaped accordingly — practical, rhythmical, shaped by the harvest calendar, and populated by farmers, drovers, millers, and the traders who buy what the fields produce. Hall of Scales students walk down from the north on weekends for the town's cheaper beer and easier company, adding a reliable undercurrent of noise to Ashfield's otherwise early-to-bed rhythm.

## Scene Texture

Ashfield sits at the meeting point of the grasslands' broadest arable stretch and the network of cart-roads that carry food inward to Drakenvale City. The approach from any direction is the same — long open fields on either side of the road, the smell of worked soil, livestock calls carrying across the flats, and eventually the low skyline of barns, grain silos, and the town's characteristic pitched-roof houses rising above the grain line.

The town is built low. Two stories is the tall building; three stories exists but is unusual. Most construction is timber-framed with fieldstone foundations, whitewashed walls, and dark-shingled roofs. The streets are packed earth surfaced with crushed stone on the main routes, straight dirt in the residential lanes, and cobbled only in the central market square. Rain turns the edges of town to mud; summer dries everything to dust. Ashfield has opinions about both and accepts neither as an excuse.

The central market square is the town's organizing feature. Market days — tenth-days and feast-days by the old grasslands calendar — fill the square with stalls selling produce, grain, livestock by the head, dairy, cured meat, dried fruit, and the less-glamorous manufactured goods a farming population actually needs: pitchforks, horseshoes, scythe-blades, sacking, tanned hide. Between market days the square is quieter, used for civic gatherings, the occasional traveling performer, and the weekly Reeve's open hours for resident petitions and disputes.

Ringing the square are the town's anchor institutions: the Granary (the town's civic storehouse), the Grange Hall (where the Grange Master and the harvest cooperatives meet), the Reeve's office, the main inn, three taverns of varying quality, and the smithy. Residential streets radiate outward. Beyond the residential ring are the farm holdings proper, extending into the agricultural belt of Ashfield Fields.

The town's mood varies by season. Spring and early summer are busy with planting and the movement of seed-stock, summer is the long steady rhythm of field work, autumn is the concentrated chaos of harvest, and winter is quiet — the town half-asleep, the fields resting, the taverns full in the evenings and empty by morning.

Weekend nights during the Hall of Scales' academic terms are Ashfield's predictable exception. Students arrive from the north in groups of four to twelve, fill the cheaper tavern (the Broken Plough) and the better one (the Grain Dragon) in roughly equal proportion, drink more than they intend, and leave by morning. The town tolerates this with the quiet amusement of a population that was, in many cases, students once themselves — the Hall draws locals as well as travelers, and many Ashfield residents are Hall alumni. The students' misbehavior is low-stakes by tradition, and Ashfield has its expectations.

## Functions

- **Agricultural production and distribution.** Ashfield is the primary anchor for the realm's food belt. The fields immediately around the town (the Ashfield Fields region) produce the bulk of the realm's grain, root crops, dairy, and livestock. The town serves as the aggregation point, the commercial interface, and the administrative anchor for the whole agricultural region.
- **Market hub.** Tenth-day and feast-day markets draw buyers from Drakenvale City, from the Hall of Scales, from the outer villages (Scalemere, Silvercut, Dewhollow in smaller volumes), and from the caravan traders moving food toward the city and manufactured goods back outward.
- **Grange coordination.** The Grange Hall is the civic-cooperative institution managing harvest logistics, shared equipment, seed-stock banking, inter-farm dispute resolution, and the annual harvest-contribution to the city's granaries. The Grange Master is one of the town's most influential figures.
- **Student traffic.** Unofficially but reliably, Ashfield absorbs the Hall of Scales' weekend overflow. This has been true for generations and the town's economy is adjusted to it — two of the three taverns run discounted student pricing on rest-day nights, and the bookbinders have a quiet side trade in academic supplies for students who ran out mid-term.
- **Civic administration.** The Ashfield Reeve handles local governance, light judicial matters, and the interface with the Administrative Quarter in the Stronghold. Serious matters escalate; routine matters resolve here.

## Anchor Figures

- **The Ashfield Reeve** — the town's appointed civic authority, handling daily governance, minor judicial work, and representation to the broader realm. Tier-3 generative role. Typically a long-tenured local of stable temperament.
- **The Grange Master** — head of the harvest cooperative, coordinator of inter-farm logistics, and the town's practical economic authority. Tier-3 generative role. Usually a senior farmer elected from among the region's holdings.

## Character

Ashfield does not ask to be interesting. It gets up early, works hard, and expects the same of its residents. It is not unsophisticated — many residents have attended the Hall of Scales, read regularly, follow realm politics with informed opinions — but its sophistication is practical, not performative. A traveler asking for directions gets clear directions. A traveler asking for a drink gets a drink, not a story. A traveler asking for a story gets one if the weather's bad and the speaker's inclined, and it's probably a good one.

The town's relationship to Drakenvale City is that of a working hinterland to a capital — respectful, slightly skeptical of the city's tempo, proud of its own contribution to the realm, and inclined to roll its eyes at outsiders who assume a farming town is simple. Ashfield sustains the city. The city's residents eat Ashfield's grain and drink Ashfield's ale. Ashfield remembers this.

## Access

The town is fully open. The civic road from Drakenvale City arrives from the west, the road from the Hall of Scales from the north, the local routes to Scalemere from the south, and the Draconic Grasslands Edge corridor passes nearby. Caravans move through daily during market season. Travelers passing through are welcomed, lodged at reasonable rates, and generally left alone unless they invite conversation.

Dragon Guard presence is light — a single patrol station on the main road, staffed by two or three riders. Wardens are effectively absent except during rare anomaly responses. Local order is maintained by the Reeve's office, the tavern-keepers, and the town's own social fabric.
