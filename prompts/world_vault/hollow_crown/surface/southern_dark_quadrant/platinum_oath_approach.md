# Platinum Oath Approach

The secured mountain path ascending from the southern dark quadrant's lower terrain to the Platinum Oath Monastery at the summit. The Approach is not a pilgrim's road — it is a working military-monastic access corridor maintained by the Vigil and patrolled by the elite Wardens, with walled stages, manned gatehouses, and the engineered defensive architecture of an order whose heritage as the Platinum Accord has never truly been set aside. Visitors are welcomed in principle; the journey itself is the filter. The terrain is unforgiving, the distance is substantial, the corruption-adjacent surrounding country discourages casual approach, and the Vigil's checkpoints admit or decline travelers on the basis of genuine evaluation rather than ceremony. Those who reach the summit have earned their arrival. Those who do not are not the Vigil's concern.

## Scene Texture

The Approach begins where the southern dark quadrant's rough terrain meets the base of the monastery's mountain. There is no formal trailhead in the realm's ordinary sense — no signpost, no marked crossing, no visible transition from wilderness to road. A traveler approaching from Greymantle or from the broader dark-quadrant corridor simply finds, after enough walking, that the path underfoot has become something more than path. The ground has been cleared and graded. The edges have been shaped. The surface has been laid with cut stone. And then, without drama, a low stone wall rises on either side of the road, and the traveler understands that they have entered the Approach.

The first stretch — call it the **Lower Approach** — extends roughly a mile along the base of the mountain before the true ascent begins. The terrain here is still ordinary dark-quadrant country: broken ground, sparse vegetation, the particular stillness that characterizes the southern dark quadrant's closer reaches to the Rift. But the road itself is clearly different from the terrain it crosses. Stone walls flanking the road stand chest-high and are cut from dark local stone. They are not defensive walls in any serious tactical sense — a determined traveler could climb them without difficulty — but they are unmistakable: the road is claimed, and the claim is the Vigil's.

The **First Gatehouse** stands at the point where the mountain begins to rise in earnest. This is the Approach's first manned checkpoint. The gatehouse is a squat stone structure built into the mountain face itself, incorporating a heavy wooden gate that can be closed across the road when conditions require, with guard positions above and to either side. The gatehouse is always manned. A rotating detachment of Vigil members handles day-to-day operations, with elite Warden reinforcement during higher-alert periods.

A traveler reaching the First Gatehouse is received and evaluated. The evaluation is not hostile, but it is thorough. Vigil members ask questions about the traveler's business, examine any documentation the traveler carries, observe demeanor and apparent intent, and make a judgment. Most travelers with legitimate purpose — Conservatory scholars carrying proper introductions, Greymantle traders on established trade business, occasional Council-sanctioned visitors, approved pilgrims and devotees — pass through after the conversation. Travelers whose purpose is unclear, whose credentials are questionable, or who set off the Vigil's trained instincts for trouble are turned back. The Vigil does not engage in dispute; travelers who are not admitted receive a direct explanation and are expected to depart.

Past the First Gatehouse, the road begins the **Middle Ascent**. The grade here steepens substantially — the path climbs the mountain's shoulder in a series of switchbacks cut into the stone face. The walls flanking the road grow taller as the ascent continues, reaching shoulder-height and then head-height at the steeper stretches. The outer wall in particular — the one facing the drop-off down the mountain's side — is built to catch a traveler who stumbles on the path. Travelers crossing the Middle Ascent on clear days can see the surrounding dark-quadrant terrain spread out below; the view is stark rather than beautiful, but it is genuine, and it orients the traveler to how far they have come.

**Guard towers** punctuate the Middle Ascent at intervals. These are small stone structures, two stories tall, built into the mountain wall on the road's inner side. Each tower holds a Vigil observation post — continuous manning during daylight, reduced watch at night, with the watch capable of summoning reinforcement from the First Gatehouse or the Second Gatehouse within minutes. The towers are unobtrusive in operation but their silhouettes are visible on the mountain face from below, making clear to any observer that the Approach is watched.

The **Second Gatehouse** sits at the top of the Middle Ascent, marking the transition from the steeper switchback climb to the more gradual approach run toward the monastery itself. This is the Approach's primary checkpoint — more substantial than the First Gatehouse, with more extensive facilities. Travelers received here have already passed the First Gatehouse's evaluation; the Second Gatehouse handles documentation, coordination with the monastery's expected-arrivals records, and preparation for the final approach.

The Second Gatehouse includes a small **waystation** — not an inn, but a covered structure where travelers awaiting monastery reception can rest, take water, and consult briefly with Vigil staff about the monastery's current schedule and capacity. Some visitors end their journey here, receiving what they came for (a document transfer, a brief consultation with a Vigil liaison, a Council-sanctioned communication) without proceeding to the monastery itself. Others continue upward.

The **Upper Approach** runs from the Second Gatehouse to the monastery gates. The terrain is the least forbidding of the Approach's sections — the mountain's upper shoulder is broader and gentler than the switchback climb, and the road runs along a series of broad terraces rather than climbing continuously. But the Upper Approach is the most visibly militarized section. The flanking walls here are at their tallest and most complete. Arrow-slit embrasures open in the walls at regular intervals, directed outward across the approach zone below. The road passes through a series of smaller gates at strategic points — not checkpoints in the full sense but defensive chokepoints that could be closed if the monastery came under active threat.

The final ascent brings the traveler to the **monastery gates** — the formal entrance to the monastery proper. The gates are heavy, reinforced, and under permanent guard. A traveler arriving here presents credentials one final time and is admitted into the monastery's outer courtyard. Beyond this point, the monastery proper.

## Travel Conditions

The full Approach length — from the first claimed road to the monastery gates — is approximately one full day's walking under normal conditions. Travelers carrying substantial gear or those unaccustomed to mountain travel take longer; the Vigil's staff plan for day-and-a-half to two days for most civilian visitors. Caravan wagons cannot traverse the Approach; all travel is on foot or with pack animals, and the Vigil maintains a small pack-animal stable near the First Gatehouse for travelers arriving without their own.

The **seasonal character** of the Approach is severe. Summer brings the most comfortable traveling — firm footing, long daylight, relative lack of weather hazards. Autumn introduces variable conditions; mountain weather at altitude can turn without warning. Winter is genuinely difficult — snow accumulation at the upper reaches, ice on the switchback sections, and shortened daylight hours that make the day-long crossing challenging. The Approach does not close in winter, but travelers crossing in winter do so with appropriate gear or with Vigil escort. Spring brings thaw conditions that can make the lower switchback stretches slippery before the road's surface stabilizes.

**Vigil escort** is available to travelers with legitimate business who lack the experience or physical capability to complete the Approach independently. Escort is provided without ceremony — a Vigil member accompanies the traveler, handles the checkpoint protocols, ensures the traveler's safety along the difficult stretches, and departs once the traveler is safely delivered to the monastery. Escort is a practical service, not a special dispensation.

**Weather and environmental hazards** include: mountain storms that can develop rapidly on the ascent, rockfall in specific sections (the Vigil's maintenance crews manage known fall zones, but unexpected events occur), thermal exposure on long summer climbs, cold exposure at the upper reaches year-round, and the ambient psychological weight of the southern dark quadrant's corruption that some travelers find increases with altitude as they near the monastery's elevated position overlooking the Rift.

**Water stops** exist at intervals — natural springs tapped and developed with simple stone basins, with the locations known to regular travelers and marked subtly for first-time visitors.

## Functions

- **Controlled monastery access.** The Approach is the sole sanctioned route to the Platinum Oath Monastery. Unauthorized approach by other means is not impossible but is genuinely hazardous; the terrain surrounding the monastery's mountain is difficult, and the Vigil's monitoring extends beyond the road's immediate vicinity.
- **Vigil patrol and security infrastructure.** The Vigil maintains continuous watch along the Approach's full length. The checkpoint system (First Gatehouse, Second Gatehouse, guard towers, upper-approach defensive chokepoints) is operational at all times. Travelers experience it as a gated procession; the Vigil experiences it as the operational expression of the Platinum Accord's continuing security role.
- **Greymantle trade coordination.** The established trade relationship between the monastery and Greymantle moves along this corridor. Greymantle couriers bring specialized materials, repair-craft goods, and occasional food supplies; the monastery provides coin or barter in exchange. The trade is handled at the First Gatehouse — Greymantle couriers rarely proceed further up the Approach than this point — with the Vigil handling the onward movement to the monastery itself.
- **Council sanction verification.** Council-sanctioned visitors (scholars with specific research permissions, diplomatic representatives, official communications) present their documentation at the checkpoints. The Vigil's records of expected arrivals allow rapid verification; travelers without proper documentation are handled with greater caution.
- **Emergency response corridor.** The Approach also serves as the emergency egress and response route. If the monastery requires outside assistance — rarely, but not never — responders move along the Approach at maximum speed. If the Vigil dispatches elite Wardens outward from the monastery on anomaly-response missions, those movements traverse the Approach as well. The corridor's infrastructure supports this dual use.

## Character

The Approach carries a specific atmosphere that distinguishes it from the realm's other routes. It is not unfriendly — the Vigil staff are professional and, in most cases, courteous — but it is unmistakably serious. Travelers crossing the Approach feel the weight of the institution they are approaching. The walls, the gatehouses, the watchful towers, the careful evaluation at each checkpoint all communicate the same message: this is a protected place, its security is not theoretical, and admission is a considered matter.

The Vigil staff on the Approach embody a particular professional character. They are disciplined, observant, and direct. Their conversations with travelers are efficient rather than warm; they ask the questions they need answered, they observe what they need to observe, and they make their decisions. Travelers sometimes find this experience clinical; the Vigil would consider this observation a compliment. The order's work at the Approach is not pastoral outreach. It is the operational edge of the seal that maintains the realm's continuing peace.

The Approach's difficulty — physical, evaluative, environmental — is itself a feature of the monastery's institutional design. The order that maintains this place wants visitors who have meaningfully chosen to come. Travelers who arrive at the monastery gates by this route have, by definition, committed to the visit in ways that casual curiosity does not produce. The Vigil considers this natural filter as important as the formal checkpoints; the Approach's length and demands do the work of sorting visitors that no documentation system could do.

Those who have crossed the Approach successfully carry a specific recollection of it — not unpleasant exactly, but weighty. The realm's scholars who have visited the monastery frequently reference the Approach as formative, as an experience that changed their understanding of what the Vigil actually does. This is deliberate on the order's part. The Approach is meant to be remembered.

## Access

The Approach is technically open to any traveler. In practice, access is controlled through the checkpoint system, and travelers without legitimate purpose do not reach the monastery. The Vigil does not publish formal access criteria; judgment at the checkpoints handles case-by-case evaluation.

**Typical authorized travelers:**
- Conservatory scholars with research permissions
- Council-sanctioned diplomatic or official visitors
- Recognized devotees of Bahamut seeking specific consultation or retreat
- Greymantle trade couriers (limited to the First Gatehouse area)
- Family members of monastery residents (rare but accommodated)
- Other realm officials with legitimate monastery-related business

**Unauthorized approach** — travelers attempting to reach the monastery without passing the checkpoint evaluation — is handled depending on circumstances. Lost travelers genuinely disoriented are assisted back to the main road and directed to the checkpoint. Travelers deliberately attempting to evade the checkpoints are intercepted, questioned thoroughly, and either admitted (if their cause is legitimate and their evasion was a mistake) or removed (if their cause is illegitimate). The Vigil does not engage in dispute with unauthorized approachers; the order's practical authority in its own corridor is not subject to negotiation.

**Dragon Guard presence** on the Approach is minimal — the Vigil handles operational security entirely. Coordination with the realm's broader Dragon Guard occurs at the Council level, not along the Approach itself.

**Wardens** — distinct from the elite Wardens who work with the Vigil — are not resident along the Approach but may pass through on realm-internal anomaly-response business tangentially related to the southern dark quadrant. Such passes are coordinated with the Vigil and handled as routine professional cooperation.
