# Mirefall

The stilt-city of the Mystic Wetlands, built across a patchwork of raised platforms and high-ground islands in the southwestern marshes that collect the realm's basin drainage. Mirefall's character is defined by its water — the shallow channels running between its platforms, the heavy tree coverage overhanging its walkways, the wetland flora that grows wherever the city does not actively clear it. The settlement is quiet by the standards of the realm's other cities, its life organized around the central dome-hall where the community convenes and the adjacent craft-hall where the wetland's distinctive trades are practiced. Brackenmoor supplies its rare herbs; Dracélune handles its westward trade interface; the Mystic Wetlands themselves give it the raw materials that make it economically indispensable despite its peripheral location.

## Scene Texture

Mirefall does not sit on a single site. It occupies a network of raised platforms, high-ground islets, and dry-land peripheries spread across a mile or so of the Mystic Wetlands' eastern reaches. The land is patchy — scattered clusters of firmer ground rising out of shallow flooded marsh, tangled with root systems of ancient swamp-cypress and wetland oak, threaded with slow channels of tea-colored water. The city has built on what the land offered rather than forcing the land into a city's shape.

The approach from the central realm follows the old drainage road southwestward, descending gradually from the drier grasslands into the wetland's edge. The road ends at a firm-ground threshold village — small, utilitarian, mostly inns and outfitters — where travelers leave their wheeled transport and continue by foot, flat-bottomed boat, or the guided poling-craft the wetland's boatmen offer. From the threshold, the water becomes the road. Mirefall proper begins where the walkways do.

The walkways are the city's defining infrastructure. Built from treated hardwood planking raised three to four feet above the shallow water, they connect platform to platform in a branching network that locals navigate with unconscious ease and visitors navigate slowly. The planks are darkened with years of moisture, moss-edged, strung in places with simple rope railings and in other places without anything at all. A wet plank is an ordinary hazard. A visitor who is not paying attention goes into the water once or twice during their stay, and the locals are practiced at hauling them out without comment.

The platforms themselves vary in size and function. Large central platforms host the city's civic and commercial life. Medium platforms hold workshops, multi-family residences, and smaller markets. Small platforms are single-household homes. Beyond the platforms, peripheral dry-ground holdings accommodate the structures that water-building cannot support — the larger stables, the better-appointed residences of Mirefall's senior merchant families (on drier eastern shores), and a modest cluster of poorer holdings on the drier western fringe that the city's platforms do not reach.

Tree coverage is heavy. Old-growth swamp trees rise from the water and the platform edges, their canopies overlapping above much of the city to create dappled shade even at midday. In places, the canopy is thick enough that platforms below see little direct sunlight. The effect is cooling, softening, and visually beautiful in a way that photographs the realm in few other places.

The sounds of Mirefall are specific. The constant low chorus of wetland fauna — frogs, insects, waterfowl, the occasional splash of something larger moving in the channels. The creak of the walkways under foot traffic. The distant sound of work from the craft-hall and the dull rhythmic tap of mortar-work from the herbalists. Human conversation tends to be pitched lower than in other settlements, partly because the wetland absorbs sound, partly because Mirefall has simply grown into a quieter cultural register over generations.

## The Two Halls

Mirefall's civic life is anchored by two central structures, both built on the city's largest platforms:

### The Hall

The central dome-hall — called simply "the Hall" by residents — is a circular stone and timber structure with a conical base rising to a domed top. The distinctive silhouette dominates the central platform and is visible from much of the city. The Hall serves multiple functions: civic assembly space for community meetings, shared dining hall for residents and visitors, ceremonial space for weddings and funerals and seasonal observances, and informal gathering space during the quiet hours of the day.

Meals at the Hall are a Mirefall institution. The community cooks collectively on rotating duty, serving two main meals daily (midday and evening) to any resident or visitor who wishes to attend. Contributions toward ingredients are expected from residents who can contribute and unexpected from those who cannot. The food is wetland-adapted — fish, waterfowl, rice, marsh greens, the distinctive herbs and spices the region produces — and it is consistently good. Travelers passing through often time their visits to coincide with Hall meals.

The Hallkeeper is the Hall's senior administrator and Mirefall's effective civic authority. Tier-3 generative role. Typically a long-tenured resident respected for judgment and temperament rather than any formal election or appointment process — the role accumulates to whoever the community trusts to hold it.

### The Second Hall

The craft-hall, adjacent to the Hall across a short connecting walkway, is similarly shaped — conical base, domed top — but slightly smaller and organized internally for work rather than assembly. The Second Hall is where Mirefall's craftspeople work, train apprentices, and coordinate the specialized trades the wetland supports.

Primary crafts practiced at the Second Hall:

- **Herbalism.** The realm's most skilled wetland-herb practitioners work here, producing medicinal compounds, restorative tinctures, preservation agents, and specialized preparations that institutional buyers across the realm consider essential. Brackenmoor supplies the rare raw materials; Mirefall's herbalists turn them into finished goods.
- **Specimen preparation.** Wetland fauna and flora yield rare materials for alchemical, scholarly, and arcane use — specific feathers, glandular extracts, pressed botanical specimens, bottled samples of the wetland's less-common phenomena. The Second Hall prepares and packages these for export.
- **Boat-craft.** Flat-bottomed poling boats, small channel craft, specialty wetland vessels. The Second Hall's boat-wrights produce the city's own working fleet and also sell to Dracélune and to individual traders.
- **Reed and hardwood work.** Woven matting, specialty cordage, water-treated lumber, and the small-scale carpentry that platform life requires.

The Craftmaster of the Second Hall coordinates the craft community. Tier-3 generative role. The position is functional rather than ceremonial — the Craftmaster manages apprentice assignments, coordinates commissions, and interfaces with external buyers and the realm's guild structures.

## Functions

- **Wetland production.** Mirefall is the realm's primary processing and export center for wetland-origin goods: rare herbs, restorative compounds, specialty specimens, and the small but valuable flow of divination-adjacent materials that certain scholarly and arcane practitioners seek. The economy is specialized but economically significant.
- **Basin drainage terminus.** The realm's water system ultimately flows to Mirefall's wetland. This is why the region is flooded; it is also why Mirefall's herbalists have access to botanical materials that grow only where sustained fresh-water saturation occurs. The flooding and the economy are the same phenomenon seen from different angles.
- **Regional civic anchor.** Mirefall serves as the organizing settlement for the Mystic Wetlands region. Brackenmoor, the outer fringe villages, the scattered independent herbalists and trappers of the wider wetland — all route through Mirefall for market access, civic registration, and the realm's institutional interface.
- **Quiet sanctuary.** Mirefall attracts a small but persistent flow of residents seeking quieter life than the central settlements offer. Retired scholars, meditation-inclined practitioners, artists who want the wetland's light and solitude, and people who simply prefer Mirefall's pace to anywhere else. The city absorbs them without comment.

## Peripheral Structures

Beyond the platform city, Mirefall extends into dry-ground peripheries:

- **The East Shore.** A firmer-ground eastern fringe where the wetland edges toward drier terrain. Several of Mirefall's senior merchant families maintain their residences here — substantial homes with their own stables, gardens, and the infrastructure that platform life cannot support. The merchant community is stable, small, and generally integrated with the platform city rather than separate from it.
- **The West Fringe.** A drier western strip where Mirefall's poorer residents live. The fringe is not served by the platform walkway network; residents walk or pole small boats through the shallows to reach the city center. The fringe carries a rougher character than the platforms proper, but it is not a slum — Mirefall's poverty is modest, and the community's social fabric extends to the fringe without excluding it.
- **The Threshold Village.** The firm-ground settlement at the end of the drainage road where wheeled transport ends. Small, utilitarian, mostly inns and the outfitters who equip visitors for their stay. Not a distinct settlement — administratively part of Mirefall — but physically separate.

## Character

Mirefall's disposition is quiet, unhurried, and deeply local. The community has been here for generations; most residents are born into Mirefall and stay. The Hall's collective meals, the shared labor of wetland production, and the slow rhythms the environment enforces create a social fabric that is tight without being closed. Visitors are welcomed. Outsiders who stay long enough to understand the place become less outside. People who want a city that asks nothing of them find the pace congenial; people who want to be interesting find themselves ignored in polite ways.

The wetland itself is treated with the same pragmatism Dracélune extends to the Feywood — the wetland is what it is, and living well here means respecting what deserves respect. The channels carry things. The canopy hides things. The deeper marsh, beyond the city's boundaries, is not safe. Residents know which waters to avoid and which paths to stay on; they teach these things to children and to visitors, and they notice when someone is not paying attention.

## Access

Mirefall is open to travelers. The drainage road from the central realm arrives at the Threshold Village, where travelers transition to wetland-appropriate transport. From there, the platform network extends throughout the city. Visitors are not screened or restricted; the city is small enough that anyone causing trouble is identified by the community before trouble becomes serious.

Dragon Guard presence is minimal — the wetland's geography makes large-force deployment impractical, and Mirefall has not required it. The Dragon Guard maintains a small liaison station at the Threshold Village primarily for regional coordination and emergency response. Warden presence is functionally absent.

Civic authority is informal but real. The Hallkeeper handles most matters. Disputes are typically resolved through community mediation in the Hall. Serious matters escalate to the Administrative Quarter at the Stronghold, with coordination through Dracélune as the nearest major civic hub.

The Mystic Wetlands themselves extend well beyond the city in all directions. Brackenmoor lies to the north, along the wetland's drainage approach. The deeper marsh extending south and west is wild territory — not hostile exactly, but not settled either. The wetland's dangerous zones are known to residents and marked informally on the working maps the boatmen carry. Travelers venturing beyond the city's immediate boundaries should hire local guidance or accept the risk of not doing so.

Mirefall is authored as a single parent settlement node. Its internal structure (platforms, halls, peripheral shores) is described in scene texture rather than broken into sub-nodes. The Threshold Village is explicitly part of Mirefall rather than a separate settlement node — it is the wet/dry interface rather than a distinct community.

Hallkeeper and Craftmaster of the Second Hall are Tier-3 generative roles. These positions are Mirefall-specific and do not have realm-wide canonical entries.

The connection to `dracelune` represents the westward trade route — Mirefall's wetland goods move to Dracélune for broader realm distribution and for SSTC-handled external trade. This matches the existing topology baseline.

The connection to `greymantle` matches the baseline's existing `greymantle → mirefall` link. Mirefall is the nearest civic settlement to Greymantle, and trade between them occurs though the relationship is politically complicated (Greymantle's outlaw character, Mirefall's civic character).

The connection to `brackenmoor` is two-way. Brackenmoor is Mirefall's primary upstream supplier; goods flow from Brackenmoor to Mirefall and residents move in both directions.

The connection to `southwestern-mystic-wetlands` is the regional parent reference. Travel into the broader wetland beyond the city resolves through this node.

The "two halls" structure (civic Hall + craft-hall) is a canonical feature. These are NOT authored as separate location nodes but are described within this node's scene texture. The Hall's collective-dining tradition is a reliable cultural hook for any Mirefall scene.

Threat level 1 reflects wetland environmental hazards (drowning risk for the inattentive, disease, wildlife, the deeper marsh's unknowns) rather than civic danger. Mirefall itself is among the safest civic environments in the realm.

The herbalism and specimen-preparation economy is canonically established here. Mirefall is the realm's primary center for wetland-origin medicinal and alchemical goods, distinct from (but complementary to) the Draconic Forge's ceremonial work, the Conservatory's theoretical work, and Deephollow's mineral-origin arcane materials.

The "quiet sanctuary" framing — Mirefall as a place the meditation-inclined or solitude-seeking retire to — is a narrative hook for character backstory work. Characters with contemplative, herbalist, or reclusive origins can plausibly anchor to Mirefall.
