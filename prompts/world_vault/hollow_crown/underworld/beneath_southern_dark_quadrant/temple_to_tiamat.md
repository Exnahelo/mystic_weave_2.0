# Temple to Tiamat

The sealed temple to the five-headed dragon goddess, built deep beneath the Shadowed Hollows at the founding of the sanctuary and disputed in every age since. The chromatic dragons of the founding coalition insisted on its construction as acknowledgment of their heritage and as formal respect for the principle of balance that underlay the entire Ptarian Pact; the metallic and gem dragons accepted the insistence as the price of unity, with the clear understanding that the temple would remain sealed rather than active. That understanding has held for a thousand years, with one catastrophic interruption. The Discordant War began when a rogue chromatic faction attempted to unseal the Temple; it ended when the Platinum Warden gave his life to reseal it. The Temple has remained sealed since. The Vigil maintains its wards. The elite Wardens patrol its threshold on rotation. No one living has seen its interior, and the realm's continuing peace depends on that not changing.

## Scene Texture

The Temple is not visited. The description that follows is of the threshold — the approach corridor and the outer face of the seal itself. What lies beyond the seal is not canonically known to any living figure except possibly Tiamat, and canon does not confirm her awareness.

The approach is a descent. From the Shadowed Hollows' deeper interior, a single passage descends through rock of increasing density and decreasing warmth, opening eventually into a vaulted forechamber carved into the bedrock far below the surface corruption. The forechamber is small — perhaps thirty paces across — and its walls are lined with the ancient ward-work of the founding dragons, still visible as fine silver-platinum inlay traced across the dark stone. The inlay glows faintly. It has glowed faintly for a thousand years.

The forechamber ends at the seal.

The seal is a single pair of massive stone doors, sized for dragons and sealed closed by work the founding coalition performed together across weeks of continuous effort. The doors are carved with a composition depicting the Ptarian Pact itself — metallic, chromatic, and gem dragons rendered at equal scale, circled around the Heartstone, with Bahamut above and Tiamat below. The carving is masterwork. The seal itself is not visible as a physical object; it is magical work woven through the doors, the frame, the surrounding stone, and the air itself. A visitor standing in the forechamber can feel the seal. Trained visitors can see traces of it — faint lines of light tracing the doors and the walls, pulsing slowly in patterns that correspond to the ongoing maintenance cycle.

The seal is active. It requires continuous attention. The Vigil conducts ward-maintenance rotations on a schedule that has not varied in a century: a senior Vigil member descends with a small team of elite Wardens, spends approximately three days at the threshold conducting inspection and reinforcement work, and departs. A fresh team arrives before the previous team's effects have fully ended. The seal has never been unattended since Vindrael resealed it. It will not be.

The air in the forechamber is cold — not the ordinary cold of deep stone, but a specific cold that visitors describe as subtracted rather than present. The forechamber is silent in the same subtracted way. Sounds made here travel oddly. A visitor speaking in ordinary tones finds their voice muted, absorbed by something, returning with a quality that is not echo. Most visitors do not speak much in the forechamber. The ones who do say what they came to say and leave.

Beyond the seal, behind the doors, is the Temple's interior. Its architecture is recorded in the founding documents, which remain in the Archive Block of the Administrative Quarter under Council seal. Those documents describe the chambers, the altars, the cardinal arrangements, the original ceremonial configurations the founding chromatic dragons specified. The documents are not publicly available. The Vigil has read them. The elite Wardens have read excerpts relevant to their duty. The Council has read all of them. No one else in the realm has.

The interior is sealed precisely because it is not empty. The founding chromatic dragons did not merely consecrate an empty space; they placed objects, inscribed names, and performed ceremonies of founding-weight. What accumulated in the Temple between its consecration and its sealing during the Discordant War is not recorded in the publicly known canon. What entered the Temple during the brief period the rogue chromatic faction had partial access before Vindrael's intervention is even less recorded. The seal exists to keep those things inside.

## Historical Weight

The Temple's construction was the most contentious element of the Ptarian Pact. The coalition of founding dragons had to balance three competing truths:

The chromatic dragons could not be formal equals in the sanctuary if their heritage received no acknowledgment. Refusing the Temple's construction would have made the Pact a metallic-and-gem coalition that tolerated chromatic presence, rather than a genuine tri-alignment accord. The chromatic dragons would not have accepted that terms; the sanctuary would not have been founded.

The Temple, once built and consecrated, could not be active. Tiamat's worship, in any operational form, was incompatible with the Ptarian Code's balance principles. The chromatic dragons themselves did not propose active worship; they proposed acknowledgment. The distinction was rigorously maintained during the founding.

The sealing was therefore part of the original design, not an afterthought. The Temple was consecrated and sealed in the same ceremonial sequence. The seal was not a repair of a mistake; it was the architecture the founders intended. The Pact required the Temple to exist. The Pact required the Temple to be sealed.

This arrangement held through the long Age of Harmony. The Temple was known, its existence acknowledged in civic and religious canon, and its sealed state treated as permanent. Chromatic dragons who sought formal recognition of their heritage found that recognition in the Temple's existence alone. They did not need to enter it; they needed to know it was there.

The Discordant War broke this arrangement. A faction of chromatic dragons — a minority of the full chromatic community, but enough to start a war — argued that the Temple's sealed state was an insult, that the founding compromise had been a betrayal, and that opening the Temple was not theological apostasy but historical correction. Their attempt to unseal the Temple triggered the war. Vindrael's sacrifice at the Rift of Discord resealed it at the cost of his life.

The sealing that followed Vindrael's sacrifice was stronger than the original. The Council, the Vigil, and the surviving founding dragons conducted new ward-work that layered additional protections over the original seal. The elite Wardens who maintain the seal today maintain both layers — the founding work and the post-war reinforcement. They maintain both as a single continuous system. The distinction between them matters only to specialists.

## Contemporary Significance

The Temple's contemporary role is threefold:

First, it is a historical monument that cannot be visited. The realm's theological and civic memory is bound up in its existence. Every generation's children learn that the Temple is there. Every adult knows that the continued sealing is a duty the realm collectively carries. The Temple shapes the realm's self-understanding even though almost no one alive has been near it.

Second, it is the Vigil's defining responsibility. The Platinum Oath Monastery exists, in its deepest purpose, to maintain the Temple's seal. Everything else the Vigil does — the Rift monitoring, the elite Warden training, the Greymantle trade, the public monastic face — is structured around and funded by this central duty. The Vigil has not forgotten. They will not.

Third, it is a latent question the realm does not voluntarily confront. What would happen if the seal failed? Is the seal truly permanent? What, exactly, is behind the doors? The Council addresses these questions only in confidence. The Vigil's senior members carry working answers that are not shared outside their order. The elite Wardens understand their duty without needing to know the full stakes. Ordinary residents do not ask. The questions are present, however. They are the background silence of the realm's sacred life.

## Access

The Temple is not accessible to ordinary travelers. The surface descent from the Shadowed Hollows is protected, patrolled, and physically difficult to locate without specific guidance. The Vigil and the elite Wardens know the path. Senior Council members know the path. Specific Arcane Conservatory ward-specialists know the path as a professional necessity. That is the complete list of living figures with ordinary canonical knowledge of the approach.

A character attempting to find the Temple without this knowledge would face:

- **The Shadowed Hollows.** The surface region above the Temple is itself a corrupted zone. Traversal requires skill, preparation, and luck. Getting lost here is how visitors die. The Vigil does not maintain patrol routes through the Hollows' interior, because doing so would compromise the Temple's security; characters entering the Hollows are on their own.
- **The descent.** The path from the Hollows' deeper interior to the Temple forechamber is not marked. Multiple false passages exist. The actual route requires specific knowledge that the Vigil protects rigorously.
- **The forechamber threshold.** A character somehow reaching the forechamber without Vigil authorization would be in a space where the Vigil patrols. Interception is likely. Non-hostile interception means escort back to the surface and debriefing. Hostile interception means different outcomes, depending on the character's intent and the Vigil's judgment.

Approaching the Temple with Vigil sanction follows a different protocol: escorted descent, controlled entry, specific purpose. This is the standard for ward-maintenance rotations. It is not a protocol casual visitors can invoke.

Breaching the seal — unsealing the Temple — is not addressed here in mechanical terms. Any storyline attempting this is a realm-shaking event requiring explicit narrative authorization. It is not an action a player character performs casually.

## Authoring Notes

The Temple to Tiamat is a SEALED-SITE type, distinct from the settlement, location, lair, and hazard types used elsewhere. This type indicates a location that is canonically known and mapped but not navigable in ordinary play. Characters can approach the threshold under specific conditions; they cannot ordinarily enter.

The interior of the Temple is canonically NOT AUTHORED. The founding documents describing the interior exist in the Administrative Quarter's Council-sealed archives but are not available in this node or elsewhere in public canon. This opacity is deliberate and should be preserved. If a major storyline ever requires Temple interior content, that content should be authored at that time as a distinct Council-level narrative event.

The connection to `shadowed-hollows-proper` is the canonical surface-to-subterranean descent path. Shadowed Hollows Proper is currently a placeholder exit per the topology baseline. When that node is authored (scheduled as a later concern), the reciprocal connection should be established. Until then, this connection is a forward reference.

The connection to `southern-dark-quadrant` is the regional parent reference. The underworld region beneath the southern dark quadrant is the Temple's broader geographic context.

Threat level 4 reflects the site's extreme hazard to unprepared visitors. The forechamber itself is survivable for brief periods with adequate training; prolonged exposure or attempts to breach the seal are canonically catastrophic. The threat is not combat — the Vigil is not hostile to recognized visitors — but environmental, psychological, and consequential.

`known_npcs` is empty. The Vigil members conducting maintenance are transient and rotate. No named NPC resides at the Temple. Scene-specific instantiation is appropriate when the narrative requires a Vigil figure.

`discovered: false` at the canonical level. The Temple's existence is public knowledge; its physical approach path is not. A character who has not canonically visited or been briefed on the approach does not know how to reach the threshold. Characters who gain approach knowledge through play should have their session state updated; the canonical `discovered` field remains `false`.

The Ptarian Pact framing — Temple construction as necessary for the coalition to form — is canonically established here. This is consistent with existing `history.md` and `world.md` content but adds specific detail about the chromatic dragons' motivations and the sealed-from-the-beginning design. Any future worldbuilding that touches the founding should preserve this framing.

The Temple's interior objects, inscriptions, and historical ceremonial configurations are canonically acknowledged as existing but not described. This reserve is intentional. Specific narrative elements (a named artifact within the Temple, a specific inscription, a particular chamber) should be authored only when a storyline requires them and with Council-level narrative weight.

The seal's two layers — founding work plus post-war reinforcement — are canonically established. Any storyline involving ward-work at the Temple should acknowledge both layers. The distinction matters for specialist narratives and can be ignored for general play.

The "background silence" framing — the Temple as a present but unaddressed question in the realm's sacred life — is deliberate tonal guidance. The realm does not pretend the Temple does not exist; the realm simply does not discuss it more than necessary. GPT narration in any Temple-adjacent scene should reflect this restraint.
